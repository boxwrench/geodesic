# Sweetwater County, WY — Stage-3 (Validate) WATER ECONOMICS

**Date:** 2026-06-13 (agent run; some sources dated through 2026-06)
**Mode:** PILOT-STAGE Mode-B DRAFT — EVERYTHING tagged 🔍. Nothing here is verified ✅. Human verification + integration required. Do not act on a kill/go from this alone.
**Stage-3 question:** Is extending water to a Geodesic-scale load physically AND economically real?
**Demand frame (placeholder, from prior stages):** ~0.5–0.8 MGD entry → ~6–9 MGD full build-out (~7,000–10,000 ac-ft/yr if continuous; consumptive use lower).

---

## TL;DR (the Validate read)

- **Surface (Green River system): PHYSICALLY REAL, economically plausible at build-out — via ACQUISITION of an existing large right or a new junior permit, NOT via reliable senior supply.** The strongest single data point: a brand-new ~**14,500 ac-ft/yr** Green River industrial water right was permitted for the **Pacific Soda / Dry Creek Trona** project (BLM ROD May 2025). That is *larger* than Geodesic full build-out — so a new appropriation of this magnitude is demonstrably obtainable in this basin. BUT it is **junior / curtailment-exposed** under the Colorado River Compact. 🔍
- **Groundwater (potable, near corridor): WEAK as a sole municipal source at scale.** Existing corridor towns relying on groundwater (Wamsutter, Superior) pull from deep (1,300–2,000 ft) low-yield wells; the regional Battle Springs / deep aquifers run high-TDS + radionuclides. No evidence of a high-yield potable aquifer that alone supports 6–9 MGD near the I-80 corridor. Desalination of saline GDB groundwater is the fallback, at a real but not prohibitive cost order (~$350–$800/ac-ft OPEX-range for brackish RO; site-specific). 🔍
- **Conveyance: the Green River main-stem and the existing JPWB system are INSIDE the ~20–30 mi threshold** for a Rock Springs / Green River / I-80 corridor site. The Green River runs through the City of Green River; Rock Springs is ~15 mi east and already fed by a JPWB pipeline. So conveyance is NOT the binding constraint on the Green River side. 🔍
- **Where it FLIPS:** Not primarily on the demand number (entry vs build-out both fit physically). It flips on **(a) seniority/curtailment risk** — a new junior right is the first cut in a compact call — and **(b) whether the project buys firm supply (acquire senior right / Fontenelle contract storage) vs. relies on a fresh junior permit.** Entry-scale (0.5–0.8 MGD) is trivially absorbable; build-out (6–9 MGD) is the level where you must secure a dedicated, drought-firm right rather than ride spare system headroom. 🔍

## 1. SURFACE OPTION — Green River system

### 1a. Existing municipal supplier headroom (JPWB) 🔍
- **Rock Springs/Green River/Sweetwater County Joint Powers Water Board (JPWB)** is the regional supplier: serves Green River, Rock Springs, Reliance, White Mountain, Clearview, Ten-Mile, plus industrial sales (Simplot/SF Phosphates). Source = **Green River, direct diversion.** [waterplan muniuse memo]
- **Capacity (2026 reporting / GM Brian Seppie):** peak-day demand has run ~**23 MGD**; treatment/production capacity ~**32–34 MGD**. [WebSearch GRB BAG; SweetwaterNOW 2026-04] So the *existing* system carries roughly **~9–11 MGD of nominal spare treated-water headroom** at peak — coincidentally near Geodesic full build-out. 🔍
- BUT (from the 2001 plan memo, still the structural read): JPWB surface water rights on the Green River total ~**40 cfs (≈79 AFD)**, all **junior** — including a senior-most 1871 territorial 4 cfs right but the bulk on 1928–1989 enlargement permits. "Simply having water rights does not mean they can meet demands in drought years… priority dates must withstand regulation in times of shortage." [waterplan muniuse memo] 🔍
- **DRAFT read:** A 0.5–0.8 MGD entry load is absorbable inside existing JPWB headroom with a wholesale/industrial water-sales contract (JPWB already sells to Simplot). A 6–9 MGD build-out would consume essentially ALL nominal spare headroom AND ride on junior rights — JPWB itself would not commit its drought-exposed margin to a single new mega-customer without the customer bringing its own firm supply. **GAP: actual uncommitted JPWB capacity + willingness must come from JPWB directly (307-875-4317) + current rate/contract terms.** 🔍

### 1b. New municipal/industrial appropriation 🔍
- **Proof a build-out-scale new right is obtainable:** **Pacific Soda LLC / Dry Creek Trona Mine** secured a new water right of up to **~14,500 ac-ft/yr** off Wyoming's Green River allotment (~4,796 gpm continuous). BLM Record of Decision **May 2025**; project ~south of Green River, Sweetwater County. [Cowboy State Daily 2024-03/09; BLM ROD 2025-05; WebSearch] 🔍
  - This is the single most important Validate data point: **a fresh appropriation LARGER than Geodesic full build-out was just permitted in this exact basin/county.** Physical + permitting feasibility of a new junior Green River right at the 7,000–10,000 ac-ft/yr scale is therefore demonstrated. 🔍
  - Caveat: trona = junior right, explicitly "among the first to lose rights" in a Colorado/Green River call. [WebSearch] Same exposure would attach to a new Geodesic right. 🔍
- **Acquisition / transfer / conversion path:** The basin has large existing industrial (trona) and agricultural rights. A senior ag right purchased + changed to municipal/industrial use is the classic way to get a *drought-firmer* (older priority) supply rather than a new junior permit. WY allows change of use/transfer via SEO petition (injury/no-enlargement tests). **GAP — no specific acquirable senior right identified; needs e-Permit water-right search + a water-rights broker/attorney.** 🔍

### 1c. Fontenelle / Flaming Gorge contract (federal storage) water 🔍
- **Fontenelle Reservoir** (Seedskadee Project, Reclamation): active capacity ~**150,500 ac-ft**, total ~**345,360 ac-ft**. Wyoming holds M&I storage rights here; reporting indicates WY can use on the order of ~**120,000 ac-ft/yr**, with potentially ~**80,000 ac-ft more** accessible if the dam is fully armored ("rip-rap" project). [Wikipedia Fontenelle; WyoFile; WebSearch] 🔍
  - Implication: **Fontenelle is the most plausible route to FIRM, drought-buffered supply** at Geodesic scale — buying/contracting a block of Fontenelle M&I storage (single-digit thousands of ac-ft) is small against 120k+ ac-ft and would convert junior-flow risk into stored-water reliability. **GAP — uncommitted Fontenelle M&I contract availability + price must be confirmed with Reclamation UC Region + WWDC.** 🔍
- **2026 stress signal (live):** Fontenelle was at **~49% capacity early April 2026** (low snowpack, not yet a downstream draw). **Flaming Gorge** (downstream) is being drawn down **660,000–1,000,000 ac-ft through April 2027** to prop Lake Powell. Reclamation says no Fontenelle drawdown planned, but the regional anxiety is explicit. [Cowboy State Daily 2026-04-28] 🔍
- **2026 curtailment posture (JPWB GM, on record):** "not yet to a point where a compact call would be implemented on the larger system" — but "we're getting closer to that threshold than we ever have been." Restrictions ARE happening on tributaries (Blacks Fork) for ag, NOT yet on the Green main-stem. [SweetwaterNOW 2026-04-28] 🔍
- 2007 Interim Guidelines expire end-2026; post-2026 rules unresolved → **the probability of a hard compact call on a new junior Green River right by 2027+ is the dominant unquantified risk.** [prior stage-2 §4] 🔍

## 2. GROUNDWATER OPTION

### 2a. Potable aquifer near the corridor? 🔍
- Corridor/county towns on groundwater illustrate the ceiling: **Wamsutter** (3 wells, 1,365–2,010 ft, ~342 gpm total right = ~1.5 AFD), **Superior** (3 wells ~1,700 ft, ~1,260 gpm = ~5.6 AFD). These are *town*-scale, deep, modest-yield. [waterplan muniuse memo] 🔍
- Regional hydrogeology (USGS SIR 2004-5214, prior stage): shallow groundwater is widespread but **largely marginal-to-unsuitable for potable use (high TDS); quality worsens with depth → moderately saline to briny below a few thousand feet; Battle Springs Fm carries elevated radionuclides (uranium country).** [prior stage-2 §2] 🔍
- **No evidence located of a high-yield, potable, fresh aquifer near Rock Springs/Green River/I-80 that could alone firm 6–9 MGD.** Groundwater here is a supplement, not a standalone municipal base supply at build-out scale. **GAP — site-specific well logs + a Water-Supply/Yield study (SEO Ground Water Div 307-777-6163) required.** 🔍

### 2b. Desalination of saline GDB groundwater (fallback) 🔍
- If forced onto saline groundwater, **brackish-water reverse osmosis (BWRO)** is the technology. Rough cost order (Texas TWDB / Sandia / literature, NOT WY-specific):
  - Produced-water cost ~**$357–$782/ac-ft** for brackish GW desal (Texas operating plants). [TWDB 2012, infl-adjusted higher today] 🔍
  - OPEX ~**$0.39–$0.66/m³** at 10,000–70,000 m³/d; ~**$1.50 per 1,000 gal** order-of-magnitude. CAPEX highly capacity/quality dependent. [NCBI/PMC review; Sandia] 🔍
  - BWRO membranes target **1,000–10,000 mg/L TDS** feed; OPEX not very sensitive across 2,000–6,000 mg/L. Higher TDS / deeper saline → worse. 🔍
- **Concentrate (brine) disposal** is the real inland gotcha — in an endorheic GDB setting (no ocean outfall) it means evaporation ponds or deep-well injection, both adding cost + permitting. Radionuclides in Battle Springs water would complicate residuals handling. **GAP — not costed here; this is a project-killer-or-not question that needs an engineering pre-feasibility.** 🔍
- **DRAFT read:** Desal is *physically* a backstop but it is the expensive, permitting-heavy path. It only becomes the answer if (a) surface acquisition fails AND (b) a site sits on the GDB side away from the Green River. For a corridor site, surface (Green River) beats desal on both cost and reliability. 🔍

## 3. CONVEYANCE vs the ~20–30 mi threshold 🔍

- **Green River main-stem runs through the City of Green River**; the JPWB treatment plant + intake are on it. **Rock Springs is ~15 mi east**, already served by a JPWB transmission pipeline (the 15 MGD Rock Springs pipeline). [waterplan muniuse memo; geography] 🔍
- A Geodesic site anywhere in the **Green River–Rock Springs I-80 corridor is within ~0–20 mi of an existing diversion/treatment node** → **comfortably inside the 20–30 mi spec.** Conveyance is NOT the binding constraint on the Green River side. 🔍
- A site deep in the **Great Divide Basin interior** (e.g., toward Wamsutter, ~40+ mi NE of Rock Springs along I-80) would likely **exceed** the threshold to a reliable Green River source and would push toward local (saline) groundwater + desal. **So the conveyance answer is site-location-dependent: corridor = fine; GDB interior = fails the distance test for surface water.** 🔍
- **GAP — exact site coordinates not fixed; conveyance read must be redone against the chosen parcel + actual pipe routing/elevation lift.** 🔍

## 4. WHERE DOES THE ANSWER FLIP? 🔍

| Lever | Entry ~0.5–0.8 MGD | Build-out ~6–9 MGD |
|---|---|---|
| **Fit in existing JPWB headroom** | Yes — absorbable via wholesale/industrial contract (JPWB already sells to industry). 🔍 | Marginal — consumes ~all nominal ~9–11 MGD spare peak headroom; JPWB unlikely to commit drought-exposed margin to one customer. 🔍 |
| **New junior Green River permit** | Easily obtainable. 🔍 | Obtainable (Pacific Soda 14,500 ac-ft/yr precedent) — but fully curtailment-exposed. 🔍 |
| **Need for own firm/senior supply** | Low. 🔍 | **High — this is the flip.** Build-out needs a dedicated drought-firm right: acquire senior ag right + change use, OR contract Fontenelle M&I storage. 🔍 |
| **Conveyance (corridor site)** | Inside 20–30 mi. 🔍 | Inside 20–30 mi. 🔍 |
| **Groundwater-only feasibility** | Possibly (small, deep wells like corridor towns). 🔍 | No (no high-yield potable aquifer; would force desal). 🔍 |

**The flip is NOT mainly the MGD number — it's the RELIABILITY tier.** Entry load rides spare system headroom / a small junior permit. Build-out forces the project to **own drought-firm supply** (senior-right acquisition or Fontenelle storage contract) to survive a post-2026 compact-call scenario. The physical water and the conveyance both exist at build-out scale; the binding question is **priority date / curtailment insurance + securing a firm block**, not whether 6–9 MGD physically exists in the Green River. 🔍

A *second* flip is **geographic**: a corridor (Green River/Rock Springs/I-80) site passes on surface + conveyance; a deep-GDB-interior site fails conveyance-to-surface and is pushed onto saline groundwater + desal, where the economics get materially worse. 🔍
