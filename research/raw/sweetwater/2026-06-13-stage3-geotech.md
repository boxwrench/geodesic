# Sweetwater County, WY — Stage-3 (Validate) Geological / Geotechnical Screen
**Project:** Geodesic | **Stage:** 3 (Validate) | **Mode:** B DRAFT | **Date:** 2026-06-13
**Scope:** Physical showstoppers for city-scale build (foundations, roads, water/sewer) on plausible Sweetwater sites.
**Truth audit:** Mode-B → EVERYTHING tagged 🔍 (unverified draft). No fabrication. Human gaps flagged 🧑.

---

## TL;DR VERDICT 🔍
**Geotech = COST, not a KILL.** No physical showstopper for a city sited on the Rock Springs–Green River / I-80 corridor (Rock Springs Uplift flank). The serious hazards (trona-mine collapse subsidence, dunes, interior playa/sodic soils) are **spatially separable** — they cluster in zones you would site *away from* anyway. The real geotech cost drivers are routine for the high-desert West: expansive/sodic clays (Wasatch Fm claystone) and corrosive saline soils → engineered foundations + corrosion-protected buried infrastructure. Relative to the water / land-assembly / federal (sage-grouse, checkerboard) constraints already on the board, geotech ranks **well below** as a gating risk.

## 1. SUBSIDENCE / VOID RISK 🔍

### Trona (the headline hazard)
- World's largest trona deposits, Green River Basin, mostly Sweetwater Co. Beds ~800–3,000 ft deep; main mined Bed 17 ~1,500 ft deep, ~12 ft thick, ~100 mi² extent. Mined by room-and-pillar, longwall, and ISR solution. 🔍
- **Mining is geographically CONCENTRATED WEST of Green River**, in a belt running roughly W/NW from Green River toward Granger / Little America. The WSGS seismic report explicitly treats "the trona mine region west of Green River" as a distinct seismic sub-zone. 🔍
- **Documented collapse events (real subsidence/seismic risk):**
  - **3 Feb 1995 — Solvay mine collapse, near Little America.** A ~3,000 ft × 7,000 ft (≈1 km × 2 km) panel collapsed abruptly; **M5.3 (WSGS) / reported M5.1 (press)** event, 1 fatality. ~9 cm of surface subsidence measured NW of the mine. MSHA attributed it to long-term pillar-floor strength degradation. ⚠️ **Magnitude discrepancy flagged (5.1 vs 5.3) — needs reconcile.** 🔍
  - **30 Jan 2000 — M4.4**, ~20 mi W of Green River, Solvay roof collapse.
  - **25 Jun 1994 — M3.7**, General Chemical mine partial collapse.
  - Numerous M2–3 events 1985–2001 in the Granger/Green River mine belt, several confirmed or suspected mine-related.
- **Read:** Catastrophic room-and-pillar collapse is a REAL, demonstrated hazard — but it is a hazard *over the active mine panels west of Green River*, not under the Rock Springs/I-80 corridor proper. A city should NOT be sited over or immediately adjacent to active/abandoned trona panels. Mineral-estate + active-mine-footprint mapping is mandatory before any parcel is committed. 🔍 🧑

### ISR Uranium (Lost Creek)
- Lost Creek ISR (Ur-Energy), south-central Sweetwater, operating since 2013; expansion + deeper "KM Horizon" + Class I disposal wells injecting 5,622–9,561 ft deep. 🔍
- ISR dissolves uranium in place via injection/recovery wells — **no large mined voids → low classic subsidence risk** (unlike trona room-and-pillar). Main ISR concerns are groundwater/aquifer integrity, not ground collapse. No subsidence concern surfaced in search. 🔍
- Location is well away from the urban corridor (it's out in the basin interior). Not a foundation issue. 🔍

### Oil/Gas + legacy coal
- Oil/gas across the basin; **legacy underground COAL mines under/near Rock Springs & Reliance** are historically documented (1910 & 1930 quakes coincided with coal-mine cave-ins near Rock Springs/Reliance). ⚠️ **Old shallow coal workings under the Rock Springs area are a genuine localized void/subsidence concern — parcel-level abandoned-mine-land (AML) check required.** 🔍 🧑
- Induced seismicity from injection: no specific documented swarm found for Sweetwater in this pass; Lost Creek deep-disposal wells are a watch item, not a known problem. 🔍

**Subsidence vs corridor net:** Concentrated trona-collapse risk is W of Green River; ISR is basin-interior; the live corridor concern is **legacy shallow coal under Rock Springs/Reliance**. All three are *avoidable by siting* + parcel-level AML/mineral mapping. 🔍

## 2. SEISMICITY 🔍
**Source: WSGS "Basic Seismological Characterization for Sweetwater County" (Case, Toner, Kirkwood, 2002).**
- Baseline hazard is **LOW**. County is mostly **UBC Seismic Zone 1** (5–10%g effective peak accel.); far W/NW edge nudges into Zone 2. 🔍
- USGS probabilistic PGA: **500-yr ~5–9%g** (Intensity V); **2,500-yr ~13–20%g** (Intensity VI–VII). Rock Springs/Green River ~20%g / ~18%g at 2,500-yr → Intensity VII (slight–moderate damage to ordinary structures, worse for unreinforced masonry). 🔍
- Active faults are mostly distant/peripheral: Chicken Springs fault system (NE corner, Holocene, ~M6.5 capable — worst near Bairoil, far from corridor); South Granite Mountain, Bear River, Rock Creek, Grey's River systems all produce <2–5%g at the urban corridor. 🔍
- **Note (amplification):** USGS PGA assumes firm rock/soil; soft, saturated basin sediments AMPLIFY shaking. Site-specific response matters where shallow water table + soft sediment coincide. 🔍
- **Read:** Seismicity is a **routine IBC design input, not a constraint.** Standard modern building code (IBC, which the county was adopting) fully covers it. Mine-collapse-induced events (the M5.3 class) are the larger ground-motion source — and again that's a *don't-build-over-the-mine* issue, not a regional baseline. 🔍

## 3. SOILS (SSURGO / WSS) 🔍 🧑
**⚠️ HUMAN GAP: SSURGO/Web Soil Survey is an interactive AOI tool — it cannot return map-unit data without drawing/uploading a polygon (coordinates) for the candidate site. I could not run it without a defined AOI. This section is from regional/parent-material reasoning, NOT a site-specific SSURGO pull.** 🧑
- **Expansive clays:** Wasatch Fm (drab/variegated claystone & siltstone) and marine-shale parent materials are widespread → **shrink-swell (expansive) clay risk is real and regionally common.** Wyoming is the type locality for swelling bentonite. Expect engineered/over-excavated foundations, moisture control, possibly piers in claystone areas. 🔍
- **Saline / sodic soils:** Documented to occur naturally in basin depressions underlain by sodium-bearing marine shale, and acutely in the **Great Divide Basin interior** (endorheic → salts concentrate; alkali flats). Sodic (ESP>15) / saline-sodic soils → poor bearing, dispersive soils (erosion/piping in road & dam fills), and **high corrosion risk to buried steel/concrete (water & sewer pipe, rebar)**. → corrosion-protected/PE pipe, sulfate-resistant concrete. 🔍
- **Shallow water table / playa:** concentrated in basin lows and playas (esp. Great Divide Basin) → seismic amplification + septic infeasibility + dewatering for trenching. **Septic implication: alkali-flat / shallow-WT / sodic ground is poor for on-site septic → favors centralized sewer (consistent with a planned city anyway).** 🔍
- **Read:** Soils are a **COST + design** layer (expansive clay foundations, corrosion mitigation, avoid playa/sodic lows), not a kill. Worst soils (interior Great Divide playa/sodic) coincide with land you'd avoid for other reasons (remote, no infrastructure, sage-grouse). 🔍

## 4. TERRAIN / BUILDABLE LAND 🔍
- **Rock Springs Uplift:** ~56 × 28 mi domal uplift; Green R. canyon-cut tableland of spires/buttes/scarps rising 500+ ft → **badlands/steep slope locally**, but broad benches and the I-80/UP rail corridor are buildable and already developed (Rock Springs, Green River, Reliance, Superior). 🔍
- **Killpecker Sand Dunes:** ~109,000 ac, world's 2nd-largest ACTIVE dune field, stretching ~55 mi E from the Green River Basin across the Continental Divide; dunes 100+ ft, actively migrating, **N of Rock Springs.** **Active migrating dunes = do-not-build / avoid** (burial, instability). Spatially distinct from the I-80 corridor. 🔍
- **Red Desert / Great Divide Basin interior:** high desert, dunes + alkali flats + endorheic — remote, poor soils, no infrastructure → effectively non-buildable for a city for *non-geotech* reasons too. 🔍
- **Read:** Ample buildable land exists ON the developed I-80 / Rock Springs–Green River corridor (gentle benches off the uplift). Dunes and badlands are real but **localized and avoidable**. Buildable-land availability is NOT a constraint if you stay on the corridor. 🔍

## 5. NET READ — KILL / COST / NON-ISSUE 🔍
**COST (low-to-moderate, manageable), trending NON-ISSUE if sited on-corridor.**
- The two "scary" items (trona-collapse subsidence + M5+ mine-induced quakes) are the SAME hazard and are **spatially bounded to the trona belt W of Green River** — solved by siting + mineral/mine-footprint mapping.
- Legacy shallow coal under Rock Springs/Reliance is the one corridor-proximal void risk → parcel-level AML check.
- Baseline seismicity is low (Zone 1) → routine IBC design.
- Soils (expansive clay, sodic/saline, corrosion) and dunes/badlands are standard high-desert engineering + avoidance — real cost, not a gate.
- **Geotech does NOT rise to the level of water / land-assembly (checkerboard) / federal (sage-grouse) constraints already identified.** Rank it LOW among Sweetwater's gating risks.
