# Sweetwater County, WY — Stage 3 (Validate): Transmission Interconnection Headroom

**Date:** 2026-06-13 (research run); cache filename retained per task spec.
**Mode:** B (DRAFT). **Everything below is 🔍 UNVERIFIED draft.** Nothing here is ✅.
**Question:** Is a tie-in for a new ~100–200 MW *load* (data center) at/near Jim Bridger / Anticline / the 500 kV corridor physically + procedurally real? Where does the answer flip on the real spec load (10 MW entry vs 200 MW build-out)?
**Key reframe carried from Stage 1/2:** proximity ≠ headroom. A large new LOAD goes through the same kind of study/contract gauntlet as new generation.

---

## 0. BOTTOM LINE (draft) 🔍

- **Proximity = strong, headroom = unconfirmed and contested.** The Jim Bridger / Anticline 230–500 kV hub is right there, but Wyoming's grid is being hit by a *statewide* data-center demand wave (proposals up to ~10,000 MW vs ~10,200 MW total state generation). Whether ~100–200 MW of *firm* headroom exists at this specific hub is **not knowable from public sources** — it requires a PacifiCorp system impact study. 🔍
- **The process is well-defined and PacifiCorp publishes it.** Rocky Mountain Power (RMP, the PacifiCorp division serving WY) runs a formal **Large Service Request** process for loads ≥25 MW, $150k minimum study fee, ~6-month study target. 🔍
- **The flip point is bright-line and official: 200 MW.** RMP's own published WY thresholds: **25–199 MW** = case-by-case, Master Electric Service Agreement (MESA); **≥200 MW** = **Special Contract requiring Wyoming PSC commission approval, full project-cost security, and incremental generation requirements**. So the spec "build-out ~200 MW" lands *exactly* on the line that converts this from a contract into a regulated transmission-and-generation project. 🔍
- **Coal transition is a double-edged answer, leaning "not a clean handoff."** Bridger Units 1&2 already converted to gas (2024); Units 3&4 are slated for carbon capture ~2030 and operation to ~2039–2042 (plan has flip-flopped gas↔CCS across IRPs). The switchyard is NOT being vacated — it stays energized and is a transmission anchor — but freed-up *injection* capacity from a smaller/retiring plant does not automatically become *load-serving* headroom, and CCS retrofits actually consume station power (parasitic load). **Net effect on available load headroom: indeterminate, more likely neutral-to-negative than a windfall.** 🔍

## 1. HEADROOM — is there room for ~100–200 MW of new load here? 🔍

**What's physically present (from Stage 1/2, carried forward):**
- Jim Bridger complex switchyard, ~10 mi N of Point of Rocks. 230/345/500 kV in the immediate area. 🔍
- **Anticline substation** (500/345 kV, near Point of Rocks) — terminus of the Aeolus–Anticline 500 kV line; 5-mi 345 kV tie to the Jim Bridger substation. 🔍
- **Gateway West** (Aeolus → Jim Bridger, 230 + 500 kV) and **Gateway South** 500 kV (~34 mi through the county). 🔍
- These were built to **deliver up to ~1,500 MW** outbound, principally west/south to Utah & Idaho, and to integrate >1 GW of new WY wind. (NS Energy / RMP) 🔍

**Why proximity does NOT settle headroom:** 🔍
- The Gateway/Anticline capacity was justified and largely *committed* to moving WY wind generation OUT to Utah/Idaho load and improving reliability — it is an **export corridor**, not spare import/local-load capacity sitting idle. 🔍
- Statewide context (WyoFile/Governing, Aug 2025): Wyoming generation ≈ **10,200 MW**; in-state consumption ≈ 40% of that, the rest exported. Announced data-center interest (Crusoe/Tallgrass ~1,800 MW → "hyperscale" to 10,000 MW; Prometheus +1,200 MW in **SW Wyoming**; Microsoft/Meta around Cheyenne) **could equal or exceed the entire state's generation.** Utilities say they "field new inquiries weekly." 🔍
- **Implication:** even where wires exist, the binding question is firm capacity + generation to serve a 24/7 load. A ~100–200 MW load is small against 10,200 MW statewide but is competing in a queue against multi-GW requests, and the corridor's purpose is export. **Headroom for THIS load at THIS hub = unknown, study-dependent.** 🔍
- **WECC flagged this exact risk class:** WECC published a "Large Loads Risk Assessment" (2025) — i.e., the reliability coordinator is treating concentrated large-load interconnection as a system risk, not a routine add. (Document is on wecc.org; not fully parsed here — see gaps.) 🔍

**Headroom verdict (draft):** Cannot confirm firm headroom for 100–200 MW at Jim Bridger/Anticline from public data. Proximity is a genuine PASS; headroom is an **open, study-gated question** trending toward "constrained / must-be-paid-for-by-causer," consistent with WY's "causer pays" regulatory posture. 🔍

## 2. COAL TRANSITION — does Bridger's wind-down free up capacity or strand it? 🔍

**Status of the units (multiple sources, plan has changed repeatedly):** 🔍
- **Units 1 & 2:** converted coal→natural gas, **completed 2024** (stopped burning coal ~2023–24, operating on gas). 🔍
- **Units 3 & 4 (~700 MW combined):** plan has oscillated — 2023 IRP said gas-convert by 2030; 2024 IRP switched to **carbon capture (CCUS) retrofit ~2028–2030**, operate through **~2039 (some sources 2042)**. PacifiCorp's own analysis flags CCUS as **higher-cost than alternatives (~$1B+/unit, ~$1.3B for 3&4)** — realism of the CCS path is uncertain. 🔍
- Plant slated to **fully close ~2039**. 🔍

**Effect on switchyard / load headroom:** 🔍
- **The switchyard stays.** Whether units run on coal, gas, or CCS-coal, the Jim Bridger/Anticline buses remain a live, high-voltage transmission node — so the *interconnection point* is durable, not disappearing. This is good for siting a load nearby. 🔍
- **But "retiring generator ≠ free load headroom."** Three reasons it's not a clean handoff: 🔍
  1. Capacity freed by a retiring *generator* is generation-side injection headroom on an *export* corridor; converting that into firm *load-serving* capacity is a different study and may require new generation/imports. 🔍
  2. **CCS retrofits add parasitic load** — a CCS-equipped Bridger 3&4 consumes a meaningful slice of its own output, *reducing* net deliverable capacity, not adding room. 🔍
  3. PacifiCorp is short on dispatchable capacity region-wide (2025 IRP adds 4.7 GW renewables / 1.7 GW storage by 2031) — the system is *adding* resources to meet load growth, signaling the area is capacity-hungry, not capacity-rich. 🔍
- **FERC angle (generator-replacement interconnection):** PacifiCorp has a FERC-approved process to let new generation reuse interconnection rights of *retiring* plants — relevant if the project paired a new on-site/adjacent generator with the load using Bridger's interconnection rights. (Utility Dive coverage; anti-competitive concerns were raised by UAMPS/NewSun.) This is a **generation** mechanism, not a load mechanism, but it's the kind of lever a co-located generate-to-serve-load play would use. 🔍

**Coal-transition verdict (draft):** Switchyard durability = mild positive for siting. "Freed capacity" windfall = **largely a myth here** — neutral to negative for load headroom once parasitic CCS load and export-corridor commitment are accounted for. 🔍

## 3. PROCESS / TIMELINE — RMP large-load interconnection + WECC pathway 🔍

**Source: RMP "Large Service Requests" page (primary, fetched 2026).** 🔍

RMP Wyoming large-load process (loads ≥25 MW; <25 MW handled by Builders & Contractors / standard service):
- **Step 1 — Request:** email Largeloads@PacifiCorp.com → get Customer Information Form + Financial Information Requirements; submit by email. 🔍
- **Step 2 — Submittal:** package is "Submitted" only when the **Large Load Engineering Service Agreement (LLESA) is executed AND payment received.** 🔍
- **Step 3 — Evaluation:** WY accepts requests **on a continual basis** (note: UT uses fixed Apr 1 / Oct 1 windows; WY is rolling). 🔍
- **Step 4 — System Impact Study → Service Proposal:** RMP returns required interconnection mods/upgrades, **cost estimates**, an estimated service timeline, and identifies needed **Transmission Service Requests (TSRs)**. For **>200 MW**, also identifies **incremental generation requirements**. 🔍
- **Step 5 — Contract:**
  - **25–199 MW → Master Electric Service Agreement (MESA)** (or special contract if applicable). 🔍
  - **≥200 MW → Special Agreement / Special Contract.** 🔍

**Cost & timing (official):** 🔍
- **Study fee: minimum $150,000**, payable on execution of the Engineering Service Agreement. 🔍
- **Study target: ≤6 months** after submittals + payment received — *but* explicitly "can be impacted by … integration with other large load requests on the system" (i.e., the queue of competing data centers can stretch it). 🔍
- **Customer pays all incremental costs:** design/EPC of large-load facilities, **incremental generation costs**, long-term O&M of those facilities, and (to the extent FERC permits) **transmission system improvements.** This is full "causer pays." 🔍

**Commission approval:** 🔍
- **≥200 MW special contracts REQUIRE Wyoming PSC approval.** <200 MW analyzed case-by-case, generally not requiring a full PSC proceeding. 🔍

**WECC / transmission-service pathway (the slower track):** 🔍
- If serving the load needs new transmission capacity, the project also needs **Transmission Service Requests on PacifiCorp OASIS (OATI)** and the upgrades flow through PacifiCorp transmission planning + WECC review. 🔍
- PacifiCorp moved its generator interconnection to a **cluster, "first-ready, first-served"** process; the broader interconnection backlog is enormous (historically tens of GW vs ~12.6 GW peak load). General industry median request-to-operation ≈ **~5 years**; transmission-dependent large loads can hit the often-cited **24–72 month** power-availability delay. 🔍

**Regulatory backdrop (WY-specific, 2025–2026):** 🔍
- WY has long-standing **"large load" special-contract rules** intended to insulate regular ratepayers (Black Hills/Cheyenne Light pioneered with Microsoft in 2015; contracts allow interruptibility + market-sourced power so no new plants get built on the backs of residential ratepayers). 🔍
- Wyoming Energy Authority ran a 2025 stakeholder working group; **Gov. Gordon signed Executive Order 2026-03 "Data Centers the Wyoming Way" (June 3, 2026)** — 60-day deadline for agency policy/legislative recommendations. Regulatory framework is **actively in flux**, which adds timeline/political risk to a build-out-scale request. 🔍
- Cross-state signal: **Oregon PUC approved PacifiCorp penalties for large-load customers** whose actual use deviates from forecast — PacifiCorp is tightening large-load terms system-wide; expect similar discipline (minimum take, deviation penalties, long-term security) in WY contracts. 🔍

## 4. WHERE THE ANSWER FLIPS ON THE REAL SPEC LOAD 🔍

| Spec load | Regime (RMP WY, official) | Practical read 🔍 |
|---|---|---|
| **~5–10 MW entry** | **Below 25 MW** → standard Builders & Contractors / business service, NOT the large-load process. | **Trivial / near-non-event.** A ~10 MW load near an existing 230/500 kV hub with local distribution is an ordinary service connection. No special contract, no PSC, no $150k study. This is a PASS. 🔍 |
| **25–199 MW (mid build-out)** | **Large Service Request → System Impact Study → MESA.** $150k+ study, ~6-mo target, case-by-case PSC. Customer pays incremental T + generation. | **Real project but contractual.** Doable if the study finds capacity; risk is study outcome + cost allocation + queue position behind GW-scale data centers. 🔍 |
| **~200 MW full build-out** | **≥200 MW → Special Contract.** **Requires WY PSC approval, FULL project-cost security, identified INCREMENTAL GENERATION requirements.** | **This is the flip.** At ~200 MW the project stops being "plug into spare wires" and becomes a **regulated transmission + generation project**: you must likely bring/contract new generation, post full-project security, win a PSC proceeding (public, ratepayer-protection scrutiny, currently under fresh EO/legislative review), and possibly fund transmission upgrades + TSRs through the WECC/OASIS track (multi-year). 🔍 |

**One-line answer:** It flips **at exactly 200 MW**, per RMP's own published Wyoming threshold — the build-out target sits on the bright line where a contract becomes a commission-approved, generation-backed, fully-secured transmission project. Entry-scale (~10 MW) is trivial; the strategic risk lives entirely in the build-out tail. 🔍
