## 2. BLM half — checkerboard land exchange feasibility 🔍

### Mechanism exists, with WY precedent 🔍
- BLM **land exchanges** (FLPMA §206, 43 USC 1716) are the standard tool to consolidate checkerboard: private party trades scattered inholdings elsewhere for BLM sections to build a contiguous block. Must be **equal value** (appraisal) and in the **public interest**.
- **Active WY precedent:** BLM-proposed exchange in **Campbell County** with Quarter Circle 7 Ranch — transfer up to **4,550 ac scattered BLM** to private in exchange for **~2,418 ac private**, goal = consolidate public land into a contiguous ~8,500-ac block. Demonstrates BLM *will* do consolidation exchanges in WY. [WebSearch result — confirm via BLM NEPA register]
- Legal backdrop specific to this checkerboard: **Leo Sheep Co. v. United States** (Carbon Co., UP land-grant checkerboard) — SCOTUS held the Union Pacific Act reserved **no implied federal easement** across private sections. Relevant to access, not exchange mechanics.

### **The binding 2024 complication — Rock Springs RMP 🔍**
- The federal sections in the Sweetwater checkerboard fall under the **BLM Rock Springs Field Office RMP** (~3.6M ac surface / 3.5M ac mineral; covers Sweetwater + 4 counties).
- **Dec 20, 2024:** BLM issued the **Record of Decision** approving the final Rock Springs RMP — then **DOI placed a temporary hold** on it.
- Final RMP reportedly **expanded ROW/utility EXCLUSION areas from ~400,000 → >900,000 ac** and **ROW AVOIDANCE areas from ~730,000 → >1,000,000 ac**, including across the checkerboard. Gov. Gordon filed a **Consistency Review** (Oct 2024) seeking to scale these back.
- Sources: https://www.blm.gov/press-release/blm-updates-management-plan-rock-springs-field-office ; https://www.greenriverstar.com/story/2024/10/31/county/gov-submits-blm-rmp-consistency-review/12119.html ; https://www.sweetwaternow.com/county-commissioners-discuss-next-steps-regarding-blms-rmp-decision/ ; https://www.uwyo.edu/law/centers/center-for-land-and-water-law/blog/rock-springs-resource-management.html
- **Implication (🔍):** (a) Land-tenure/disposal categories and exchange eligibility are governed by this *contested, on-hold* RMP — feasibility and stipulations are a **moving target** until the RMP is finalized/litigated. (b) The same RMP's exclusion/avoidance overlays are a **direct ROW risk** for transmission/pipeline — cross-reference the transmission Stage-3 file.

### Rough feasibility / timeline (🔍, judgment)
- **Feasible in principle, slow in practice.** A consolidation exchange to build a clean ≥5,000-ac contiguous block from checkerboard sections is exactly what FLPMA exchanges are for, and BLM-WY is doing one (Campbell Co.).
- **Timeline:** BLM exchanges routinely run **3–7+ years** (appraisal, NEPA, public comment, litigation risk). The on-hold Rock Springs RMP adds **uncertainty on top of that**.
- **You'd still need the private counterparty** (Sweetwater/URC) to agree, since you must contribute private inholdings to trade — so the exchange path is **downstream of, not a substitute for, the Orion/Sweetwater negotiation.**
- **Human gap:** pull the Rock Springs RMP land-tenure (disposal/retention) maps from BLM eplanning (project 13853) to see which specific sections are exchange-eligible. https://eplanning.blm.gov/eplanning-ui/project/13853/510

## 3. Labor pool within ~60 min — vs spec ≥10,000 construction-relevant 🔍

### Hard numbers 🔍
- **Sweetwater County civilian labor force (BLS/FRED, WYSWEE7LFN): 19,739 (Apr 2026)**; ~19,889 (Dec 2025); ~20,000 (Jan 2026). https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/WYSWEE7LFN
- Unemployment rate **~4.3% (Dec 2024 & Dec 2025)**; ~3.3–3.6% mid-2023. At ~4.3% of ~19,700, only **~600–850 people actively seeking work** countywide. https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/WYSWEE7URN
  - 2023 WyoFile: "only 633 people seeking work" in Sweetwater (corroborates the very thin slack).
- County population **~42,000**; principal towns Rock Springs + Green River (both within ~30 min of each other and of I-80 sites).

### Construction-relevant base 🔍
- **2024 was a DOWN year for construction in Sweetwater:** Q2 2024 the county **lost >300 construction jobs**; Q4 2024 county lost 575 jobs total incl. construction + mining. [WY DWS QCEW news Q2/Q4 2024]
- Trona industry employs **~2,300** across 4–5 majors (Tronox, Tata, Solvay, Sişecam/Ciner) — skilled industrial/maintenance workforce, but **employed, not available**, and not all construction-craft.
- No published figure found for "construction-relevant workers within 60 min" specifically → estimated from total construction employment. **County construction employment is on the order of low-thousands at most, and shrank in 2024** → **well below 10,000.** → **human gap** to get exact QCEW NAICS-23 headcount (pull WY DWS QCEW table for Sweetwater).

### The decisive context — region is ALREADY import-dependent for construction 🔍
- WyoFile (Oct 2023): **5 major projects** (trona expansions, 2 CO2/carbon projects, TerraPower Natrium $4B nuclear at Kemmerer in adjacent Lincoln Co.) collectively need **>6,000 temporary construction workers over 5 years** — and developers say flatly: **"we just don't have the population in Wyoming to support all of these jobs… even if everyone stayed… we just don't have the population"** (Craig Rood, Project West). https://wyofile.com/construction-boom-labor-and-housing-challenges-loom-in-sw-wyoming/
- Single Pacific Soda project alone: avg ~2,100 construction workers, peak ~4,200. [wyomingnews Rocket Miner]
- Housing is the binding bottleneck (avg Rock Springs home ~$400k in 2023; rental stock weak).

### **Labor read vs threshold (🔍, judgment)**
- **FAIL on the literal ≥10,000-construction-within-60-min spec.** Total county civilian labor force is only ~19,700; construction is a small, *shrinking* slice; effective local unemployed pool is <1,000.
- **It is IMPORT-ONLY for a project of meaningful scale**, competing directly against TerraPower/trona/CO2 projects for the same national traveling craft workforce AND for scarce housing in the same 5-year window. This is a **labor + housing competition risk**, not just a headcount shortfall.
- Mitigants: existing skilled industrial base (~2,300 trona), Western Wyoming Community College + SW WY Manufacturing Partnership training pipeline, I-80 access. But these don't close a 10,000-worker gap locally.
