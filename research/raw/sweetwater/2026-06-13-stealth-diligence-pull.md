## 0. Headline read

Sweetwater is a **pro-development, single-industry (trona/extraction) county where the litigation energy runs in the OPPOSITE direction from the "Greenbelt Alliance" pattern.** The county government itself is the would-be plaintiff — against the *federal* government — fighting *conservation restrictions* that limit development. There is no evidence (in this pull) of an organized local slow-growth/anti-development litigant with a win record. The dominant land-use fight is the **BLM Rock Springs Resource Management Plan (RMP)**, where conservation groups push protections and the county + state + industry push back hard for development access.

For a new-city/large-project developer, the relevant signal is inverted: the *political class wants the development*. The friction is (a) **federal land control** (BLM manages ~3.6M acres out of the Rock Springs office, the bulk of the county) and (b) **sage-grouse / wildlife-corridor conservation overlays**, not a local NIMBY litigation machine. 🔍

## 1. Litigation scrape — developer / water litigation

### 1a. The central fight: BLM Rock Springs RMP (county as would-be plaintiff vs. feds)
- BLM finalized the Rock Springs RMP (Record of Decision **Dec. 20, 2024**, end of Biden admin) on a conservation-heavy "Alternative B" that would designate ~1.8M acres (~half the planning area) as Areas of Critical Environmental Concern (ACECs). 🔍
- State officials and the Sweetwater County Board of County Commissioners loudly opposed it, saying BLM denied all protests and that the plan hinders development and public use. 🔍
- **County considered suing BLM.** Chief Deputy County Attorney noted negotiation would precede any suit; Special Counsel Danielle Bettencourt discussed the standing/harm bar (county must show present, not future, harm). As of the pull, posture = *evaluating litigation*, not confirmed filed. 🔍
- April 2025: county "turned to [Rep. Harriet] Hageman for help" — i.e., pursuing a political/congressional remedy in parallel. 🔍
- Trump admin put the RMP on hold after Jan 2025; Oct 2025 it was **reopened for public comment**; Dec 2025 reporting says feds were ordered to redo the 12-year plan on a compressed timeline. So the RMP is in active flux. 🔍

### 1c. Water litigation
- **No Sweetwater-specific developer water-rights lawsuit surfaced.** What surfaced is statewide/regional: WY joined a 24-state suit challenging the federal "waters of the U.S." (WOTUS) rule; the historic Wind/Big Horn (Big Horn III) tribal adjudication; Montana v. Wyoming (Powder/Tongue, Yellowstone River Compact). None is Sweetwater-seated litigation. 🔍
- WY water-right *transfers* (change of location/use) need Board of Control approval, must show no injury to other appropriators, and run 12–36 months — a procedural drag on any project needing to move water, but administrative, not litigation. 🔍

### 1d. Litigation posture summary
**Litigious arena? Mixed, and pointed at the feds, not at developers.** The county is comfortable threatening/contemplating litigation — but to *unlock* development against federal conservation, not to block private projects. A private developer is more likely to find the county as an *ally* than an adversary, provided the project reads as economic growth. The real legal risk to a project is being caught inside an ACEC / sage-grouse / migration-corridor overlay where *conservation* plaintiffs (national groups) litigate the federal permit. 🔍

## 2. Referendum / election history & board turnover

- **WY has no county referendum mechanism** (as flagged in the brief) — no "ballot-box veto" of a project exists here. The lever is board composition + turnover, not direct democracy. 🔍
- **Deeply one-party (R).** Countywide partisan races are decided in the GOP primary. (Statewide context: Trump ~71.6% / Harris ~25.8% in 2024.) County-level R/D registration split not pulled — see Gaps. 🔍
- **Board of County Commissioners — recent results:**
  - **2024 general:** Republicans **Mary Thoman** (9,519) and **Taylor Jones** (9,303) won/re-won, defeating Democrat Joyce Jansa Corcoran. 🔍
  - **2024 primary:** characterized as "unexpected results"; long-serving commissioner **Robb Slaughter did NOT seek re-election** (retired after ~40 yrs of county service) — turnover by retirement, not defeat. (Separately, state Rep. Clark Stith, HD-48 Rock Springs, lost his GOP primary to Darin McCann — a legislative, not commission, seat.) 🔍
  - **2022 primary (commission):** Slaughter 5,051; Island Richards 4,494; Keaton West 3,898; **incumbent Roy Lloyd 2,788 (lost)**; others trailing. An incumbent commissioner *was* unseated in 2022. 🔍
  - **2020 primary (commission):** incumbent **Wally Johnson finished 3rd and did not advance**; Mary Thoman and incumbent Lauren Schoenfeld advanced. Another incumbent displaced. 🔍
- **Flip-frequency read:** Partisan flips ~zero (R lock). But **intra-GOP turnover is real**: incumbents have lost or been displaced in the primary in multiple recent cycles (2020 Johnson, 2022 Lloyd), plus a 2024 voluntary retirement and a "surprise" primary. Translation: the *party* is stable, but *individual commissioners* are not safe — the board can change faces every cycle without a partisan swing. A multi-year project should not assume the same five votes throughout entitlement. 🔍

## 3. Standing opposition organizations (the "Greenbelt Alliance pattern")

**No local slow-growth / anti-city / land-trust litigant with an anti-development win record surfaced.** The organized groups in Sweetwater are *conservation/sportsmen* coalitions oriented at **federal land & wildlife**, and they generally frame themselves as pro-"responsible development in the right places," not anti-growth:

- **Greater Little Mountain Coalition (GLMC)** — coalition fighting to defer/limit BLM oil-&-gas leasing in the ~173,000-acre Greater Little Mountain Area until the RMP is done; explicitly says lands can be protected "while still allowing for responsible development in the right places." Allied with the County Commissioners *and* the Governor in 2018 on deferral requests — not adversarial to the county. 🔍
- **Wyoming Wildlife Federation** (Joy Bannon / Jessi Johnson) — public-lands advocacy, sage-grouse, migration corridors. 🔍
- **Wyoming Wilderness Association** — wildlands organizing. 🔍
- **Muley Fanatic Foundation** (Josh Coursey) — Rock Springs-based mule-deer/sportsmen group. 🔍
- **Theodore Roosevelt Conservation Partnership** — sportsmen/habitat. 🔍
- **Wyoming Stock Growers Land Trust** — operates a sage-grouse conservation-bank model (ag/ranch land conservation), partner in the nation's first greater sage-grouse conservation bank. 🔍

These are habitat/public-lands actors, mostly focused on the *federal* leasing/RMP process. None reads as a Greenbelt-style litigant suing to stop a private city/subdivision. Their litigation channel is federal NEPA/ESA on BLM land, not local land-use approvals. **Caveat:** absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence; a targeted search of WY District Court (3rd Judicial District) and federal D. Wyo. dockets was NOT performed. See Gaps. 🔍

## 4. Tax-base concentration — single-industry? salvation or threat?

**Yes — heavily single-industry (extraction, trona-led). Reads as SALVATION-oriented toward new economic projects.** 🔍

- ~**67% of total county revenue tied to mineral extraction.** 🔍
- Sweetwater holds the world's largest natural trona reserves ("Trona Capital of the World," Green River); ~72% of WY non-metallic/quarrying output is here. Trona contributes **>$1B/yr** to the economy. 🔍
- Four trona majors (Tronox, Tata Chemicals, Solvay, Ciner Wyoming) employ ~2,300+ (~10% of all jobs). Largest employment sector overall = Mining/Quarrying/Oil & Gas (~3,066). Other base: oil & gas, uranium, a power plant (Jim Bridger), a fertilizer plant. 🔍
- Pop. ~41.5k (42,272 in 2020 census; 4th-most-populous WY county). 🔍
- **Behavioral evidence = salvation framing:** the commission *unanimously* approved Ag→Mineral-Development rezonings for **WE Soda "Project West"** (~$2.6B trona/soda-ash plant; ~1,300 peak construction, ~300 permanent; projected $104M local sales/use tax + ~$327M property tax over life). The county courts big extraction capex; it does not treat large projects as threats. 🔍
- **Threat caveat:** single-industry counties can also be *protective* of the incumbent industry. A new project competing for scarce labor (worker shortage already flagged) or water, or threatening trona/energy, could flip sentiment. The salvation reflex applies to projects that *add* to the extraction/industrial base; an outside "new city" that reads as non-industrial or as a water/labor competitor is less certain. 🔍
