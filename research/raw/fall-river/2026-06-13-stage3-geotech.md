# Fall River County, SD — Stage 3 (Validate): Geological / Geotechnical Screen

**Date:** 2026-06-13
**Mode:** Mode-B DRAFT — *every claim tagged 🔍, nothing fabricated. Site-specific GIS data (SSURGO, FEMA NFHL at AOI, borings) flagged as HUMAN GAP.*
**Stage-3 question:** Are there physical ground constraints (karst/subsidence, expansive/collapsible soils, seismicity, radionuclides, floodplain) that make construction of a ~50,000-person master-planned city (15,000–50,000 acres, residential + light industrial) on the SW flank of the Black Hills near Hot Springs / Edgemont a **cost problem** or a **kill**? Does a buildable bench exist near the water/transmission corridor, or do hazards force the footprint away from it?

> Spec (50k people, 15k–50k acres, residential + light industrial) is a **placeholder carried from the Sweetwater pilot** — used to screen where the answer flips. Treat as screening tool, not requirement.

## TL;DR

- 🔍 **Karst/subsidence is the headline hazard, and unlike Sweetwater's trona it is NOT a clean "site-around" geometry — it is a pervasive STRATIGRAPHIC condition wrapped around the entire southern Black Hills flank.** The risk is driven by deep dissolution of **anhydrite/gypsum in the Minnelusa Formation** (~200 ft of anhydrite removed at depth) propagating upward as **breccia pipes → cover-collapse sinkholes**, PLUS shallower **gypsum in the Spearfish Formation** (the "Red Valley" red beds that ring the Hills). Hot Springs literally sits on six river terraces cut into the Spearfish, and the **Mammoth Site is a textbook collapse sinkhole inside the city limits**. ([USGS SIR 2005-5160 Field Trip Guide 1](https://pubs.usgs.gov/sir/2005/5160/PDF/FTPart1.pdf))
- 🔍 **This is the central CONCERN, possibly approaching KILL for the obvious Spearfish/Red-Valley parcels.** It is not theoretical: the **Hideaway Hills** subdivision (Black Hawk, ~8 mi NW of Rapid City, same general Black Hills gypsum problem) saw an 85-ft sinkhole open in April 2020, ~158 homes affected, ~150 homeowners suing the state for **~\$45M**, homes "essentially worthless." That is the cautionary tale for building a residential subdivision on Black Hills gypsum. ([SD Searchlight](https://southdakotasearchlight.com/2025/10/08/sinkhole-victims-ask-supreme-court-to-help-them-seek-compensation-from-state/); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/south-dakota-neighborhood-hideaway-hills-giant-sinkholes/))
- 🔍 **The buildable answer is a SITING question, not a "no":** the hazard is concentrated in the **Spearfish red-bed "Red Valley" ring and the Minnelusa/Minnekahta outcrop belt right around Hot Springs**. Moving the footprint **outboard (SE / downdip) onto the Cretaceous shales and the Pierre/Inyan Kara plains, or onto stable terrace/plains ground toward Edgemont**, escapes the worst evaporite-collapse stratigraphy — but trades into expansive-clay shale and pushes you away from Hot Springs' springs/utilities corridor. **A buildable bench plausibly exists, but probably NOT directly under Hot Springs proper.** (HUMAN GAP — needs site geology overlay.)
- 🔍 **Soils:** two competing problems. Spearfish red beds = gypsum/sulfate-bearing, locally collapsible/dissolution-prone (and corrosive to concrete/sulfate attack). Cretaceous shales outboard (Pierre, Belle Fourche, Skull Creek) = classic **expansive/swelling clays**. Either way, **site-specific SSURGO + geotech borings are mandatory**; routine but real foundation cost (engineered fill, stiffened slabs/piers, sulfate-resistant concrete). HUMAN GAP for AOI-specific values.
- 🔍 **Seismicity: LOW. Routine.** South Dakota is one of the lowest-hazard states; Black Hills PGA (2% in 50 yr) is in the low range. Expect **Seismic Design Category A or B** — no seismic kill, no special cost. (Confirm exact Ss/S1 at coordinates via USGS — HUMAN GAP, but directionally certain.)
- 🔍 **Radionuclides: a real but MANAGEABLE residential-siting flag, NOT a kill.** The **Inyan Kara Group (Fall River + Lakota Fms) hosts uranium** — this is the Edgemont uranium district and the active **Dewey-Burdock ISR project** (~10–13 mi NW of Edgemont). Uranium → radium → **radon**. Fall River County is **EPA Radon Zone 2** (moderate). Implication: avoid building directly over Inyan Kara outcrop where avoidable; **radon-resistant new construction (passive sub-slab) is cheap and standard.** Bigger issue is reputational/regulatory adjacency to active uranium permitting, not ground safety per se.
- 🔍 **Floodplain: localized and avoidable.** Fall River, Hot Brook, Cold Brook, Cheyenne River, and Angostura Reservoir define the drainage; flash-flood history exists (2008 Hot Springs–Cascade, Edgemont creeks). Floodways are narrow canyon/valley bottoms — easy to zone out of. Not a constraint on a 15k–50k-acre footprint if you stay on terraces/uplands. (FEMA NFHL at AOI = HUMAN GAP.)
- 🔍 **NET READ: COST → CONCERN, with karst as the swing factor.** Not a clean kill, but the geotech profile is materially worse than a "blank plains" site. The project lives or dies on **siting the footprint OFF the Minnelusa/Spearfish evaporite-collapse belt** while staying close enough to the Hot Springs water + 230 kV corridor. That bench likely exists toward the SE plains / Edgemont side, but its existence and size are a **HUMAN GAP requiring a geologic-map + sinkhole-inventory overlay before any land position.**

## 1. Karst / Subsidence — the defining hazard

### 1a. The mechanism (why it's stratigraphic, not a point-source like trona)
🔍 The southern Black Hills "Red Valley / racetrack" is the eroded ring of soft red beds around the uplifted core. Four stratigraphic units in the Black Hills contain dissolvable evaporite (gypsum/anhydrite) and have produced **sinkholes and subsidence that have damaged houses and water/sewage facilities** (USGS Karst Interest Group framing). The two that matter near Hot Springs:

- **Minnelusa Formation (Pennsylvanian–Permian):** interbedded sandstone/limestone/dolomite/anhydrite, >1,000 ft thick in the southern Hills. As much as **~235 ft of anhydrite** observed in subsurface near Jewel Cave. Dissolution at depth (~1,000 ft below surface, post-uplift, post-Cretaceous) removes the anhydrite, the overlying section founders, producing **collapse breccia, breccia pipes (up to ~76 m / 250 ft diameter), distorted bedding, and steep-walled cover-collapse sinkholes**. ([USGS SIR 2005-5160 FT1, Stop 1 Hot Brook Canyon & Stop 2 Mammoth Site](https://pubs.usgs.gov/sir/2005/5160/PDF/FTPart1.pdf))
- **Spearfish Formation (Permian–Triassic red beds):** red sandy shale/siltstone/sandstone with **~70 ft of interbedded gypsum** at Cascade Springs; contorted gypsum, breccia pipes, secondary fracture porosity. This is the surface unit Hot Springs is built on.
- Also **Minnekahta Limestone** (carbonate karst, scattered sinkholes up to ~300 ft diameter / 35 ft deep) and **Pahasapa (Madison) Limestone** (the regional karst aquifer; swallow holes, caves — Wind/Jewel Cave).

🔍 **Key distinction vs. Sweetwater trona:** Sweetwater subsidence was tied to a mappable mining footprint you could draw a box around. Here the hazard is the **natural geology of an entire formation belt** that rings the Hills — you cannot "mine around" it; you have to **choose stratigraphy** by moving the footprint to units that lack the buried evaporite or sit far enough outboard that breccia pipes don't reach surface.

### 1b. Hot Springs is in the bullseye
🔍 The USGS field guide is unambiguous that the **city of Hot Springs is built on all six Fall River terraces cut into the Spearfish Formation**, with the **Mammoth Site sinkhole inside the southern city limits** — a ~150×120 ft elliptical collapse over a Minnelusa breccia pipe, fed by an artesian spring. Roadcuts immediately around town show "white gypsum in Spearfish redbeds," **brecciated Minnelusa**, undulating Minnekahta from differential collapse, and Minnekahta sinkholes along Hwy 71 toward Cascade Springs (one ~300 ft diameter). This is **active, mapped, pervasive collapse terrain at and around the town center.** ([USGS SIR 2005-5160 FT1](https://pubs.usgs.gov/sir/2005/5160/PDF/FTPart1.pdf))

### 1c. The real-world cost signal: Hideaway Hills
🔍 Black Hills gypsum + residential subdivision = a documented disaster. **Hideaway Hills** (Black Hawk, near Rapid City) sits over an old gypsum mine; an **85-ft-deep, ~100×650-ft sinkhole opened April 2020**, 40+ people from 15 homes evacuated, ~158 homes affected, **~150 homeowners suing the state for ~\$45M**, "homes essentially worthless," still unresolved 6 years later. While that case involves an abandoned *mine*, the engineering lesson (pulverized gypsum in subsurface soils, dissolution, collapse) is directly transferable to natural Spearfish/Minnelusa gypsum ground. ([SD Searchlight 2025-10-08](https://southdakotasearchlight.com/2025/10/08/sinkhole-victims-ask-supreme-court-to-help-them-seek-compensation-from-state/); [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/south-dakota-neighborhood-hideaway-hills-giant-sinkholes/))

### 1d. Verdict on karst
🔍 **PERVASIVE, not point-source. CONCERN bordering on KILL for the Spearfish/Minnelusa belt at Hot Springs; survivable only by siting outboard.** Mandatory work before any land position: SDGS geologic quad maps + a **sinkhole/subsidence inventory overlay** at the AOI; likely geophysics (resistivity/microgravity) and borings on any pad near the Red Valley. **HUMAN GAP: site-specific karst footprint at candidate AOIs.**

## 2. Soils — expansive vs. collapsible/corrosive

🔍 Two regimes depending on where the footprint lands:

- **On/near the Spearfish red beds (Red Valley ring):** gypsum/sulfate-bearing soils → **collapsible/dissolution-prone ground and sulfate attack on ordinary Portland concrete.** Foundations need sulfate-resistant concrete, possible engineered overexcavation/replacement, and dissolution screening. The red beds themselves are the "red sandy shale, siltstone, sandstone, minor limestone, abundant gypsum" of the Spearfish. ([Spearfish Fm — USGS SGMC](https://mrdata.usgs.gov/geology/state/sgmc-unit.php?unit=SDTRPs%3B0))
- **Outboard onto Cretaceous shales (Skull Creek, Belle Fourche, Mowry, Pierre):** classic **high-plasticity expansive (swelling) clays** — seasonal shrink/swell, heave on slabs. Standard mitigation: moisture control, stiffened/post-tensioned slabs, drilled piers, select fill. Routine on the northern Great Plains but a real per-lot cost.

🔍 **Foundation implication:** neither regime is exotic, but **both add cost** vs. a benign granular plains site, and the **gypsum/sulfate regime is the worse of the two** (couples soil problem with the karst problem). **HUMAN GAP:** SSURGO + NRCS soil-rating data and geotech borings at the actual AOI — do not guess shrink-swell or collapse indices without coordinates. (County publishes a soils GIS layer: [Fall River County GIS soils](https://fallriver.sdcounties.org/gis-maps/fr_soils-2/).)

## 3. Seismicity — LOW / routine

🔍 South Dakota is among the **lowest seismic-hazard states**; the Black Hills are intraplate with low historic seismicity. USGS National Seismic Hazard maps put SD PGA (2% in 50 yr) in the low bins. Practical outcome: residential + light-industrial construction falls in **Seismic Design Category A or B (ASCE 7 / IBC)** — **no seismic premium, not a constraint.**

🔍 **HUMAN GAP (directionally certain):** pull exact **Ss / S1 / SDC at AOI coordinates** from the USGS Seismic Design Maps / Unified Hazard Tool ([USGS NSHM tool](https://www.usgs.gov/natural-hazards/earthquake-hazards/seismic-hazard-maps-and-site-specific-data); [seismicmaps.org](https://www.seismicmaps.org/)). Verdict won't change — confirm value only.

## 4. Radionuclides — manageable flag, not a kill

🔍 **Inyan Kara Group (Fall River + Lakota Formations) is uranium-hosting** sandstone — this is the historic **Edgemont uranium district**. The active **Dewey-Burdock in-situ recovery (ISR) project** (~10–13 mi NW of Edgemont, ~12,600 surface acres) targets uranium in Inyan Kara sandstones; EPA has issued UIC Class III/V permits, construction targeted ~2027. ([EPA Dewey-Burdock permits](https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/epa-issues-permits-dewey-burdock-uranium-recovery-project-near-edgemont-south-dakota); [USGS Dewey-Burdock core data](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/solid-phase-data-cores-proposed-dewey-burdock-uranium-situ-recovery-mine-near-edgemont))

🔍 **Residential implications:**
- **Radon:** uranium → radium → radon. Fall River County is **EPA Radon Zone 2** (moderate; SD has no Zone 3). Mitigation is standard and cheap: **radon-resistant new construction (passive sub-slab depressurization)**, easy to code-mandate citywide. ([EPA SD radon map](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2014-08/documents/south_dakota.pdf))
- **Direct gamma / soil radium:** a flag **only if** the footprint sits directly on Inyan Kara outcrop or legacy mine/mill spoil. **Avoid Inyan Kara outcrop and any legacy Edgemont mining/milling ground** (there is a historic Edgemont mill-tailings legacy — verify location). Otherwise not a ground-safety issue.
- **Reputational/regulatory:** building a master-planned residential city in the same district as an active, contested uranium ISR operation is a **narrative and permitting headwind** more than a geohazard. Worth a deliberate buffer.

🔍 **Verdict: CONCERN-minor / manageable.** Not a kill; codify radon-resistant construction, screen the AOI for uranium-bearing outcrop and legacy mine/mill ground. **HUMAN GAP:** uranium-outcrop and legacy-tailings overlay at AOI.

## 5. Floodplain / drainage — localized, avoidable

🔍 Drainage features: **Fall River** (spring-fed, runs through Hot Springs canyon), **Hot Brook / Cold Brook**, the **Cheyenne River**, and **Angostura Reservoir** (~10 mi S of Hot Springs on the Cheyenne). Documented flash-flood history: July 2008 saw 1–2 in/half-hour flooding Hot Springs to Cascade Falls and flash flooding on creeks SW of Edgemont. ([NWS historic floods](https://www.weather.gov/unr/summary-of-historic-floods-and-flash-floods))

🔍 The flood hazard is confined to **narrow canyon/valley bottoms and the Cheyenne/Angostura corridor** — a small fraction of a 15k–50k-acre footprint and straightforward to zone out of by keeping pads on terraces/uplands. Fall River County requires building permits only within the floodplain and supports FEMA LOMA processes. ([Fall River County Planning & Zoning](https://fallriver.sdcounties.org/planning-zoning/))

🔍 **Verdict: COST-minor / avoidable.** Not a constraint at this footprint scale. **HUMAN GAP:** FEMA NFHL / FIRM panels at the exact AOI to draw the Zone A/AE lines.

## NET READ (cost vs kill)

🔍 **Overall: CONCERN — a real geotech tax, not a kill, with one swing variable (karst siting).**

- **Karst/subsidence is the make-or-break.** Unlike Sweetwater (a boxable trona-subsidence footprint), Black Hills evaporite collapse is a **formation-belt condition** wrapped around the entire southern flank. Building a residential subdivision on Spearfish/Minnelusa gypsum ground is exactly the Hideaway Hills failure mode (~\$45M, homes worthless). **You do not site a 50k-person city on the Red Valley.**
- **A buildable bench plausibly EXISTS — but probably not under Hot Springs proper.** The escape is to push the footprint **outboard/downdip onto the Cretaceous-shale plains toward the SE / Edgemont side**, off the evaporite-collapse stratigraphy. That trades karst for **expansive clay** (routine cost) and trades **distance from the Hot Springs springs + 230 kV node** (the tension with the water/transmission stages). The corridor question — can you find ground that is simultaneously (a) off the gypsum belt, (b) off Inyan Kara uranium outcrop / legacy mine ground, (c) out of the flood corridors, and (d) within reach of the Hot Springs water + power corridor — is the **single most important HUMAN-GAP deliverable** before any land position.
- **Soils, seismicity, radon, floodplain are all COST-tier and routine** with standard mitigation (sulfate-resistant concrete + engineered fill OR stiffened slabs; SDC A/B = no premium; passive radon systems; floodplain zoning).
- **Cost framing:** expect a **geotech premium** (geophysical karst survey + dense borings on every pad near the Red Valley, engineered foundations, radon code) and a **smaller/constrained buildable envelope** than the raw acreage suggests. Survivable, but it makes Fall River a **worse geotech site than a blank-plains alternative** and **better only if** the outboard bench checks out.

**One-line verdict:** Not a kill — **CONCERN/COST**, gated on proving a karst-free, flood-free, uranium-free buildable bench close enough to the Hot Springs water/transmission corridor. Provisionally that bench exists toward the SE plains/Edgemont, but it is **unconfirmed and must be mapped before committing.**
