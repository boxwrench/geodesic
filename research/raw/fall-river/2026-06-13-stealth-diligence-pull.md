## 1. LITIGATION POSTURE 🔍

**Headline: Fall River is the epicenter of SW South Dakota's 20-year uranium fight. This is NOT a quiet county. There is ACTIVE, multi-front litigation/permit conflict as of mid-2026.**

### A. Dewey-Burdock ISR uranium mine (enCore Energy, ex-Powertech/Azarga) 🔍
- ISR (in-situ recovery) uranium mine ~13 mi NW of Edgemont, straddling **Fall River + Custer Counties**, near WY line. In regulatory/legal limbo ~2 decades.
- Owner chain: Powertech → merged w/ Hong Kong-based Azarga Resources (2014) → enCore Energy Corp (2021, Texas-based). 🔍
- **Federal permits status (as of late 2025 / 2026):** NRC Source Materials License and EPA UIC permits described by company as "final and effective." Project added to federal **FAST-41** fast-track permitting program **Aug 28, 2025** (Trump admin). Forecast permitting/licensing completion Q3 2026; engineering early 2026; construction of central processing plant + wellfields anticipated early 2027. 🔍
- **Litigation/appeal track record (opposition has WON rounds, repeatedly delaying):**
  - **Sept 2024:** EPA Environmental Appeals Board (EAB) ruled FOR Oglala Sioux Tribe — sent EPA Region 8 UIC permits back for further review. Opponents: Oglala Sioux Tribe, Black Hills Clean Water Alliance, NDN Collective. 🔍 (BHCWA: "they are not mining and it's 2024… they won't be anytime soon.")
  - **Sept 16, 2025:** EPA EAB *denied in full* a later petition for review by the same coalition against Class III/V UIC permits — i.e., a company-side win after EPA reissued. 🔍 (Note the back-and-forth: opponents win 2024 remand, lose 2025 petition. Net = ~20 yrs of delay.)
  - Earlier: D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals upheld NRC's grant of the extraction license over Oglala Sioux NEPA challenge. 🔍 (NOT 8th Circuit — D.C. Cir. Search did not surface an 8th Cir. Dewey-Burdock ruling; flag.)
  - **April 14, 2026:** NRC Executive Director granted extension of final completion dates for Section 106 consultation and BLM's EA / Mine Plan of Operations. 🔍
  - **May 2026:** BLM seeking public comment on Dewey-Burdock (comment deadline ~May 14, 2026 per KOTA). 🔍

### B. Chord exploration project (Nexus Uranium / Clean Nuclear Energy Corp) — THE HOT, ACTIVE 2026 FIGHT 🔍
- Canadian-parented (Nexus Uranium, CEO Jeremy Poirier; sub = Clean Nuclear Energy Corp). Filed **March 2024** application to drill exploratory holes near **Craven Canyon**, ~3 mi SE of Dewey-Burdock, southern Black Hills.
- Scope: claims ~10 sq mi; Chord project ~6 sq mi mixed federal/state; **1 sq mi of STATE land in Fall River County** is the part before the SD Board of Minerals & Environment (BME). 50 drill sites, holes to 700 ft, ~10,000 gal water/day from municipal + private sources. Plus ~17 drill sites on USFS land (Black Hills NF, project #67625) — USFS deciding if EA needed; **~1,000 public comments, opposition from six tribal nations.** 🔍
- **State preemption ruling (KEY for Wild-West/no-zoning read):** Fall River's 2022 voter-approved uranium-nuisance ordinance was raised against Chord. Clean Nuclear's attorneys argued **state law supersedes county ordinances; BME AGREED.** Hearing officer Bob Morris ruled the decision hinged on SD's distinct legal definitions of "mining operation" vs "uranium exploration operation" (separate acts). **Takeaway: county-level land-use levers are legally weak here — state minerals law preempts.** 🔍
- **Permit hearing saga (2026):**
  - Jan 2026 procedural hearing — BME member Bob Morris told opponent (Oglala Lakota organizer Taylor Gunhammer) "Your goal in life is to be a jerk." Opponents won relocation of hearing from Pierre → **Hot Springs**. 🔍
  - Hearing set Apr 13 → **postponed** (DANR notice, no reason given).
  - **Reconvened May 18–22, 2026, Mueller Civic Center, Hot Springs.** Opened with hours of opposition testimony; dozens attended to oppose. 🔍
  - **May 20, 2026 — FEDERAL LAWSUIT FILED.** Opponent **Elizabeth Lone Eagle** (pro se, no attorney) sued the BME, SD Dept of Agriculture & Natural Resources (DANR), Clean Nuclear Energy, and state employees — alleging **due-process + Lakota-language-access violations** (board failed to produce a promised Lakota interpreter). 6 other plaintiffs incl. 5 Lakota first-language speakers. Hearing **PAUSED**, no resumption date set. 🔍
  - Tied to a new SD language-translation law being tested "before its effective date" (KOTA, May 31, 2026). 🔍

### C. Historical / legacy 🔍
- Cold-War-era open-pit uranium mining + milling in the **Edgemont** area through the 1970s; some pits never reclaimed → legacy contamination is a live argument in current fights. 🔍
- City of Hot Springs has formally registered concern re: region-wide water pollution / radioactive contamination. 🔍

**Litigation posture summary:** HIGH conflict, sophisticated and persistent opposition with a documented record of delaying (not always defeating) extractive projects via federal NEPA/EPA channels + tribal treaty claims. Active federal suit + paused state permit hearing as of May–June 2026. County's own anti-mining ordinance was ruled preempted — local land-use power is thin.

## 2. REFERENDUM / ELECTION HISTORY + BOARD TURNOVER 🔍

**SD DOES have county initiative/referendum (unlike WY) — and Fall River HAS USED IT on a large extractive project.**

### Referendum 🔍
- **Nov 8, 2022:** Citizen initiative declaring uranium mining an **"unlawful public nuisance"** in Fall River County **PASSED with 56% of 3,530 votes cast.** Aimed squarely at Powertech/Dewey-Burdock. 🔍
- **BUT practical effect is contested/likely void:** SD law holds counties can't declare a nuisance out of something the state expressly permits/authorizes by statute. BME has since sided with this preemption view (see §1B). Dakota Free Press headline: "…Perhaps to No Avail." 🔍
- **Read:** Demonstrated willingness + organizational capacity to put a large project to a county-wide vote AND win a majority. The direct-democracy lever exists and has been pulled. Even if legally blunted, it's a potent signal of local sentiment and a re-usable mobilization template.

### Board of Commissioners / turnover 🔍
- **2024 cycle:** Commissioner seats hotly contested. GOP primary (June 2024) for commissioner was the **closest race — ~109 votes separated the top 5 of 7 candidates for 3 ballot spots.** Incumbent **Heath Greenough LOST his re-election bid** (chadronradio.com headline). 🔍
- Incumbents running: Joe Allen, Les Cope, Heath Greenough. Challengers: Jeannine Lecy, John Staben, Trudy Valenzuela, Sandra Wahlert. 🔍
- Board composition as of **Dec 27, 2024 minutes:** Joe Allen, Les Cope, Joe Falkenburg, Heath Greenough, Deb Russell (5-member board). 🔍
- Note: Fall River uses **hand-count** of ballots — 2024 count "lasted until 2 AM." 🔍 (Election-administration friction / distrust signal worth flagging.)
- **GAP:** Could not confirm final Nov 2024 GENERAL election winners / exact margins / full post-2024 board roster from this pull. County election-results pages are JS/PDF-gated; not fully parsed.

**Read:** Real electoral churn (at least one incumbent ousted, razor-thin primary margins). A board that can flip. Not a rubber-stamp body. Combined with the referendum tool, local political risk for a contested project is **non-trivial** despite no zoning.

## 3. STANDING OPPOSITION ORGANIZATIONS 🔍

Mature, networked, repeat-player opposition ecosystem — among the strongest in the pilot set.

- **Black Hills Clean Water Alliance (BHCWA)** — exec dir **Lilias Jarding**; lead organizer **Duane Two Bulls**. Successor to the 1980s **Black Hills Alliance** ("40-year legacy"). Tracks ALL extractive projects in the Hills (uranium, gold, graphite, lithium, rare earths, sand/gravel). Active litigant/commenter. Win record: part of coalition on 2024 EPA EAB remand win. 🔍 (bhcleanwateralliance.org)
- **Oglala Sioux Tribe** — named winning party in 2024 EPA EAB remand; longtime lead litigant against Dewey-Burdock. Pine Ridge Reservation borders Fall River (county neighbors Oglala Lakota County). Treaty claims (1868 Ft. Laramie) over all of western SD. 🔍
- **NDN Collective** — Rapid City-based Indigenous-rights org; co-petitioner. Organizer **Taylor Gunhammer** (Oglala Lakota). 🔍
- **Great Plains Tribal Water Alliance** — attorney **Chase Iron Eyes** active on Craven Canyon cultural-resource claims. (tribalwateralliance.org) 🔍
- Individual repeat opponents: **Elizabeth Lone Eagle** (lead federal plaintiff, May 2026), plus Lakota first-language plaintiffs. 🔍
- **Six tribal nations** filed formal opposition to the Chord USFS portion; 40+ people/orgs raised concerns on the state-land portion; ~1,000 USFS public comments. 🔍
- Note: this coalition is currently ALSO fighting graphite (Pe' Sla — lawsuit filed Apr 2026), gold (Dakota Gold, F3, Agnico Eagle), and lithium across the Hills — i.e., a broad, well-funded, litigation-ready standing apparatus, not a single-issue ad hoc group. 🔍

**Read:** Any project touching water, Indigenous cultural/treaty interests, or extraction in the southern Black Hills will draw a fast, experienced, multi-org response with proven federal-process leverage. Cultural/sacred-site + aquifer arguments are their winning playbook.

## 4. TAX-BASE CONCENTRATION — SALVATION vs THREAT 🔍

### Profile 🔍
- Population ~**7,385** (2025 est), slowly declining (~ -0.05%/yr). County seat **Hot Springs** ~3,700. 25th-largest of SD's counties (small).
- **Median age 54.5** (county) / **59.4 in Hot Springs** — heavily retiree-skewed (Hot Springs 65+ = ~31% vs ~16% US). 🔍
- Median household income ~$61k–$68k; **poverty ~19.7%**; SD Searchlight calls Fall River the **10th-most-impoverished county in SD** (neighbors Oglala Lakota County, the poorest). 🔍
- Employment ~3,100 jobs. Top sectors: **Health Care & Social Assistance (~626) > Agriculture/Forestry/Fishing/Hunting (~331) > Retail Trade (~330).** 🔍

### Pillars 🔍
1. **Government / healthcare anchor — Hot Springs VA Medical Center (Battle Mountain Sanitarium, 1907).** Major employer BUT **under chronic closure threat** — VA has proposed shuttering the Hot Springs campus / moving long-term care to Rapid City; state senators fighting to keep funding. A fragile anchor. 🔍 Also state Veterans Home in Hot Springs.
2. **Agriculture** — ranching/forage/grain; underpinned by **Angostura Reservoir / Dam** (Bureau of Reclamation, 1949, on the Cheyenne River) for irrigation. Angostura also = recreation/tourism draw. 🔍
3. **Tourism** — **Mammoth Site** (Hot Springs), **Wind Cave NP**, **Angostura Recreation Area**, southern Black Hills gateway. Tens of thousands of visitors/yr. 🔍
4. **Retirees** — fixed-income, in-migration of older residents; healthcare-dependent.

### Concentration / disposition read 🔍
- **Semi-diversified but FRAGILE and anchor-dependent.** No single dominant private industry; instead leans on (a) a federally-controlled, closure-threatened VA/healthcare anchor, (b) climate/water-exposed ag, and (c) tourism that depends on an *unspoiled* Black Hills landscape + clean water.
- **Salvation vs threat = SPLIT / ambivalent.** A poor, shrinking, aging county *should* read a big tax-base/jobs project as salvation. BUT: (1) the dominant non-extractive assets — tourism + retiree quality-of-life + ag irrigation — all depend on **clean water and an intact landscape**, which is precisely what the organized opposition weaponizes; and (2) voters already declared uranium mining a *nuisance* by referendum. So the county's economic structure cuts BOTH ways: it needs investment but its tourism/retiree/ag base is structurally **threatened by water-intensive or extractive** projects.
- **Implication for a Geodesic-type project:** A NON-extractive, low-water, low-visual-impact project that brings tax base/jobs could read as salvation to a struggling county — *if* it avoids the water + sacred-site + extraction triggers that activate the standing opposition machine. A water-hungry or visually intrusive project inherits the full uranium-fight opposition apparatus regardless of zoning absence.
