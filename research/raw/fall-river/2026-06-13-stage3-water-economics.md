# Fall River County, SD — Stage 3 (Validate): WATER ECONOMICS

- **Date:** 2026-06-13
- **Mode:** Mode-B DRAFT — **everything tagged 🔍 (unverified)**. Nothing here is ✅. Do not promote any figure to a decision without the HUMAN-GAP closures listed at bottom.
- **Stage-3 question:** Is extending WATER to a master-planned new city in Fall River County, SD physically AND economically real?
- **Demand frame (placeholder spec, screen against these):**
  - **Entry:** ~2,000–5,000 people / ~0.5–0.8 MGD
  - **Build-out:** ~50,000 people / ~6–9 MGD ≈ ~7,000–10,000 ac-ft/yr
- **Geography:** county seat Hot Springs (~3,400), Edgemont (~700); county pop ~7,453. Prior-appropriation state, DANR (Dept. of Agriculture & Natural Resources) administers water rights via the Water Management Board.

---

## TL;DR — the Validate read

🔍 **WATER = FAIL at build-out; CONCERN (conditional) at entry.** This is the single hardest "no" of the three pilot counties on the water axis.

- 🔍 The **entire current public water supply for all of Fall River County is ~0.80 MGD** (USGS/county profile), and **Hot Springs itself serves ~813,000 gal/day** (city CCR). The **entry city (0.5–0.8 MGD) is, by itself, as large as everything the county pumps today** — and roughly **2x the city of Hot Springs**. Entry is not trivially absorbable; it likely needs its own new appropriation + wells, not a hookup to spare capacity.
- 🔍 **Build-out (6–9 MGD / 7,000–10,000 ac-ft/yr) has no identified firm source.** It would be ~10–12x the county's entire current public draw and is in the same magnitude class as Powertech's 8,500-gpm (~12 MGD) Inyan Kara industrial request — which the SD Water Management Board **paused/denied amid overwhelming opposition** and never granted.
- 🔍 The regional hydrology is moving the **wrong way**: a July 2025 USGS study (SIR 2025-5067) found Black Hills aquifer withdrawals already **exceed recharge** in the most-populated subareas, and the region's own fix is a **$2–4B Missouri River pipeline (WDRWS)** that is decades out and not yet through a federal feasibility study.
- 🔍 **Durability is poor:** declining Madison levels, a confined karst aquifer shared with senior municipal users, **unresolved 1868 Treaty / Great Sioux Nation reserved-water claims**, and an organized, battle-tested aquifer-protection opposition (Black Hills Clean Water Alliance, Oglala Sioux Tribe, Great Plains Tribal Water Alliance) that has fought water appropriations here for 40 years.
- 🔍 **Where it flips:** the project is conceivable only at the **low end of entry (~0.5 MGD, ~1 small Madison/Inyan Kara wellfield)** and **breaks well before build-out** — likely somewhere in the **~1.5–3 MGD band**, where a new appropriation moves from "another small permit" to "a regionally significant industrial draw" that triggers the Powertech-style fight and runs into the recharge ceiling.

## 1. SOURCE AT SCALE — what could firm 0.5–0.8 MGD (entry) vs 6–9 MGD (build-out)?

### 1a. Madison aquifer (confined Black Hills karst — the regional municipal workhorse)
- 🔍 The Madison is "the biggest source of municipal water supplies in Hot Springs and several other area communities." (Popular Resistance / WISE-Uranium summary of regional reporting.)
- 🔍 **Regional recharge ceiling:** USGS SIR 2025-5067 (pub. July 30, 2025; cooperator = Western Dakota Regional Water System) estimates **mean annual precipitation recharge to the Madison at ~57,000 ac-ft** and Minnelusa ~98,100 ac-ft across the whole Black Hills study area. Total mean recharge for all six aquifers ~278,900 ac-ft; total recoverable storage ~356.9 million ac-ft (across SD+WY).
- 🔍 **The bad news for scale:** the study found modern well withdrawals and/or total appropriations **exceed mean annual recharge** in the most populous/high-growth subareas — explicitly **subarea 4 (Rapid City)** for Madison, Minnelusa AND Inyan Kara, plus Inyan Kara over-recharge in subareas 3 and 5. AE2S engineer (quoted in SD News Watch): "We're already exceeding the recharge rate in those areas… We don't feel that this level of growth and use of the Madison is sustainable."
- 🔍 **Open question / GAP:** the documented deficits cluster in the *northern/eastern* Black Hills (Box Elder–Rapid City–Sturgis). **Whether the southern Black Hills subarea(s) covering Fall River/Hot Springs still have Madison/Minnelusa recharge headroom is NOT resolved in the HTML abstract** — it requires the subarea tables in the 27 MB PDF (SIR 2025-5067). This is the central physical question and is a HUMAN GAP. Even if local headroom exists, a 6–9 MGD draw (~7,000–10,000 ac-ft/yr) is ~12–18% of the *entire Black Hills* Madison recharge — implausibly large for one subarea.

### 1b. Inyan Kara aquifer (Lakota/Fall River sandstones — the uranium-fight aquifer)
- 🔍 Inyan Kara is "currently a major drinking source for livestock" in the Dewey-Burdock area and is the aquifer Powertech sought to pump at **8,500 gpm (~12.2 MGD)** for ISR uranium mining.
- 🔍 **Water-quality penalty:** Inyan Kara water "may require treatment for gross alpha radiation, iron, manganese, sulfate, and hardness before use in public water systems" (USGS). For a municipal new-city use that's a meaningful treatment-capex/opex line, especially given the area's uranium geochemistry.
- 🔍 **Already stressed:** USGS SIR 2025-5067 found Inyan Kara appropriations exceed recharge in subareas 3, 4, and 5.
- 🔍 Inyan Kara at 6–9 MGD is essentially "be Powertech, but for houses" — same aquifer, same magnitude, same opposition coalition. See §2.

### 1c. Minnelusa aquifer
- 🔍 Larger recharge (~98,100 ac-ft/yr regionally) but **water-quality limits in the southern Black Hills**: "hardness (high calcium) and high concentrations of iron and arsenic" (USGS). Usable with treatment; not a clean drop-in. Headroom by subarea = same PDF GAP as Madison.

### 1d. Cheyenne River (surface) + Angostura Reservoir / Angostura Irrigation District
- 🔍 Cheyenne River enters SD near Edgemont; **Angostura Dam/Reservoir (completed 1949, Bureau of Reclamation, Pick-Sloan)** sits on the Cheyenne ~10 mi SE of Hot Springs.
- 🔍 **Storage:** total ~195,121 ac-ft; conservation pool ~138,761 ac-ft. Serves the **Angostura Irrigation District (~12,218 irrigable acres**, primary purpose IRRIGATION).
- 🔍 **Reliability/appropriation stress is real:** irrigation to **~50 junior water-right holders along the Cheyenne upstream of Angostura was shut off in 2008** — direct evidence the system runs short in dry years and that junior rights are not firm. The Cheyenne is an intermittent/flashy prairie river; surface yield is drought-sensitive.
- 🔍 **Could ag water be converted?** Theoretically a municipal entity could acquire Angostura District irrigation entitlements and seek a change-of-use through DANR. BUT: (i) it's Reclamation project water (federal contract, repayment, NEPA, district board consent — not a simple market buy); (ii) change-of-use in a prior-appropriation state can't injure other appropriators; (iii) treaty/tribal claims cloud Cheyenne basin water (see §4). Feasibility of an ag-to-municipal conversion at 6–9 MGD = **HUMAN GAP** (needs Reclamation Angostura Division contract terms + DANR change-application precedent).

### 1e. Regional / rural water systems
- 🔍 **Fall River Water Users District** (rural water; authorized by P.L. 105-352, 1998) serves rural Fall River County; recently **expanding capacity** (Hwy 79 pipeline; $1.4M ARPA grant for the Fairburn well, pump station, storage). This is a **rural-domestic-scale** system, not a bulk municipal/industrial supplier at MGD scale — it is itself capacity-constrained and just adding a single well.
- 🔍 **Southern Black Hills Water System** studied serving "south of Hot Springs to Edgemont" — corridor-relevant but again small-system scale.
- 🔍 **Western Dakota Regional Water System (WDRWS):** the only entity contemplating bulk new water at scale — a **161–165 mi, 71–72 in. Missouri River pipeline**, est. **$2–4B**, 2,000-ft lift, **47 communities** signed on (Winner→Fort Pierre→Rapid City). **Future Use Permit issued by the Water Management Board May 7, 2025**; still seeking **Congressional authorization for a Bureau of Reclamation feasibility study**. Timeline: "two decades or more." Fall River/Hot Springs is at the **far southwest edge** — not a named core node in the reporting, so even if built, a Fall River spur is speculative and far in the future. **GAP: is Fall River/Hot Springs/Edgemont a WDRWS partner?**

## 2. APPROPRIATION STATUS / AVAILABILITY (DANR)

- 🔍 SD is prior-appropriation; DANR + the **Water Management Board** grant permits. A new municipal/industrial appropriation must show **(a) unappropriated water is available, (b) the use is beneficial, (c) it won't unlawfully impair existing rights, (d) public interest.**
- 🔍 **Region-wide signal of scarcity:** USGS SIR 2025-5067 finds **total appropriations already exceed mean annual recharge** in subarea 4 (Rapid City) for Madison/Minnelusa/Inyan Kara and in subareas 3 & 5 for Inyan Kara. A Future Use Permit regime exists (Rapid City, WDRWS) — meaning available water is being *reserved* by incumbents, which works against a newcomer.
- 🔍 **The governing precedent is Powertech/Dewey-Burdock:** applications to withdraw **8,500 gpm + 551 gpm** from the Inyan Kara (industrial). The Water Management Board **paused the case in 2013 and in Oct. 2021 unanimously rejected the motion to restart** the water-permit process, after opposition from "dozens of citizens, intervenors and state officials." **Net: there is NO precedent for a large (multi-MGD) new industrial/municipal appropriation succeeding here — the one attempt was effectively blocked for a decade+.**
- 🔍 **Is the basin formally "closed/over-appropriated"?** Could not verify a formal Fall River basin closure. SD does designate fully-appropriated aquifers (e.g., Tulare/Spink). **DANR's per-aquifer availability status for the Fall River/southern Black Hills subareas and the live water-right records are JS-/portal-gated → HUMAN GAP** (danr.sd.gov OfficeOfWater water-rights & aquifer pages did not return queryable data via fetch).

## 3. CONVEYANCE ECONOMICS (vs ~20–30 mi threshold)

- 🔍 **Local groundwater is the only short-haul option.** Madison/Inyan Kara/Minnelusa wells sited in the Hot Springs–Edgemont corridor are **on-site to single-digit miles** — well inside the economic threshold. Conveyance is NOT the binding constraint for groundwater; **source availability + permitability + water quality are.** (Depth/yield of Madison wells in the deep southern basin and pumping head/energy cost = GAP.)
- 🔍 **Angostura surface:** ~10 mi from Hot Springs → inside threshold geographically; the constraint is water-right conversion + treatment + drought reliability, not distance.
- 🔍 **WDRWS Missouri pipeline:** the Missouri intake is **~160+ mi** away at Oahe (north of Pierre); even the trunk to Rapid City is far north of Fall River. A Fall River spur off WDRWS would be **far beyond the 20–30 mi threshold** and dependent on a $2–4B, 20-yr project — **economically out of frame for this developer to firm independently.**
- 🔍 **Edgemont↔Hot Springs corridor:** ~40-ish road miles apart (Cheyenne River runs through both); any build site choice trades proximity to Angostura/Cheyenne (south, near Edgemont/Angostura) vs Madison municipal infrastructure (Hot Springs). **Exact build-site coordinates not set → conveyance number is site-dependent (GAP).**

## 4. DURABILITY / CURTAILMENT RISK

- 🔍 **Aquifer decline trajectory (NEGATIVE):** documented declining Madison levels; USGS 2025 says growth is outpacing recharge in the high-growth Black Hills; drought already forced early water restrictions at Pactola (Rapid City's reservoir) in spring 2025. Climate/drought risk is explicitly flagged in USGS storage analysis.
- 🔍 **Prior-appropriation seniority:** a brand-new city's permit would be **junior to every existing municipal, rural-water, and irrigation right** — i.e., first to be curtailed in a call/drought (cf. the 2008 Cheyenne junior-irrigator shutoff). Bad seniority = bad durability.
- 🔍 **Tribal / treaty claims (MAJOR cloud):** The **1868 Fort Laramie Treaty** established the Great Sioux Reservation including the Black Hills; ownership/treaty claims remain live. **Great Plains Tribal Water Alliance** (Oglala Sioux, Standing Rock, Rosebud, Cheyenne River [2023], Flandreau Santee) is "dedicated to the protection and preservation of Tribal water rights" and actively opposes Black Hills water projects (it has criticized the WDRWS pipeline as "economically infeasible"). **Unquantified Winters/reserved water rights could cloud any large new appropriation in the Cheyenne basin / Black Hills aquifers.**
- 🔍 **Organized opposition (battle-hardened):** Black Hills Clean Water Alliance + Oglala Sioux Tribe + GPTWA have fought Dewey-Burdock for 40 years with water/aquifer protection as the central playbook — and **won** the practical fight (Powertech still stalled). A 6–9 MGD new-city draw on the *same aquifers* would inherit this opposition machine. Even at entry scale, "new city drilling the Madison/Inyan Kara that locals fear" is a live political risk.

## WHERE DOES THE ANSWER FLIP? (entry vs build-out)

| Dimension | Entry (~0.5–0.8 MGD / 2–5k ppl) | Build-out (~6–9 MGD / 50k ppl) | Flip point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scale vs. county** | = 100% of county's current public supply (~0.8 MGD); ~1x–2x Hot Springs | ~10–12x entire county public draw; ~8–11x Hot Springs | Stops being "another small system" almost immediately above entry |
| **Source** | 1 small Madison/Inyan Kara/Minnelusa wellfield, treated | No identified firm source on local aquifers; would need Angostura conversion or WDRWS pipeline | ~1.5–3 MGD: a single permitted wellfield can't cover it |
| **Appropriation feasibility** | Plausible small new permit (subject to local headroom GAP) | Powertech-class draw (8,500 gpm ≈ 12 MGD was blocked) | Crosses into "regionally significant industrial appropriation" → WMB fight |
| **Conveyance** | On-site/single-digit miles — not binding | Local wells can't firm it; pipeline options >threshold ($2–4B, 20 yr) | When you must leave local groundwater |
| **Recharge ceiling** | Marginal slice of subarea recharge (maybe OK) | 12–18% of *entire Black Hills* Madison recharge | Hits the USGS "exceeds recharge" wall |
| **Durability/politics** | Quiet small system might fly under radar | Triggers BHCWA / OST / GPTWA opposition + treaty claims | The moment the draw is "uranium-scale" |
| **VERDICT** | **CONCERN (conditional)** — possible at low end IF local subarea headroom verified | **FAIL** — no firm source, recharge-capped, politically blocked, treaty-clouded | **Breaks ~1.5–3 MGD** |

**Bottom line:** the answer flips from *conceivable* to *not real* somewhere around **1.5–3 MGD** — i.e., **well below the principal's 6–9 MGD build-out**, and potentially as low as the *top* of the entry band (0.8 MGD) if local Madison/Minnelusa headroom turns out to be thin. **Phasing does not rescue build-out** — the terminal load has no home; you'd be betting the city on a $2–4B regional pipeline outside your control.
