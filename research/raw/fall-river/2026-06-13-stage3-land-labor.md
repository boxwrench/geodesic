# Fall River County, SD — Stage 3 (Validate): LAND ASSEMBLY + ABSORPTION (labor & housing)

- **Date:** 2026-06-13
- **Mode:** Mode-B DRAFT — **everything tagged 🔍 (unverified)**. Nothing here is ✅. Parcel-level contiguity, exact prices, and live ownership are HUMAN GAPs — do not promote to a decision without the closures at bottom.
- **Stage-3 question (joined):** (A) Can a ≥5,000-ac private land block be ASSEMBLED economically, avoiding grassland interspersion + tribal land? (B) Can the region ABSORB the build — construction labor + housing within commute? L16: the binding constraint MIGRATES to absorption at full scale even when entitlement is feasible.
- **Demand frame (placeholder spec — screen against these):**
  - **Entry:** ~2,000–5,000 people / ~1,000–2,000 DU / ~500 jobs / land footprint plausibly ~2,000–5,000 ac.
  - **Build-out:** ~50,000 people / ~20,000 DU / 15,000+ jobs / **15,000–50,000 contiguous acres**. ~7x the county.
- **Spec absorption gate:** ≥10,000 construction-relevant workers within 60 min.

---

## TL;DR — the Validate read

🔍 **LAND ASSEMBLY = PASS at entry / CONCERN (assemblable, not trivial) at build-out.** Unlike the water axis, land is the *good news* for Fall River. Near **Edgemont** there is at least one **single-owner ~10,323-ac contiguous open-range ranch** ("Open Range Ranch," Fall River Co., listing #94540) — a textbook single-seller asset case that **clears the ≥5,000-ac entry/early threshold in one transaction** and is large enough to seed a 15,000-ac build-out with 1–2 neighbor adds. SW SD ranch land is **cheap** (~$1,080/ac avg SW-district pasture, 2024; ~$1,500/ac non-irrig cropland). Land *cost* is a rounding error against water/transmission. The friction is (i) the grassland interspersion still forces a careful site pick that **stays off the checkerboarded USFS sections**, and (ii) full 50k build-out (15k–50k ac) likely needs a USFS land *exchange* to consolidate — a NEPA item, not a kill.

🔍 **ABSORPTION = CONCERN at entry / FAIL (import-only) at build-out.** This is where L16 bites, hard.
- **Fall River's entire civilian labor force is ~3,196** (Jan 2026), **~3,071 employed, ~125 unemployed at 3.9%.** The county cannot self-staff even the *entry* build; the entry city's ~500 permanent jobs alone is ~16% of the county's whole workforce.
- The **only real labor pool is Rapid City MSA**, and its **entire** mining/logging/construction sector is **~6,000–6,350 workers** — already ~2% unemployment, already in a documented multi-year labor + housing shortage, and **57 mi / ~62 min from Hot Springs** (Edgemont is *farther*, ~75–90 min). So the spec's "**≥10,000 construction workers within 60 min**" is **NOT met** — the realistic 60-min construction pool is roughly **6–8k and fully employed**, i.e. you'd be bidding crews away from Rapid City's own shortage, not drawing from slack.
- **Housing bootstrap is the binding wall:** county has **~4,101 housing units, ~3,226 occupied**, **~2% rental vacancy (Hot Springs ~0%)**. A regional workforce-housing crisis is so acute that the **Hot Springs hospital is spending ~$2.3M to enable just 48 units** (KOTA, Jun 2026). There is **nowhere for thousands of construction workers or early residents to live** before the city builds its own stock — classic chicken-and-egg, worse than Sweetwater because there's no nearby boomtown with slack rooms.

🔍 **NET:** Land says *yes*; absorption says *the project is import-only and self-housing from day one*, and at build-out it must essentially **import and house an entire construction economy** in one of the tightest small-market labor/housing regions in the state. **Where it flips: absorption.** Entry is a stretch-but-conceivable man-camp problem; build-out (50k / 20k DU / 15k+ jobs) is a regional-scale labor + housing build that the southern Black Hills cannot supply and can barely host.

## SECTION A — LAND ASSEMBLY

### A1. Is there an assemblable ≥5,000-ac private block off the grassland + off tribal land?

🔍 **YES — and a single-seller version exists near Edgemont.**
- **Open Range Ranch** (FARMFLIP/LANDFLIP #94540, Dakota Land Co., listing agent Graham Schuetzle): **"10,323.28± acres of contiguous open range grassland,"** Fall River County, near **Edgemont, SD 57735.** Described as one large operation split into an **East Ranch (~6,247 ac)** and **West Ranch (~4,076 ac)**, full perimeter ("boarder") fencing, 50+ stock tanks, plus a **1,637-AUM federal grazing permit** (i.e. it already *uses* adjacent grassland AUMs — relevant to the exchange/permit path below). **Listing now shows "Expired"** — so it is not necessarily on-market today, but it proves a contiguous single-ownership ~10k-ac block of *deeded* grassland exists in this county. 🔍 **This is the asset case:** one ranch ≈ 2x the 5,000-ac threshold, deeded/contiguous, near rail (Edgemont is a historic rail town) and the Cheyenne River.
- Other large single tracts seen: **Indian Canyon Ranch** ~1,450 ac (1,000 deeded + 450 BLM) ~30 min from Hot Springs near Angostura; **Cheyenne River Ranch** ~480 ac near Edgemont; a 480-ac recreational tract. These show the *fragmented* tail; the Open Range Ranch is the outlier large block.
- **Edgemont vs Hot Springs:** the assemblable large *private* block is **Edgemont-side (SW county)** — open prairie, big ranches, fewer interspersed USFS/tribal parcels than the Hot Springs/Angostura/Buffalo Gap corridor. **But Edgemont-side is the WORSE side for absorption** (farther from Rapid City, ~75–90 min) — so land and labor pull the siting decision in opposite directions. Hot Springs-side is closer to labor/services but more federally/checkerboarded interspersed (Buffalo Gap + Wind Cave/Angostura) → smaller clean private blocks.

### A2. Ownership pattern + single-seller (asset) case + grassland exchange path

🔍 **Pattern = a few large ranches over open range, with USFS Buffalo Gap sections interspersed** (L8 confirmed). The big-ranch structure is *favorable* for assembly (fewer counterparties than a row-crop county) — the Open Range Ranch shows you can get ~10k contiguous deeded acres from essentially **one seller.** That is the single biggest land-side positive in the whole Fall River screen.
- **Grassland exchange/permit path & NEPA cost:** USFS (Nebraska National Forest & Grasslands, R2) **routinely does Buffalo Gap land exchanges to consolidate the intermixed checkerboard** — documented examples: **Cain Creek** (TNC↔USFS, purpose "consolidate land ownership where private and NFS lands intermix"; analyzed via **Environmental Assessment**), **Blacktail Creek** (USFS↔White Ranch near Oelrichs; **Decision Memo / Categorical Exclusion**), **Sand Creek**, and a ~760-ac-for-800-ac Whitcher exchange. **Takeaway:** consolidating around interspersed grassland sections is a *known, doable* USFS process; NEPA burden ranges from **Categorical Exclusion (months)** to **EA (1–2+ yrs)** depending on scope/acreage — much lighter than the water-permit fight, and far below a full EIS *if* the deal is framed as ownership consolidation. 🔍 A 15k–50k-ac master-city footprint touching multiple grassland sections would likely push toward **EA, possibly EIS** at build-out scale, and exchanges of this magnitude are unprecedented locally → **HUMAN GAP on exchange feasibility at city scale.**
- **Mineral estate / split estate:** SW SD has uranium (Powertech/Dewey-Burdock) and oil/gas legacy — **mineral ownership may be severed** from surface on these ranches. Not checked. **HUMAN GAP** (title work).

### A3. Land-cost order of magnitude

🔍 **Cheap — the cheapest axis of the whole project.** SDSU Extension 2024 Farm Real Estate Survey (Davis & Le):
- **SW district pasture/rangeland avg ~$1,080/ac (2024)** — lowest in the state alongside NW (~$890/ac).
- **SW district non-irrigated cropland avg ~$1,513/ac (2024)** — also state-low.
- Listing-level comps run higher (recreational/improved tracts; a 402-ac Custer Co. ranch ≈ $5,162/ac), but **bulk open range trades far below that**, closer to the survey pasture number for a 10k-ac operation.
- **Order-of-magnitude land bill:**
  - Entry footprint ~5,000 ac × ~$1,000–1,500/ac ≈ **$5M–$7.5M.**
  - Build-out footprint 15,000 ac × ~$1,000–1,500/ac ≈ **$15M–$22M;** 50,000 ac ≈ **$50M–$75M.**
  - Even the high end (50k ac) is **small relative to the water and transmission capex** flagged in the sibling Stage-3 files. **Land is NOT the binding constraint.** A premium over ag value to assemble/optionize quietly (the "stealth" pull) still leaves land cheap.

## SECTION B — ABSORPTION CAPACITY (L16 — the decisive check)

### B1. Construction labor — can the region staff a multi-thousand-worker build?

🔍 **No, not locally. Import-only, and the import pool is itself tight.** SD DLR county labor force (Jan 2026, NSA):

| Area | Civilian labor force | Employed | Unemployed | Unemp rate | Drive to Hot Springs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fall River Co.** | **3,196** | 3,071 | 125 | **3.9%** | in-county |
| Custer Co. | 4,536 | 4,399 | 137 | 3.0% | ~39 min (Custer town) |
| Oglala Lakota Co. | 3,742 | 3,541 | 201 | 5.4% | adjacent S (Pine Ridge) |
| **Pennington Co. (Rapid City)** | **58,923** | 57,560 | 1,363 | **2.3%** | **~57 mi / ~62 min** |
| Meade Co. | 16,060 | 15,682 | 378 | 2.4% | >75 min |
| Lawrence Co. (Spearfish) | 15,055 | 14,748 | 307 | 2.0% | >75 min |

- 🔍 **Fall River's whole labor force (~3,196) is smaller than the construction crew a 50k city needs at peak.** The entry city's ~500 permanent jobs ≈ 16% of the county workforce; you cannot staff even entry from the local pool. Total unemployed in Fall River = **~125 people.**
- 🔍 **The only sizeable pool is Rapid City MSA (Custer+Meade+Pennington).** But the MSA's **entire mining/logging/construction sector is ~6,000–6,350 workers** (BLS/FRED RAPI646NRMN; ~6.0k Jan 2025, ~6.35k Dec 2025) at **~2% unemployment** — i.e. **fully employed.** There is **no idle construction army** to redeploy; you would be **bidding crews away from Rapid City's own building boom and its documented multi-year labor shortage.**
- 🔍 **Commute geometry kills the "within 60 min" test.** Hot Springs↔Rapid City is **~57 mi / ~62 min** — *over* the 60-min line, and the construction labor *lives in* Rapid City, so it's a 60-min outbound commute to a site that has nowhere for them to stay. **Edgemont-side (the good land) is ~75–90 min** from Rapid City — worse. Custer (~4,536 LF) and Oglala Lakota (~3,742 LF) are closer but small and also low-slack.
- 🔍 **Spec gate (≥10,000 construction-relevant workers within 60 min): NOT MET.** The honest 60-min construction-specific pool is ~**6–8k and already at work.** Even counting *all* employed persons (not just construction) within ~60 min, the realistic draw is Rapid City's ~57k employed — but only a sliver is construction-skilled and available, and pulling them stresses the region. **This is import-from-out-of-state-by-man-camp at build-out**, not a local-workforce build.
- 🔍 **Competing pull:** the **national construction labor crisis (2026)** — ~349k net new workers needed industry-wide, retirements + ICE enforcement tightening supply — plus SD's own Black Hills boom and statewide 2.5% unemployment, means crews are scarce *everywhere* and command premiums. No specific named regional megaproject was confirmed pulling these exact crews, but the **baseline is a fully-utilized, shortage-state market.** 🔍 GAP: scan for a specific competing megaproject (e.g., Ellsworth AFB B-21 buildout near Rapid City) that could be drawing the same trades — Ellsworth is a plausible large regional sink. **HUMAN GAP.**

### B2. Housing — the bootstrap problem (where do workers + early residents live?)

🔍 **This is the hardest wall on the absorption axis — arguably harder than labor.**
- 🔍 **Existing stock is tiny and full.** Fall River Co. ≈ **4,101 total housing units; ~3,226 occupied** (2020 census). **Rental vacancy ~2% county-wide; Hot Springs ~0%.** There is effectively **zero slack** to absorb an influx.
- 🔍 **The market is already in crisis at trivially small scale.** Fall River Health Services (Hot Springs hospital) is investing **~$2.3M in subdivision infrastructure to enable just ~48 affordable workforce units** (KOTA, Jun 9, 2026) — because it cannot recruit staff without housing. If 48 units is a multi-million-dollar regional achievement, **the city needs ~1,000–2,000 DU at entry and ~20,000 DU at build-out** — i.e. **the project would have to build essentially all of its own housing**, including for the very crews building it.
- 🔍 **No nearby boomtown overflow.** Unlike a shale play with man-camps and motels, the southern Black Hills tourist towns (Hot Springs, Custer) run **near-zero off-season slack and are seasonally booked**; Rapid City has its own housing shortage (SDPB 2021; statewide $200M Housing Infrastructure Financing Program targeting the Black Hills). **Early construction workforce = purpose-built man-camp on-site from day one; early residents = the first phase of the city itself.** The bootstrap is fully internalized — a cost and schedule item, not something the region can absorb.
- 🔍 **Rapid City commute as a housing substitute = poor.** Workers *could* sleep in Rapid City and commute, but at **~62–90 min each way** that's a 2–3 hr/day round trip into a town that is itself short on housing — not a durable plan for a multi-year, multi-thousand-worker build.

### B3. Compare to spec

| Spec absorption requirement | Reality (🔍) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| ≥10,000 construction-relevant workers within 60 min | ~6–8k construction workers in Rapid City MSA, fully employed; Hot Springs is 62 min out, Edgemont 75–90 min | 🔍 **FAIL — not met; import-only** |
| Local labor force can staff entry build | Fall River LF ~3,196, ~125 unemployed; entry needs hundreds | 🔍 **FAIL locally / CONCERN (import)** |

## WHERE DOES THE ANSWER FLIP? (entry vs build-out)

| Axis | ENTRY (~2–5k ppl / ~1–2k DU / ~500 jobs / ~2–5k ac) | BUILD-OUT (~50k ppl / ~20k DU / 15k+ jobs / 15k–50k ac) | Flip point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Land — block availability** | 🔍 PASS — one ~10k-ac single-seller ranch (Open Range, Edgemont) covers it | 🔍 CONCERN — needs that ranch + neighbor adds + likely USFS exchange to reach 15k–50k contiguous | Build-out needs assembly beyond one ranch + grassland consolidation |
| **Land — cost** | 🔍 PASS — ~$5–7.5M | 🔍 PASS — ~$15–75M; trivial vs water/transmission | Never the binding axis |
| **Land — federal/NEPA** | 🔍 PASS (stay off USFS sections) | 🔍 CONCERN — exchange/consolidation → EA, possibly EIS | When footprint crosses interspersed grassland sections |
| **Construction labor** | 🔍 CONCERN — import crews + man-camp; Rapid City pool exists but tight | 🔍 **FAIL** — must import & house a whole construction economy; >10k-worker gate unmet | Flips as peak crew exceeds Rapid City's ~6–8k fully-employed construction sector |
| **Housing bootstrap** | 🔍 CONCERN — self-build man-camp; ~0–2% local vacancy | 🔍 **FAIL** — must build ~20k DU with no regional overflow; crisis at 48 units | Flips almost immediately above local slack (~hundreds of units) |
| **Overall absorption** | 🔍 CONCERN (stretch, internalized cost) | 🔍 **FAIL** | **Build-out scale** |

🔍 **The binding constraint migrates to ABSORPTION (housing first, then labor) at full scale — confirming L16.** Land is feasible; the region cannot supply or host the labor/housing a 50k city requires.
