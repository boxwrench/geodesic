# Fall River County, SD — Stage 3 (Validate): Transmission & Core Infrastructure

**Date:** 2026-06-13
**Mode:** Mode-B DRAFT — *every claim tagged 🔍, nothing fabricated. Gated/GIS data flagged as HUMAN GAP.*
**Stage-3 question:** Is extending high-voltage transmission (≥115 kV) and core infrastructure (power, gas, road/rail, broadband) to a new master-planned city in Fall River County (seat: Hot Springs; also Edgemont) physically AND economically real — at entry (~2,000–5,000 people / ~5–10 MW) and at full build-out (~50,000 / ~100–200 MW, possibly more if heavy industrial)?

> Spec ranges (entry ~5–10 MW peak; build-out ~100–200 MW; substation-proximity threshold ~10–15 mi) are **placeholders carried from the Sweetwater pilot** — used here only to screen where the answer flips. Treat thresholds as screening tools, not requirements.

---

## TL;DR

- 🔍 **Transmission is BETTER than Stage-1/Stage-2 feared.** Hot Springs has a **230 kV substation** that is an explicit node on Black Hills Energy's (BHE) 230 kV backbone — the same backbone listing Spearfish, Lead, Rapid City (SD) and Gillette (WY). The system is fed by the **Wyodak–Hot Springs 230 kV line** and the **Teckla–Osage–Rapid City 230 kV** corridor. So a ≥115 kV node sits **at/near Hot Springs itself**, not 50+ miles away. ([BHE 10-yr plan via PUC](https://puc.sd.gov/commission/commissionaction/10yearplan/BHE2024.pdf); [USFS Teckla-Osage-Rapid City](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2011/08/26/2011-21712/black-hills-national-forest-sd-thunder-basin-national-grassland-wy-teckla-osage-rapid-city))
- 🔍 **Entry load (~5–10 MW): PASS.** This is ordinary distribution-scale load for BHE's existing Hot Springs 230/69 kV infrastructure. No transmission build needed.
- 🔍 **Build-out (~100–200 MW): CONCERN, not fail.** BHE is *actively courting* large loads — 500 MW of data-center demand in its 5-yr plan, a **>3 GW pipeline**, a dedicated "BHE Data Centers" business unit, the new **99 MW Lange II** plant (2026), the \$350M **Ready Wyoming** project now tying its WY and SD grids together (energized Dec 29, 2025), and a pending **NorthWestern merger explicitly keyed to data-center / large-load growth**. The utility *wants* this load. The open question is whether the **local 69 kV sub-transmission around Hot Springs/Edgemont** (vs. the 230 kV backbone) can absorb 100–200 MW without a dedicated new 230 kV tap + substation — a **cost/queue item, likely customer-funded.**
- 🔍 **BYO-generation likely, like Sweetwater.** BHE's large-load tariff model (built with Microsoft) already contemplates **customer-owned, utility-dispatched on-site generation** for backup/reliability. Expect the same posture at 100–200 MW here. SD PUC siting permit triggers at **50 MW** of generation — relevant if the city builds its own plant.
- 🔍 **Rail = strong but DECLINING fast.** Edgemont is a historic **BNSF crew-change** on the Powder River coal corridor (Gillette–Alliance), but BNSF is **closing/bypassing the Edgemont yard** (2025) as coal declines. An active main line still runs through Edgemont; the *yard/crew base* is going away. Spur access for industry = HUMAN GAP.
- 🔍 **Highways:** US-18 + US-385 cross at Hot Springs; US-18 + SD-471 at Edgemont. Adequate two-lane federal highway access; no Interstate. ✅ at any scale for trucking.
- 🔍 **Gas:** Black Hills Energy is the regional gas + electric utility; gas service to Hot Springs is **probable but UNCONFIRMED at scale** (capacity for 100–200 MW industrial = HUMAN GAP).
- 🔍 **Broadband:** **Golden West Telecommunications fiber-to-the-home** covers ~90% of Hot Springs (1 Gbps). ✅ at entry; carrier-grade/long-haul capacity for a 50k city = upgrade item, not a blocker.

**VERDICT:** Entry **PASS** · Build-out **CONCERN** (transmission upgrade + likely BYO-gen are cost/queue items, not physical kills; rail-yard loss and unconfirmed gas/transmission headroom are the real diligence gaps).

## 1. Transmission — what exists ≥115 kV near Hot Springs/Edgemont

🔍 **A 230 kV substation exists at Hot Springs.** BHE's transmission system description (carried in its SD PUC 10-Year Energy Facility Plan filings) states the 69 kV transmission system "is supported by the 230 kV transmission system with substations in **Spearfish, Lead, Rapid City, Hot Springs**, South Dakota, and **Gillette, Wyoming**." So Hot Springs is a named 230 kV node on the backbone — **distance to nearest adequate ≥115 kV substation is effectively ~0 (in-town), well inside the ~10–15 mi screening threshold.**
Source: [BHE 2024 10-yr plan (PUC)](https://puc.sd.gov/commission/commissionaction/10yearplan/BHE2024.pdf)

🔍 **Feeding lines / corridors:**
- **Wyodak–Hot Springs 230 kV** line (named in a USFS special-use authorization for existing BHE 230 kV / 69 kV powerlines). (verify exact endpoints — HUMAN GAP)
- **Teckla–Osage–Rapid City 230 kV** project (~150 mi, WY→Rapid City) strengthened the regional backbone serving the southern Black Hills. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2011/08/26/2011-21712/black-hills-national-forest-sd-thunder-basin-national-grassland-wy-teckla-osage-rapid-city))
- **Ready Wyoming** (\$350M; 3× 230 kV lines, 2× 115 kV lines, multiple substations) — **fully energized Dec 29, 2025** — now **interconnects BHE's Wyoming and South Dakota electric systems**, materially increasing import/transfer capability into the southern Black Hills and reducing reliance on third-party transmission. ([Cowboy State Daily, Jan 2026](https://cowboystatedaily.com/2026/01/09/ready-wyoming-transmission-project-links-wyoming-and-south-dakota-power-grids/); [BHE IR](https://ir.blackhillscorp.com/news-releases/news-release-details/black-hills-corp-energizes-first-phase-its-ready-wyoming))

🔍 **Ownership:** Local HV is **Black Hills Energy (Black Hills Corp.)**, an investor-owned utility headquartered in Rapid City — the dominant electric + gas utility for the southern Black Hills. WAPA operates regional federal transmission elsewhere in SD but was **not** identified as the Hot Springs feed. Rural co-ops (e.g., Black Hills Electric Cooperative; Rushmore Electric as G&T) serve surrounding rural territory but the town nodes are BHE.
Sources: [BHE corporate](https://www.deadwood.com/business/utility-companies/black-hills-energy/); [Black Hills Electric Coop](https://www.bhec.coop/)

🔍 **Import headroom / constraint:** Cannot be quantified from public sources. The **230 kV backbone presence + Ready Wyoming interconnect** strongly suggest the *backbone* is not the binding constraint. The binding constraint for a concentrated 100–200 MW load is more likely **local step-down (230→69 kV) capacity and the sub-transmission around Hot Springs/Edgemont**, plus SPP/regional interconnection-queue position. **Actual headroom = HUMAN GAP** (requires BHE transmission planning / SPP study; not public).

## 2. Generation / load — can the grid serve ~100–200 MW at build-out?

🔍 **The utility is explicitly chasing exactly this kind of load.** Black Hills Corp.'s 5-yr plan books **500 MW of data-center demand by 2029** (Microsoft expansion + Meta AI data center) and discloses a **large-load pipeline >3 GW** (up from >1 GW). It runs a dedicated **"BHE Data Centers"** marketing unit pitching the Black Hills (Rapid City) on reliability, climate, and SD's no-income-tax business climate. A **100–200 MW** city load is well within the scale BHE is publicly soliciting.
Sources: [BHE Q3 2025 results](https://ir.blackhillscorp.com/news-releases/news-release-details/black-hills-corp-reports-2025-third-quarter-results-and); [BHE Data Centers SD](https://datacenters.blackhillsenergy.com/south-dakota); [Utility Dive on merger](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/black-hills-northwestern-agree-to-merge-with-eye-on-serving-data-centers/758238/)

🔍 **New generation in the pipeline:** **Lange II, 99 MW** (Rapid City area, online 2026) replaces aging capacity. The pending **Black Hills–NorthWestern merger** is explicitly framed around financial strength for grid expansion to serve data centers / large loads. So generation supply is growing, not shrinking.
Sources: [naturalgasintel on merger](https://naturalgasintel.com/news/black-hills-northwestern-merger-keyed-to-western-power-growth-as-data-centers-large-loads-proliferate/); [KBHB](https://kbhbradio.com/opponents-say-black-hills-energy-and-northwestern-energy-merger-is-all-about-data-centers/)

🔍 **SD PUC regulatory threshold (siting):** SD law lowered the energy-conversion / wind / solar facility permit threshold from **100 MW to 50 MW**. This applies to **generation facilities** (and 50 MW+ capacity increases), NOT to *load* per se. Implication: if the new city builds its **own generation** (e.g., a 100–200 MW gas plant or solar to self-supply / BYO-gen), that plant **trips a SD PUC Energy Conversion Facility Permit at ≥50 MW.** No distinct "large electric *load*" PUC threshold was found.
Sources: [SD PUC solar FAQ](https://puc.sd.gov/Publications/solarfaq.aspx); [SD facility permit dockets](https://puc.sd.gov/commission/dockets/electric/2024/EL24-015/Application.pdf)

🔍 **"Bring-your-own-generation" cliff (the Sweetwater parallel):** BHE's flagship large-load tariff (the WY **Large Power Contract Service**, built with Microsoft) is structured around **customer-owned, utility-dispatched on-site generation** for backup/reliability plus access to market power. This is the same architecture as Sweetwater's ~200 MW BYO-gen expectation. **Expectation:** at 100–200 MW in Fall River County, BHE would likely require some combination of (a) customer-funded transmission/substation (contribution in aid of construction), and (b) on-site backup generation. **Exact SD tariff terms = HUMAN GAP** (need BHE SD tariff filings at the PUC).
Source: [BHE Data Centers — energy solutions](https://datacenters.blackhillsenergy.com/resources/energy-solutions-data-centers)

## 3. Other infrastructure

### 3a. Natural gas
🔍 **Black Hills Energy is the regional combined electric + gas utility** (1.27M gas + electric customers across 8 states, HQ Rapid City). Gas distribution service to Hot Springs is **highly probable** but the search did not return a town-level confirmation or a delivery-capacity figure. **Capacity to serve a 100–200 MW industrial / on-site-generation gas demand = HUMAN GAP** (need BHE gas system maps + a transmission-pipeline check; whether a high-pressure interstate lateral reaches Fall River County is unconfirmed).
Source: [BHE corporate](https://www.deadwood.com/business/utility-companies/black-hills-energy/)

### 3b. Highways
🔍 **Hot Springs:** junction of **US-18** and **US-385**. **Edgemont:** junction of **US-18** and **SD-471**. SD-79 connects Hot Springs north toward Rapid City. Federal two-lane highway access is adequate for construction and trucking at any scale; **no Interstate** in the county (nearest is I-90 at Rapid City, ~60 mi north). ✅
Sources: [US-18 SD (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_18_in_South_Dakota); [Edgemont (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgemont,_South_Dakota); [Hot Springs (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_South_Dakota)

### 3c. Freight rail
🔍 **Active main line through Edgemont — but the yard/crew base is being eliminated.** Edgemont is a historic **BNSF crew-change point** on the high-tonnage Gillette (WY)–Alliance (NE) Powder River coal division (~50 pool crews in early 2023; the corridor historically moved 100M+ tons/yr). **However:** as of 2025, BNSF is **closing/bypassing the Edgemont yard**, proposing to run coal trains Alliance↔Guernsey **without stopping in Edgemont**, citing coal's structural decline; ~100 local jobs at risk. The **rails remain** (line still operational, traffic detours through Hot Springs during maintenance) but Edgemont's value as a **staffed rail hub is declining**.
Sequencing risk: a master-planned city counting on rail should NOT assume the crew base survives. An **industrial spur** would be a new build — feasibility = HUMAN GAP.
Sources: [Closure likely (FRC Herald-Star)](https://www.frcheraldstar.com/news/closure-edgemont-bnsf-rail-yard-likely); [Big changes coming to Edgemont (KOTA, Apr 2025)](https://www.kotatv.com/2025/04/29/big-changes-could-be-coming-edgemont/); [Save-BNSF committee (FRC Herald-Star)](https://www.frcheraldstar.com/news/optimism-energizes-new-committee-keep-bnsf-railroad-edgemont)

### 3d. Broadband / fiber
🔍 **Golden West Telecommunications fiber-to-the-home** serves ~90.3% of Hot Springs homes at up to **1 Gbps symmetrical**; fiber broadly ~68% available in town; T-Mobile 5G Home as alternative. ✅ at entry. For a 50k city / data-center scale, **carrier-grade long-haul / redundant fiber routes** would need verification (single-carrier dependency, route diversity) — upgrade item, not a blocker.
Sources: [Golden West Hot Springs FTTH](https://www.goldenwest.com/our-network/fiber-to-the-home/hot-springs); [BroadbandNow Hot Springs](https://broadbandnow.com/South-Dakota/Hot-Springs)

## WHERE DOES THE ANSWER FLIP?

| Dimension | Entry (~2k–5k ppl / ~5–10 MW) | Build-out (~50k / ~100–200 MW) | Flips? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Electric transmission (≥115 kV)** | ✅ 230 kV substation already in Hot Springs; pure distribution-scale add | ⚠️ Backbone present, but a concentrated 100–200 MW likely needs a dedicated 230→69 kV tap + new substation; SPP queue position matters | **Yes — cost/queue, not physical** |
| **Generation supply** | ✅ Trivial for BHE | ⚠️ Within BHE's stated large-load appetite (3 GW pipeline) but needs PPA/queue; possible BYO-gen requirement | **Yes — commercial** |

| **Broadband** | ✅ Golden West 1 Gbps FTTH | ⚠️ Need redundant long-haul / carrier-grade | **Yes — upgrade item** |

**Bottom line on flip:** The hard *physical* picture is far more favorable than Stage-1 implied — a 230 kV node sits **in Hot Springs**, and BHE's WY–SD grids are now interconnected (Ready Wyoming). The flip between entry and build-out is **economic/commercial** (customer-funded substation + transmission tap, likely BYO-gen, SPP queue) plus **two genuine diligence gaps**: (1) confirmed gas capacity at industrial scale, (2) rail's declining future. None reads as a *kill* at this stage.
