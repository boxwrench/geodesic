# Jones County, NC — Stage 3 (Validate): WATER ECONOMICS

**Date:** 2026-06-13
**Mode:** B (DRAFT — everything tagged 🔍, nothing fabricated; gated/permit-portal items flagged HUMAN GAP)
**Stage-3 question:** Is extending WATER to a master-planned new city in Jones County, NC physically AND economically real, under the binding Stage-1 constraint (Central Coastal Plain Capacity Use Area / CCPCUA groundwater cap)?
**Demand frame (placeholder spec, do not re-derive):**
- Entry: ~2,000–5,000 people ≈ **0.5–0.8 MGD**
- Build-out: ~50,000 people ≈ **6–9 MGD** ≈ 7,000–10,000 ac-ft/yr
- County context: pop ~9,172; build-out ≈ 5.5× the entire county. ~75% wetlands-dominated (Croatan NF, Great Dover Swamp, pocosins). Riparian/regulated-use state (NOT prior-appropriation).

---

## TL;DR

🔍 **Entry (0.5–0.8 MGD): CONCERN (leaning pass-with-work).** A 0.5–0.8 MGD load roughly equals the *entire current Jones County Regional Water System* (2024 avg demand 0.70 MGD, total available supply 1.17 MGD, WTP permitted only 0.90 MGD). It cannot be served off existing slack — but it is the right size to be met by either (a) a wholesale contract with the **Neuse Regional Water & Sewer Authority (NRWASA)** surface-water plant in Kinston (~24 mi; permitted 30 MGD Neuse withdrawal, ~15 MGD regional headroom), or (b) a new CCPCUA groundwater permit for a few hundred thousand gpd from the shallower Castle Hayne (subject to reduction codes). Both are plausible at entry scale.

🔍 **Build-out (6–9 MGD): FAIL on groundwater; CONCERN→FAIL on the obvious wholesale path; the ONLY credible path is surface water (NRWASA / a new Neuse intake).** A 6–9 MGD groundwater withdrawal in the CCPCUA is effectively non-grantable — this is precisely the magnitude of withdrawal the CCPCUA exists to *eliminate*. The nearest large municipal systems that could wholesale (New Bern, ONWASA/Jacksonville) are **themselves 100% groundwater and themselves capped/reduced under the CCPCUA**, with no durable surplus at the 6–9 MGD scale. The realistic source at build-out is **Neuse River surface water**, delivered via NRWASA wholesale or a purpose-built intake/WTP — a multi-hundred-million-dollar regional project, 3–5+ yr permitting, and the dominant driver of whether the city pencils.

**The answer FLIPS between entry and build-out on SOURCE TYPE:** entry can ride groundwater or a modest wholesale tap; build-out must be surface water at regional-project scale. The CCPCUA does not merely tax the deep aquifers — it removes them as a build-out option.

## 1. SOURCE AT SCALE UNDER THE CCPCUA CAP

### 1a. What the incumbent actually uses (confirms the constraint is live)
🔍 Jones County Regional Water System (PWSID 04-52-020), 2024 LWSP:
- **100% groundwater, 0% surface water.** Nine wells, depths 254–576 ft, screened in Castle Hayne (shallower wells, e.g. #5B/7/8B/9/10 at ~250–320 ft) and deeper Cretaceous (Black Creek / Upper Cape Fear; wells #1–4 at 481–576 ft).
- **CCPCUA reduction codes are stamped on every well:** the deeper wells (#1, #2, #3, #4) carry **CUA25** (25% reduction); the shallower Castle Hayne wells (#5B, #7, #8B, #9, #10) carry **CUA0** (no reduction). This is direct evidence the deep Cretaceous aquifers are the throttled resource and the **Castle Hayne is the relatively unconstrained local source.**
- WTP "WT1" permitted capacity **0.90 MGD** (sourced from Wells 9 & 10, Castle Hayne).
- Total available supply **1.1676 MGD**; 2024 avg demand 0.70 MGD, max-month avg 0.86 MGD; demand = 66% of supply.
- Long-term per-capita demand **42 gpcd** (very low — rural/residential).
- System note: *"Jones county is in the planning phase of increasing our water treatment plant capacity."* and *"participated and approved an Alternate Water Supply Evaluation Plan in 2012–2013"* studying the **Castle Hayne Aquifer**.
- Source: https://www.ncwater.org/wudc/app/lwsp/report.php?pwsid=04-52-020&year=2024

**Implication:** entry load (0.5–0.8 MGD) ≈ a second Jones County system. Build-out (6–9 MGD) ≈ **6–8× the entire county's WTP capacity.**

### 1b. Capped deep aquifers (Black Creek / Upper Cape Fear) — NOT a build-out source
🔍 The CCPCUA exists because Cretaceous-age aquifer levels fell up to **200 ft over four decades**, with saltwater intrusion / cone-of-depression decline. Rules (effective Aug 1, 2002) require permits for any withdrawal >100,000 gpd and mandated phased reductions of **up to 75%** (dewatering zone) / 30% over 2002/2003–2018. New large Cretaceous withdrawals are the *opposite* of the program's goal. **A 6–9 MGD deep-aquifer permit is not realistically grantable.** Even a fraction is unlikely. Sources: https://www.deq.nc.gov/about/divisions/water-resources/groundwater-resources/central-coastal-plain-capacity-use-area-ccpcua-water-withdrawal-permit ; https://projects.ncsu.edu/nrli/decision-making/archive/CCPCUA.php

### 1c. Surficial / water-table aquifer — small, not a primary build-out source
🔍 No evidence any large public system in the area relies on the surficial aquifer; it is thin, vulnerable to drought/contamination, and not used at MGD municipal scale here. Possible minor supplement at entry; **not a 6–9 MGD source.** (HUMAN GAP: no quantified surficial yield data pulled.)

### 1d. Surface water (Trent / Neuse) — the only physical path to build-out scale
🔍 The Trent River runs through Trenton/Pollocksville; the Neuse is the major basin. Tellingly, **no large local utility currently draws surface water** — Jones County, New Bern, and ONWASA are *all* groundwater. The one regional success is NRWASA (below). A new Trent or Neuse intake + WTP is physically possible (the water exists) but is a greenfield regional-scale project. Low-flow/saltwater-wedge limits on the lower Trent near tidal reaches would push an intake upstream (toward Neuse mainstem). HUMAN GAP: Trent low-flow / 7Q10 and salt-wedge intrusion limit at candidate intake sites — needs NC DEQ DWR surface-water modeling (gated).

## 2. CCPCUA MECHANICS

🔍 (Confirmed against DEQ + NCSU NRLI + status report.)
- **Authority/trigger:** EMC-designated 15-county area (Beaufort, Carteret, Craven, Duplin, Edgecombe, Greene, **Jones**, Lenoir, Martin, Onslow, Pamlico, Pitt, Washington, Wayne, Wilson). Jones is **confirmed in.** Rules effective **Aug 1, 2002.**
- **Permit threshold:** any groundwater withdrawal **>100,000 gpd** from the regulated Cretaceous aquifer systems needs a CCPCUA permit. Registration/reporting required for any surface or groundwater user >10,000 gpd.
- **Phased reductions:** three rounds 2002/2003→2018; up to **75% reduction** in the "dewatering zone," **30%** elsewhere, for Cretaceous-aquifer withdrawals. Codes appear per-well in LWSPs (CUA0 / CUA25 / CUA50 seen in New Bern note).
- **Are new large withdrawals grantable?** The program's purpose is to *reduce* Cretaceous withdrawals to reverse a 200-ft decline and saltwater intrusion. A *new* multi-MGD Cretaceous permit cuts directly against that mandate. New Bern's experience is instructive: it gets only a **temporary 5-yr, 50%-reduced (2.12 MGD) permit** on its deep wells, re-evaluated every 5 yr and revocable. **A 6–9 MGD new deep-aquifer permit should be treated as effectively non-grantable** pending DEQ confirmation. The Castle Hayne (shallower) is less constrained (CUA0 locally) but is not a 6–9 MGD municipal source on its own here. ### IBT (Inter-Basin Transfer) regime
🔍
- IBT certificate (G.S. 143-215.22G/L) required for a **new transfer of ≥2 MGD** of *surface water* from one major river basin to another. Full process **3–5 years**, multiple public-comment rounds, EMC decision.
- **Basin geography is favorable here:** statute lists Neuse (10-1), Contentnea (10-2), and Trent (10-3) as sub-basins of the broader **Neuse major basin**. The state's own materials note transfers *between sub-basins but within the same major basin* generally are **not** counted as regulated IBTs ("Two of the nine IBT certificates transfer water between subbasins, but the water remains within the respective major river basins"). So **Neuse-mainstem water (NRWASA) delivered to a Trent-sub-basin build site likely does NOT trigger an IBT certificate** — both are within the Neuse major basin.
- **Caveat / HUMAN GAP:** the precise major-basin vs. sub-basin boundary at candidate build sites, and whether any portion of the city's wastewater return discharges to a *different* major basin (Cape Fear is the nearest neighbor to the southwest), must be confirmed — an IBT trigger flips the schedule by 3–5 yr. Sources: https://www.ncwater.org/Permits_and_Registration/Interbasin_Transfer/IBTbasics2.pdf ; https://www.ncleg.net/enactedlegislation/statutes/html/bysection/chapter_143/gs_143-215.22g.html ; https://www.deq.nc.gov/about/divisions/water-resources/water-planning/water-supply-planning/interbasin-transfer-certification/frequently-asked-questions-faq

## 3. CONVEYANCE ECONOMICS (vs. ~20–30 mi threshold)

🔍 Trenton is "strategically located between New Bern, Kinston and Jacksonville":
- **NRWASA / Kinston ≈ 24 mi** (NC-41 corridor) — within the 20–30 mi screening threshold, at its **far edge.**
- **New Bern ≈ 24 mi.**
- **ONWASA / Jacksonville ≈ 20 mi.**
- (Reference: NC Global TransPark ~22 mi.)
Sources: https://www.distance-between.com/from/us/north-carolina/trenton/to/us/north-carolina/kinston ; https://www.aroundtheworld360.com/distance/trenton_nc_us/new-bern_nc_us/ ; https://jonescountync.gov/about-jones-county/quality-of-life/

**Economics screen:**
- A ~24 mi large-diameter raw/finished-water transmission main to carry 6–9 MGD is order **$2–5M/mi** all-in (rough industry rule-of-thumb for large mains incl. crossings; HUMAN GAP — not a quoted figure) ⇒ ~**$50–120M+** just for the trunk, before pumping stations, storage, and any WTP capacity purchase/expansion. At ~24 mi this is at the **outer edge of "economically real"** for a *new* city that must also fund the source.
- Terrain penalty: Jones County is ~75% wetlands (Croatan NF, Great Dover Swamp, pocosins). A pipeline corridor will face **wetland permitting (Section 404 / NC 401), stream crossings, and longer/curved routing** — raising cost and schedule above flat-greenfield assumptions. This is a material adder specific to this county.
- At **entry scale (0.5–0.8 MGD)**, the same ~24 mi main is far smaller-diameter and cheaper, but the per-MGD conveyance cost is high — entry is more naturally served by a **local Castle Hayne wellfield + small WTP** (mirroring the existing county system) than by a 24-mi import, *unless* the build site is pre-committed to the surface-water path for build-out continuity.

## 4. DURABILITY

🔍
- **Saltwater-intrusion trajectory:** The CCPCUA is, by most accounts, *working* — groundwater levels began recovering after the **NRWASA surface-water plant came online (Fall 2008)** shifted demand off the aquifers. That recovery is the explicit rationale for *keeping* the Cretaceous cap tight; it argues against DEQ loosening caps to admit a new 6–9 MGD groundwater user. So durability of the *groundwater* option is poor by design.
- **Reliability of a wholesale contract:** NRWASA (surface water, 30 MGD permitted) is the durable supply; a long-term take-or-pay wholesale contract is the right instrument. **Concentration risk:** the city would depend on a single authority it doesn't control, ~24 mi away, for its entire build-out supply. New Bern's surplus is *not* durable (permit-contingent, demand reaches 97% of supply by 2070). ONWASA is co-committed to Camp Lejeune + Jacksonville.
- **Sea-level / coastal vulnerability:** Coastal Craven/Jones is low-elevation (New Bern averages 10–20 ft; ~half-foot SLR by 2040, several feet by 2100 per NC 2020 Climate Science Report). Risks: (a) **salt-wedge migration up the Neuse/Trent** threatening any future *low-estuary* surface intake (favors an *upstream* Neuse/NRWASA intake, which is better situated); (b) storm-surge/flood exposure of treatment and pump infrastructure; (c) widespread wetlands = high flood-storage value but also constrains where you can build and pipe. Sources: https://www.deq.nc.gov/about/divisions/coastal-management/coastal-adaptation-and-resiliency/data-support-coastal-adaptation-resiliency ; https://www.northcarolinahealthnews.org/2026/01/22/when-wetlands-disappear-flood-risks-rise/ ; CCPCUA status report https://www.ncwater.org/Reports_and_Publications/GWMS_Reports/CCPCUAStatusRep2019final.pdf (cited; not fully fetched — HUMAN GAP).

## WHERE DOES THE ANSWER FLIP?

| Dimension | Entry (~0.5–0.8 MGD / 2–5k ppl) | Build-out (~6–9 MGD / ~50k ppl) | Flip driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Viable source type** | Local **Castle Hayne** wellfield+WTP (CUA0 locally) OR modest **NRWASA wholesale** | **Surface water only** (NRWASA wholesale or new Neuse intake/WTP) | CCPCUA removes deep aquifers at scale; Castle Hayne can't carry 6–9 MGD alone |
| **CCPCUA groundwater permit** | Possibly grantable for a few hundred k gpd (Castle Hayne) | **Effectively non-grantable** (counter to program purpose; 200-ft decline reversal) | Magnitude vs. program intent |
| **Wholesale from New Bern/ONWASA** | Plausible (today's slack) | **Fails** — both 100% groundwater, capped; surpluses not durable | Their own CCPCUA caps + own growth |
| **Conveyance** | Cheaper small main; or local wells (no import) | ~24 mi large main + pumping + wetlands routing ⇒ ~$50–120M+ | Pipe size scales with demand; wetlands adder |
| **IBT** | <2 MGD; almost certainly no IBT | Likely still no IBT (Neuse→Trent = same major basin) **IF** intake & return stay in Neuse basin | Basin geography, not volume — confirm boundary/return |
| **Durability** | Low groundwater dependence acceptable short-term | Must be surface-water-anchored; SLR favors upstream intake | Aquifer recovery policy + sea-level rise |
| **Screen verdict** | 🔍 **CONCERN (pass-with-work)** | 🔍 **CONCERN→FAIL** — feasible ONLY as a regional surface-water project; not on groundwater, not on neighbors' surplus | Source type forced from GW to SW |
