# Jones County, NC — Stage 3 (Validate): Land Assembly + §404 Wetlands at Scale + Absorption

**Date:** 2026-06-13
**Mode:** B (DRAFT — everything tagged 🔍; do not treat as verified fact)
**Stage-3 question:** Can a master-planned new city (entry ~2-5k people / ~1-2k DU / ~500 jobs; build-out ~50k / ~20k DU / 15k+ jobs on 15,000-50,000 contiguous acres) be built in Jones County, NC, given (A) land assembly, (B) the §404 wetlands permitting reality — the dominant Stage-1 constraint, and (C) absorption capacity (construction labor + housing within commute)? Lesson L16: binding constraint migrates to absorption at full scale.

---

## TL;DR (🔍 all)

1. **Land assembly — CONCERN→FAIL at scale.** Large contiguous tracts EXIST in Jones (historic plantation-scale + timber holdings), but the two biggest contiguous blocks are off-market or wet: **Croatan NF** (federal, ~75% wetland) and **Hofmann Forest** (~79,000 ac, state/foundation-owned, now locked under a $78M / 50-year timber deed + joint conservation ownership — effectively NOT acquirable). A ≥5,000-ac block of **dry upland private** land is the hard intersection; entry-scale (≤2,000 ac upland) is plausibly assemblable, build-out (15k-50k ac upland) is the binding problem. 🔍 parcel-level = HUMAN GAP.
2. **§404 — the answer MOVED post-Sackett.** *Sackett v. EPA* (2023) cut federal CWA jurisdiction to wetlands with a **continuous surface connection** to navigable waters. Many of Jones's **isolated pocosins** likely now fall OUT of federal §404. Critically, **NC SB 582 (2023 Farm Act) barred the state from backfilling** — NC will NOT regulate isolated wetlands that lost federal protection. So the §404 wall is materially LOWER than Stage-1 assumed — BUT wetlands adjacent to the Trent River / Island Creek / mapped tributaries remain jurisdictional, and a city-scale fill near those features still triggers an individual permit + likely NEPA. Net: §404 shifts from probable-KILL to **CONCERN (site-dependent)**.
3. **Absorption — CONCERN at entry, FAIL-risk at build-out.** Adjacent within ~60 min: Onslow ~72k, Craven ~40k, Lenoir ~24k labor force (+ Jones ~4k) ≈ **~140k total**. At ~6.5% construction share ≈ **~9,000 construction workers in-region, nearly all already employed** in a documented eastern-NC labor shortage. Entry phase (~1-2k DU over years) is absorbable with import. Build-out (~20k DU) demands sustained construction headcount that the local market cannot supply without large-scale import + worker housing bootstrap.

**Verdict:** Assembly = CONCERN (entry) / FAIL-leaning (build-out). §404 = CONCERN (downgraded from KILL by Sackett+SB582, site-dependent). Absorption = CONCERN (entry) / FAIL-risk (build-out).

## SECTION A — LAND ASSEMBLY

### A1. Contiguous ≥5,000-ac dry-upland private block?
- Jones is >95% undeveloped, 49% ag; the landscape DOES contain large contiguous ownerships (historic plantation-scale tracts + industrial timberland). The problem is the **four-way intersection: large + contiguous + dry upland + private + for-sale.** 🔍
- The two largest contiguous blocks are NOT private/dry:
  - **Croatan National Forest** — federal, ~167,687 ac across Craven/Carteret/Jones, ~75% wetland/pocosin. Not acquirable; mostly wet. 🔍
  - **Hofmann Forest** — ~79,000 ac (Jones + Onslow), sits on the **White Oak Pocosin** ("swamp on a hill"). See A2. 🔍
- Remaining private land tends to be ag/timber parcels interrupted by pocosin, swamp (Great Dover Swamp), and floodplain (Trent River, Island Creek, Reedy Branch). NC Coastal Land Trust is actively acquiring conservation parcels (Island Creek preserve), removing additional tracts from the market. 🔍
- **Assessment:** Entry footprint (~1,000-2,000 ac of buildable upland) is plausibly assemblable from a few large ag/timber owners. A ≥5,000-ac — let alone 15,000-50,000-ac — block of *dry upland* is the genuinely hard part and likely requires multi-owner assembly across wet interruptions. **HUMAN GAP: county GIS parcel + NWI-by-AOI overlay needed to confirm any single dry ≥5,000-ac private block.**

### A2. Hofmann Forest — acquirable? Wet?
- **History:** Owned by NC State's Natural Resources Foundation / Endowment Fund. Famously listed ~2013; agreed sale (~$150M, later ~$131M combined) to out-of-state agribusiness/timber buyers; sale **collapsed Dec 2014** when buyers couldn't close financing, amid lawsuit under NC Environmental Policy Act + faculty/public opposition. 🔍
- **Current status (off-market):** NC State retained ownership; entered a **$78M, 50-year timber deed** with Hofmann Forest Timberlands LLC (managed by Resource Management Service) on ~54,334 ac; new strategy keeps ownership and pursues **conservation of up to ~70,000 ac**; land is now jointly held/managed with NC Wildlife Resources Commission + NC Division of Parks & Recreation. **Effectively NOT acquirable for development.** 🔍
- **Wetland status:** Hofmann is **pocosin** (White Oak Pocosin) — wetland-dominated. The 2014 USACE investigation into illegal ditching (5,500+ ac cleared/ditched, ~6,700 ac wetland impact alleged) and the EPA settlement (plug ditches, restore flow, CWA-compliant water-management plan) confirm extensive jurisdictional wetland. So even if it were available, Hofmann is the wrong land type. 🔍
- **Conclusion:** Hofmann is a red herring for assembly — large + contiguous, but state/foundation-locked AND wet.

### A3. Land-cost order of magnitude
- NC statewide farmland ~**$5,150/ac** (USDA 2024 Land Values, +8.4% YoY). 🔍
- Rural eastern NC raw land generally **under ~$10,000/ac**; coastal-plain timberland cheaper; eastern counties show low market activity / long days-on-market. 🔍
- **Working estimate for Jones ag/timberland: ~$3,000-$8,000/ac** 🔍 (HUMAN GAP — get county sales comps). At 15,000 ac that is roughly **$45M-$120M** raw land alone, before the wetland-quality discount/premium and assembly friction. Entry 2,000 ac ≈ **$6M-$16M**.

## SECTION B — §404 WETLANDS AT SCALE (+ SACKETT) — the decisive physical check

### B4. Is the footprint wet enough that fill is unavoidable?
- Stage-1 framing: Jones is wetland-pervasive (Croatan ~75% wetland; Great Dover Swamp; White Oak Pocosin; coastal-plain hydrology). A 15,000-50,000-ac footprint almost certainly **intersects wetlands somewhere** — full avoidance at build-out scale is implausible. 🔍
- BUT "wetland" ≠ "federally jurisdictional wetland" after Sackett (see B5). Much of the pocosin is **isolated** (no continuous surface connection), which now matters enormously.
- **HUMAN GAP:** Only an NWI-by-AOI pull + on-site delineation can say how much of a specific candidate block is (a) wetland and (b) jurisdictional. Do not guess delineation specifics.

### B5. §404 individual permit reality + the Sackett effect (IMPORTANT — answer moved)
- **Pre-Sackett (Stage-1 assumption):** pervasive pocosin → significant-nexus jurisdiction → individual §404 + mitigation + NEPA → near-KILL.
- **Sackett v. EPA (May 2023):** CWA reaches only wetlands with a **"continuous surface connection"** to a relatively permanent navigable water — wetlands "indistinguishable" from that water. **Isolated wetlands (incl. pocosins, Delmarva/Carolina bays) lose federal protection.** Commentary explicitly names **pocosins** among the wetland types dropped from WOTUS. 🔍
- **NC's response — no state backfill:** **SB 582 (NC Farm Act of 2023 / Session Law 2023-63)** bars NC from regulating wetlands that are not WOTUS — i.e., NC will NOT pick up isolated wetlands that lost federal coverage. DEQ estimated **~2.5 million ac statewide** could lose protection. NC 401-certification/isolated-waters rules now apply only to isolated **streams**, not isolated wetlands. 🔍
- **Net effect for Jones:** A material fraction of Jones's pocosin (the **isolated**, perched, no-surface-connection portions) likely falls **OUT of both federal §404 AND state regulation** post-2023. This is the single biggest change vs. Stage 1 — it **downgrades §404 from probable-KILL to CONCERN**, contingent on siting away from jurisdictional features.
- **What remains jurisdictional:** Wetlands **adjacent with continuous surface connection** to the **Trent River, Island Creek, Reedy Branch** and other mapped tributaries/floodplain are still WOTUS. A city footprint touching those still needs an **individual §404 permit** + compensatory mitigation. 🔍
- **Permit cost/timeline (if individual permit needed):** USACE target 120 days for a complete application but **realistically multi-year**; projects requiring an EIS **average ~3 years** just for NEPA. 🔍
- **Mitigation in eastern NC:** Compensatory mitigation via banks/ILF; NC's coastal **wetland mitigation rate has been on the order of ~$560,000 per credit** (NCDEQ rate schedule, recent) 🔍 — a city-scale fill of even a few hundred jurisdictional acres at typical 2:1-ish ratios could imply **tens to hundreds of millions in mitigation**. HUMAN GAP: confirm current bank credit availability + service-area + ratios for the specific HUC.

### B6. NEPA trigger + city-scale fill feasibility
- NEPA attaches to the **major federal action** = USACE issuance of an **individual §404 permit** (and any federal funding/USFS land touch). An individual permit for a city-scale fill would very likely require an **EA→EIS** (~3 yr typical), plus ESA Section 7 consultation (coastal-plain listed species — e.g., red-cockaded woodpecker in managed pine). 🔍
- **Feasibility:** Permitting a *city-scale* fill of jurisdictional wetland is the kind of action that historically draws litigation (cf. Hofmann NEPA suit) and is slow/uncertain. The viable path is **siting to AVOID jurisdictional (now: surface-connected) wetlands** and confine impacts to isolated/non-jurisdictional ground + uplands — which Sackett+SB582 now make legally easier, though still physically constrained by how much dry/isolated land a given block contains.
- **Bottom line B:** Post-Sackett, the §404 wall is real but **no longer a categorical kill** — it becomes a **siting and delineation problem.** A skilled site selection that hugs isolated pocosin + upland could plausibly minimize federal touch. This is the most important Stage-3 correction to the Stage-1 record.

## SECTION C — ABSORPTION CAPACITY (Lesson L16)

### C7. Construction labor within ~60 min
Civilian labor force (FRED/BLS, Apr 2026, NSA):
| County (seat) | Civilian labor force | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Onslow (Jacksonville + Camp Lejeune) | **~72,400** | largest pool; military town, high turnover |
| Craven (New Bern) | **~40,200** | |
| Lenoir (Kinston) | **~24,400** | declining employment trend |
| Jones (Trenton) | **~4,000** 🔍 | tiny |
| **Adjacent total (within ~60 min)** | **~140,000** | |

- **Construction-relevant headcount:** NC construction ≈ **6.5%** of workers (above US avg). Applied to ~140k → **~9,000 construction workers in-region** 🔍 — and **nearly all are already employed.** Eastern NC has a **documented, active skilled-construction labor shortage** (HBI Fall 2025 report; local eastern-NC builder reports; immigration-driven labor decline 2024-25). 🔍
- **Vs. spec (≥10,000 construction-relevant within 60 min available):** Region has ~9,000 *total* construction workers, of which the *available/slack* portion is a small fraction. **Met only on paper; in practice import-only** for any new-city pace. Camp Lejeune/Onslow construction demand competes directly.
- **Assessment:** Entry phase (build ~1-2k DU over several years) = absorbable with regional + imported crews = **CONCERN**. Build-out (~20k DU) requires sustained thousands of construction workers for a decade+ in a shortage market = **import-only → FAIL-risk** unless the project itself houses and bootstraps a construction workforce.

### C8. Housing stock / vacancy / commute bootstrap
- **Jones County housing:** ~**4,635 units**, **~16.4% vacant** (2020 Census); ~77.6% owner / 22.4% renter of occupied. 🔍 (A "19,538 units" figure seen in one source is inconsistent and is treated as an error.) Tiny absolute stock — cannot house an in-migrating construction workforce.
- **Bootstrap problem:** Entry workers + early residents must either (a) commute from New Bern / Jacksonville / Kinston, or (b) be housed in temporary/early-phase units the project builds first. Average Jones commute is already ~28 min; New Bern (~25-35 min to Trenton), Jacksonville (~35-45 min), Kinston (~30-40 min) are **within ~60 min** 🔍 — commute is feasible for entry crews but adds cost/friction.
- **Build-out:** 50k pop ≈ 5.5× the county; ~20k DU dwarfs the entire 3-county adjacent housing stock's slack. The city must be **self-housing**, and the **first-phase chicken-and-egg** (need housing to attract workers; need workers to build housing) is the classic L16 absorption bind — acute here given the thin local base.
- **Assessment:** Entry = CONCERN (commute viable, vacancy thin but workable). Build-out = FAIL-risk without phased self-housing + workforce import program.

## WHERE DOES THE ANSWER FLIP? (entry vs build-out)

| Dimension | Entry (~2-5k pop / 1-2k DU / ~500 jobs / ≤~2,000 ac) | Build-out (~50k / 20k DU / 15k+ jobs / 15,000-50,000 ac) | Flip driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Land assembly** | CONCERN — small upland block assemblable from few owners 🔍 | FAIL-leaning — dry/isolated upland at 15k-50k ac not demonstrably contiguous; Croatan/Hofmann unavailable | Footprint exceeds available dry contiguous private land |
| **§404 / wetlands** | CONCERN (low) — small footprint can avoid surface-connected wetland; isolated pocosin non-jurisdictional post-Sackett | CONCERN→FAIL-risk — large footprint inevitably nears Trent/Island Creek jurisdictional wetland → individual permit + EIS + mitigation | Scale forces contact with surface-connected WOTUS |
| **Construction labor** | CONCERN — regional + import crews cover phased entry | FAIL-risk — sustained thousands in a shortage market = import-only | Sustained pace > regional construction slack (~<9k total, ~shortage) |
| **Housing/absorption** | CONCERN — commute from New Bern/Jacksonville/Kinston + early units | FAIL-risk — 20k DU >> 3-county slack; self-housing + bootstrap required | DU demand >> adjacent vacant stock |
| **Scale ratio (pop/county)** | ~0.2-0.5× county (OK) | ~5.5× county (past heuristic kill-review) | Build-out overwhelms host county |

**Where it flips:** Every dimension is **passable-to-concerning at entry and fails or nears-failure at build-out.** The flip is driven by (1) running out of contiguous dry/non-jurisdictional upland, and (2) absorption (labor + housing) outrunning a thin eastern-NC market. The §404 picture, notably, is *better* than Stage 1 recorded at BOTH scales due to Sackett + SB 582 — but it re-tightens at build-out as the footprint reaches surface-connected waters.
