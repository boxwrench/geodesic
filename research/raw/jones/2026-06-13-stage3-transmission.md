# Jones County, NC — Stage 3 (Validate): Transmission & Core Infrastructure

**Date:** 2026-06-13 (research run)
**Mode:** B (DRAFT). **Everything below is 🔍 UNVERIFIED draft. Nothing here is ✅.**
**Stage-3 question:** Is extending HV transmission (≥115 kV) and core infrastructure (power, gas, road/rail, broadband) to a master-planned new city in Jones County physically AND economically real — at entry (~5–10 MW) and at build-out (~100–200 MW, more if heavy industrial)? Where does the answer flip on real scale?
**Key reframe (carried from Sweetwater Stage 3):** proximity ≠ headroom. A large new *load* runs the same study/contract gauntlet as new generation. Existing nearby solar interconnection proves *export* wires, not *import/load* headroom.

---

## 0. BOTTOM LINE (draft) 🔍

- **Entry (~5–10 MW): PASS (likely trivial).** Dual electric providers (Jones-Onslow EMC co-op + Duke Energy Progress), an existing industrial park with utilities, ~75% fiber availability, US-17 four-laning underway, Piedmont Natural Gas listed as a county provider. A 5–10 MW load is an ordinary distribution/sub-transmission connection — no special contract, no NCUC proceeding. 🔍
- **Build-out (~100–200 MW): CONCERN.** This lands *on/above the bright line* in NC: **>100 MW = the NCUC "large load" integration regime** Duke is actively building out. It triggers Duke's transmission study → Letter Agreement → Energy Supply Agreement (ESA) gauntlet, with advance refundable capital, minimum-billing (≈85% take), interruptibility, and termination fees. 🔍
- **Transmission posture is the opposite of Sweetwater's, in an instructive way.** Sweetwater sits next to a 500 kV *export* hub; Jones sits in **DEP East**, a region that is **already transmission-constrained by the solar it hosts** (the Timber Mill / Trent River solar fleet is evidence of *generation export* wires that are *full*, not evidence of import headroom). The "lots of nearby solar" signal is therefore **ambiguous-to-negative** for a large new load — it implies a congested local network, not slack. 🔍
- **Nearest confirmed HV anchors are ~15–25 mi out (Jacksonville/Onslow side), not in Trenton.** DEP has a Jacksonville 230 kV substation + a new Jacksonville–Grants Creek 230 kV line (~14 mi, in service ~2020) and the Folkstone–Jacksonville 115 kV line — solid HV presence on the **Onslow/Maysville side**, weaker toward Trenton/Pollocksville. Exact distance from a Trenton site to an adequate ≥115 kV substation is a **HUMAN GAP (HIFLD/GIS).** 🔍
- **Where it flips: ~100 MW** (NCUC large-load threshold), and more sharply if the build-out's local transmission study returns DEP-East congestion upgrades. **More favorable than Sweetwater's 200 MW cliff in form (Duke uses a negotiated contract, not a mandatory PSC special-contract + new-generation requirement at a single bright line), but arguably *less* favorable in physics (constrained network vs. spare-wire-adjacent).** 🔍

## 1. TRANSMISSION — HV lines/substations near Trenton/Pollocksville/Maysville 🔍

**Dual electric service (confirmed, county ED page):** 🔍
- **Jones-Onslow EMC (JOEMC)** — electric co-op; serves the Trenton industrial park and most of the county. Co-ops typically take wholesale power and own distribution/sub-transmission; large new load on a co-op usually means the co-op's G&T (in NC, often **NCEMC**) and/or Duke must wheel/serve it — a different contracting path than direct Duke retail. 🔍 (path detail = gap)
- **Duke Energy Progress (DEP)** — serves Maysville/Pollocksville area; owns the HV transmission in the region. 🔍
- **Carteret-Craven Electric Cooperative** also listed as a county electric provider (eastern edge). 🔍

**Confirmed HV (≥115 kV) infrastructure in the area (DEP):** 🔍
- **Jacksonville 230 kV substation** (off Gum Branch Rd, Onslow County) + **Jacksonville–Grants Creek 230 kV line** (~14 mi, ~$37M, construction 2017→ in service ~July 2020). "Greater capacity and enhanced reliability for Jacksonville and across Eastern NC." This is the strongest nearby HV anchor — but it's on the **Jacksonville/Onslow side**, ~20–25 mi SE of Trenton (closer to Maysville). 🔍
- **Folkstone–Jacksonville City 115 kV line** (Onslow County) — DEP rebuilt ~14 mi (wood→steel, reconductor) for reliability/capacity. Confirms a 115 kV backbone running up the US-17 / Jacksonville–Maysville corridor. 🔍
- US-electric-transmission GIS layers (Felt, NC OneMap "Duke Transmission Easements") exist but were **not parsed for exact line routes/voltages through Jones** — HUMAN GAP. 🔍

**Distance-to-adequate-substation vs. spec (~10–15 mi):** 🔍
- **Maysville/Pollocksville (SE, US-17 corridor toward Jacksonville): plausibly within ~10–20 mi of 115/230 kV** given the Folkstone–Jacksonville 115 kV and Jacksonville 230 kV assets. Likely **PASS-ish on distance** for a SE-county site. 🔍
- **Trenton (county seat, NW interior near Croatan / Hofmann Forest): less HV-dense; distance likely toward/over the 15-mi spec.** A Trenton-centric site probably needs a **new HV tap line + substation build**. **Exact distance = HUMAN GAP (HIFLD/GIS measurement needed).** 🔍

**Does existing solar interconnection imply import/load headroom? — NO (this is the key Stage-3 nuance):** 🔍
- Timber Mill Solar (~108–112 MW + storage, Cypress Creek, in **DEP East** balancing authority) and Trent River Solar (Pollocksville, 20-yr PPA to DEP under NC CPRE) prove the local network can **inject** generation. 🔍
- **But DEP East is a known transmission-constrained, solar-saturated region.** Duke's CPRE process and queue studies repeatedly flag eastern/southeastern NC constraints; major relief projects (e.g., Lee–Milburnie line enabling ~1,600 MW solar + 260 MW storage to move power **toward the Triangle, where load is**) exist precisely *because* eastern NC over-generates relative to local load and the wires out are congested. 🔍
- **Implication:** the area is rich in *generation-export* wires that are largely *committed/congested*, and *poor* in local *load*. A large new 24/7 load could actually be **welcome** (load near generation relieves export congestion) **OR** require its own upgrades depending on where the constraints bind — only a DEP transmission study resolves it. Net: **the solar signal is ambiguous, leaning "congested network, not spare headroom."** This is a genuine concern, not a pass. 🔍

**Transmission verdict (draft):** Proximity to ≥115 kV is **good on the Maysville/Onslow side, weak toward Trenton**. Headroom for 100–200 MW is **study-gated and plausibly constrained** (DEP East congestion). Entry-scale: comfortable. Build-out: concern. 🔍

## 2. LOAD AT SCALE — can the grid serve ~100–200 MW? Duke large-load regime 🔍

**The NC threshold is bright-line and current: >100 MW = the NCUC large-load integration regime.** 🔍
- (PRIMARY: Nelson Mullins "Megawatt Minute," Aug 1 2025, summarizing Duke's **July 24 2025 NCUC filing**, Docket re: "how to integrate large load additions, *i.e., over 100 MW*.") 🔍
- Driver: NCUC now requires Duke to file **semiannual reports on major load additions**; data centers are ~⅓ of incoming projects but ~**80% of projected new demand** in NC. The regulatory regime is **actively being written in 2025–2026** (rate cases DEP Oct 2025 / DEC Jan 2026; Carbon Plan/IRP Oct 1 2025; a DEP→DEC merger docket) — timeline/political risk for a build-out-scale request. 🔍

**Duke's large-load process (current, no formal tariff yet):** 🔍
1. **Initial engagement + transmission study.** Duke advises *early* engagement; explicitly warns that **"limited transmission capacity in some high-growth areas may face longer interconnection timelines."** 🔍
2. **Letter Agreement** (lets Duke start design/procurement before the ESA). Imposes: **advance refundable capital payments, minimum-billing provisions, termination fees, interruptible-service obligations, accelerated ESA deadlines.** 🔍
3. **Energy Supply Agreement (ESA)** — final contract. The "boilerplate" includes **minimum take = greater of metered usage or ~85% of contracted capacity**, minimum contract length, early-termination penalties. 🔍
- Duke **aggregates large loads into "tranches"** resembling a generator-interconnection cluster study → **queue position risk** behind other NC data centers. 🔍
- Duke's stance: **no new tariff needed** (existing schedules allocate costs), but acknowledges this could change — i.e., the rules a 2027–2030 build-out would face are **not yet fixed**. 🔍

**NC regulatory threshold at scale + "who pays":** 🔍
- NC's live policy fight (SB 730; WRAL/Canary/DataCenterKnowledge, 2026) is **cost allocation** — protecting residential ratepayers from data-center-driven upgrades. Expect a build-out ESA to **internalize the causer-pays costs** (advance capital, minimum take, security) similar in spirit to RMP's WY "causer pays." 🔍
- Unlike WY, NC does **not** appear to have a single statutory MW number that auto-triggers a **PSC special contract + mandatory new-generation requirement**. The ≥100 MW trigger is a **process/reporting + negotiated-contract** regime, not a hard "must build a power plant" cliff. 🔍

**More favorable than Sweetwater's 200 MW cliff?** 🔍
- **In contractual *form*: YES, modestly.** Sweetwater/RMP has a hard **≥200 MW → WY-PSC special contract + identified incremental generation + full project-cost security** bright line. Duke's NC regime is a **negotiated Letter Agreement/ESA** that scales with the study; there's no single statutory MW wall that forces a generation build + commission proceeding. The flip is *softer/earlier* (~100 MW) but *less absolute*. 🔍
- **In *physics*: arguably WORSE.** Sweetwater is next to a 500 kV export hub built for ~1,500 MW; Jones is in a **constrained, solar-saturated DEP-East pocket** with HV strength on the Onslow side and weakness toward Trenton. The binding risk shifts from *contract terms* (Sweetwater) to *transmission upgrade cost + queue* (Jones). 🔍
- **Heavy-industrial (>200 MW):** would almost certainly require named transmission upgrades and could pull DEP-East congestion-relief projects onto the critical path. **Study-gated; treat as concern→fail until a Duke study says otherwise.** 🔍

**Load-at-scale verdict (draft):** Serving 100–200 MW is **procedurally real (Duke has a defined intake) but physically uncertain and cost-loaded** in this constrained pocket. CONCERN at 100–200 MW; entry-scale is a clean PASS. 🔍

## 3. OTHER INFRASTRUCTURE 🔍

**Natural gas:** 🔍
- County ED page lists **Piedmont Natural Gas** (the dominant NC gas LDC, >1M customers NC/SC/TN) **and Jenkins Gas & Oil** as county gas providers. **Presence is asserted; actual pipe size / proximity of a main to a candidate site / firm-capacity for ~50–150 MMcf/d of gas-fired backup or industrial process load = HUMAN GAP.** Rural Jones likely has limited distribution-main capacity; a large industrial gas load could need a lateral off a Piedmont/transmission main. 🔍

**Highway access (good):** 🔍
- **US-17** (Wilmington–New Bern–Jacksonville corridor) through the county; **US-17 four-laning Jacksonville→Maysville now underway**, New Bern→Jacksonville programmed to four-lane. 🔍
- **US-70** across the NE part of the county → Morehead City port (E) and toward I-95/Raleigh (W). 🔍
- **Future I-42** (US-70 "Super 70" corridor, FAST Act High-Priority Corridor #82, I-40→Morehead City) runs the US-70 alignment **near (NE of) the county**, not through Trenton — a real future interstate within reach but not adjacent. 🔍
- NC-41 to I-40 (~44 mi) and I-95 (~90 mi). **Highway = PASS at both scales.** 🔍

**Freight rail (weak in-county):** 🔍
- **No active freight rail line confirmed in Jones County itself.** Nearest Norfolk Southern (Southern Railway) freight service is **New Bern (~30 min)** and **Kinston (~30 min)**; piggyback/intermodal at Rocky Mount. Ports: Morehead City (~58 mi, NS rail) and Wilmington (~87 mi, CSX). 🔍
- Jones is part of the **NC Global TransPark Authority** (Kinston) "integrated air/rail/road/sea" region — a logistics affiliation, not on-site rail. 🔍
- **Heavy-industrial build-out wanting on-site rail = GAP/likely FAIL** without a new spur (~30 mi to nearest line). For a data-center-style load, rail is non-critical. 🔍

**Broadband (adequate, surprising for rural):** 🔍
- Trenton reports **~75% fiber availability**; providers include **Brightspeed (fiber to ~2 Gbps), EarthLink Fiber (to 5 Gbps marketed), CenturyLink (DSL), Eastern Carolina Broadband (fixed wireless)**. 🔍
- Residential-coverage stats ≠ industrial-grade redundant dark fiber / carrier diversity at a specific site. **Site-level carrier-grade fiber + route diversity = HUMAN GAP**, but baseline is **PASS-ish** and better than expected for a county this rural. 🔍

## 4. WHERE DOES THE ANSWER FLIP? (entry ~5–10 MW vs. build-out ~100–200 MW) 🔍

| Dimension | Entry (~5–10 MW) | Build-out (~100–200 MW) | Flip point / driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Electric regime** | Ordinary distribution/sub-T service; JOEMC co-op or DEP retail. No special contract. **PASS** | **NCUC >100 MW large-load regime**: Duke transmission study → Letter Agreement → ESA; advance capital, ~85% min-take, interruptibility, termination fees, "tranche" queue. **CONCERN** | **~100 MW** = NCUC large-load threshold (Duke 7/24/25 filing). Softer than WY's 200 MW PSC cliff, but earlier. 🔍 |
| **Transmission headroom** | Sub-transmission/115 kV adequate locally. **PASS** | DEP-East is solar-saturated & congestion-prone; nearby solar = *export* wires, not import slack. Upgrades likely; cost/queue study-gated. **CONCERN→possible FAIL if heavy-industrial** | Flips when the load exceeds local 115 kV / sub-T capacity and forces a 230 kV tie + DEP-East congestion study. 🔍 |
| **Distance to ≥115 kV sub** | Fine anywhere; small load taps existing distribution. **PASS** | Maysville/Onslow side ~10–20 mi to 115/230 kV (OK); **Trenton interior likely >15 mi → new HV tap + substation.** **CONCERN (site-dependent)** | Flips by *site*: SE-county passes, Trenton-centric may need new HV build. **HIFLD/GIS gap.** 🔍 |
| **Natural gas** | Piedmont/Jenkins residential-commercial service adequate. **PASS** | Large industrial/backup gas load may exceed rural distribution main; needs lateral/firm capacity. **GAP** | Flips at industrial gas volumes; capacity unverified. 🔍 |
| **Highway** | US-17/US-70 fine. **PASS** | Same; four-laning + future I-42 nearby help. **PASS** | Does **not** flip. 🔍 |
| **Freight rail** | Not needed. **PASS (N/A)** | No in-county line; ~30 mi to NS at New Bern/Kinston. Heavy-industrial needing rail → **FAIL w/o spur**; data-center → N/A. **PASS or FAIL by use** | Flips only if build-out is rail-dependent heavy industry. 🔍 |
| **Broadband** | ~75% fiber; multiple providers. **PASS** | Need carrier-grade redundant fiber at site; baseline good. **PASS (verify)** | Generally does not flip. 🔍 |

**One-line answer:** The flip is at **~100 MW** (NC's NCUC large-load line) on the *contract* axis and at **whatever the DEP-East transmission study returns** on the *physics* axis. Entry-scale is a clean PASS; the build-out's real risk is **congested-pocket transmission upgrade cost + queue**, not a single statutory generation cliff. Versus Sweetwater: **better contractual form, worse network physics.** 🔍
