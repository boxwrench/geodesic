# Principal Feedback Roadmap

Date: 2026-06-20

This note preserves principal feedback on the first site-analysis reports and separates it into two lanes:

1. **General method improvements** that should strengthen Geodesic for any large entitlement search.
2. **Current-attempt assumptions** that matter for this project but should not be baked into the generic search method.

The goal is to improve the screening process before applying it to actual candidate city sites.

## Delivery Rule

Going forward, these improvements should be folded into the normal state, county, and site reports rather than delivered as a separate companion memo. The one exception is the already-shared first-pass finding: because that artifact has already been passed along, the revised South Dakota/Jones work should be packaged as a one-time addendum that clearly states what changed, what new search was performed, and which original conclusions are affirmed, weakened, or reversed.

## General Method Improvements

These changes apply across projects.

### County Posture Needs More Depth

County friendliness should not be inferred from a shallow scan. The county screen should look harder at actual development posture, especially:

- Planning commission and zoning commission membership, cadence, and recent votes.
- County board or commission votes on large projects.
- Recent approvals, rejections, moratoria, litigation, and imposed conditions.
- Staff reports and recommendations where available.
- Local public-comment patterns and organized opposition.

The existing veto-potential framing is directionally right. The improvement is to ground "posture" in observed decisions and repeat actors.

### Land-Assembly Claims Need A Search Method

A report should not imply "no sites above X acres" unless it states the search method. A South Dakota report implied no sites above 10,000 acres, but the principal found a 38,000-acre ranch.

Future reports should separate:

- Single-owner large tracts.
- Assemblable adjacent tracts.
- Listed ranch/farm properties.
- Unlisted but identifiable ranch/farm blocks.
- Mineral, water, access, conservation, easement, and encumbrance issues.

### Water Analysis Must Distinguish New Rights From Acquirable Rights

Water should not be marked as a blocker merely because new rights look unlikely. Reports must distinguish:

- New appropriations or new rights.
- Purchase or lease of existing rights.
- Acquisition of land with attached rights.
- Transferability, change-of-use, place-of-use, and practical redirection limits.

### Flood And Wetlands Need Earlier Screening

Floodplain and wetlands constraints should be early gate signals, not late diligence. Jones County's flood-zone issue was a useful catch because similar issues killed or nearly killed prior nearby sites.

Future reports should estimate:

- FEMA flood zone and floodway exposure.
- Wetlands, hydric soils, streams, and drainage constraints.
- Buildable acreage after constrained areas are removed.
- Federal, state, and local permitting implications.
- Mitigation uncertainty and timeline risk.

### Report Provenance Should Be Explicit

The principal asked how autonomously the reports were generated. Future reports should include a short provenance note:

- What was generated autonomously.
- What sources were used.
- What assumptions came from the principal or team.
- What was inferred.
- What still requires counsel, engineering, or human confirmation.

## Current-Attempt Assumptions

These shape this project's search, but may not apply to another user's search.

### Self-Built Power

The current project expects to build its own power. Grid weakness should not dominate early scoring unless there is a hard interconnection, generation-siting, fuel, transmission, legal, or permitting issue that blocks self-build.

Generic method implication:

- Keep power in the framework, but make the project spec declare whether the search assumes grid service, self-build, or either path.

### Water Rights Acquisition Is Acceptable

For this attempt, buying rights is acceptable, including buying a farm or ranch with rights and redirecting them if lawful and practical.

Generic method implication:

- The framework should evaluate acquisition pathways, not only new-right availability.

### State-Level Land-Use Exemption Is A Final-Site Dealbreaker

For this attempt, a state-level exemption from local land-use authority is a dealbreaker for final site choice.

Friendly counties still matter because the state is unlikely to override a county's current wishes. County posture therefore remains important even if the target entitlement path ultimately needs state-level authority.

Generic method implication:

- Treat state override/preemption/exemption as a project-specific gate. Do not assume every search requires it.

### Flood/Wetlands Risk Tolerance Is Low

Flood and wetlands constraints have already killed or nearly killed prior nearby sites. For this attempt, they should be treated as early dealbreaker candidates until buildable-acreage and mitigation risk are clear.

## Roadmap

1. Update the county screen and county template so posture depth is a required evidence pull.
2. Add land-assembly search-method discipline to the signal inventory.
3. Update the water signal to distinguish new rights from acquired/transferred rights.
4. Add a provenance note to output templates.
5. Add current-attempt assumptions to the project spec rather than the generic framework.
6. Produce a one-time addendum to the already-shared South Dakota/Jones findings under the revised method.
7. Apply the revised workflow to the current target site, with the upgrades folded into the main report.
