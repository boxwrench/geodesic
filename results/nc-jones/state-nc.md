## Verdict

**North Carolina is the most *legally* favorable of the three pilot states — and the most *physically/boundary* constrained.** On the governance variables NC is strong: it has a real **statutory early-vesting regime** (GS 160D-108 permit choice + 160D-108.1 site-specific vesting plans — rights vest on plan approval, not late common-law reliance, the best vesting story of the three states); **rezonings are NOT referendable** (NC holds only elections the General Assembly authorizes, and none authorizes a rezoning referendum — so the California ballot-veto failure mode is absent, like WY and unlike SD); and the **mini-NEPA (SEPA) was gutted in 2015** (SL 2015-90 — triggers only above **$10M of *state* funds** or use of public land), so a privately financed project that takes no state money largely escapes state environmental-review litigation. The decisive-conjunction kill (private mini-NEPA + referendum reach) is **absent**. **But the Southeast risk profile is the inverse of the Mountain West:** the kill-first layer here is **federal §404/wetlands** (coastal-plain hydrology — [heuristic 1](../../framework/01-heuristics.md), and the dominant Jones Stage-1 question), **municipal ETJ** (GS 160D-202 lets a town extend zoning **1-3 miles** beyond its limits — a *hostile* nearby municipality can reach an unincorporated site, the NC boundary landmine), water is **regulated-riparian** with **Jones inside the Central Coastal Plain Capacity Use Area** (the state is actively trying to *cap* groundwater withdrawals — a hard durability constraint on a new-city demand), and **incorporation is a legislative act** (only the General Assembly can charter a town — a lobbying project with Reedy-Creek-style state exposure, the *worst* incorporation path of the three states). **Provisional posture: PASS to county stage (Jones) on governance; the binding risks are §404 + CCPCUA water + ETJ, all resolved at the county/site layer — and the entitlement path should favor county-DA + vested plan, NOT incorporation.**

## The four variables

### 1. Vesting timing
- **Rule: early-statutory (a real asset).** **GS 160D-108.1** — a **site-specific vesting plan** (PUD, subdivision plat, conditional-district zoning plan, special-use permit, etc.) "**remains vested for a period of two years**" on approval, extendable, protecting against later regulatory change; **GS 160D-108** codifies "permit choice" (applicant may choose the rules in effect at application). This is *statutory* vesting closer to the Texas Ch. 245 model than to common-law reliance → **durability purchasable relatively early**, at the plan-approval stage. ✅ (160D-108 / 108.1, secondary-confirmed against ncleg.gov PDFs; 🔍 final read for the 2-yr term + extension mechanics and whether a development agreement under GS 160D-1001+ extends the term).
- **Citations:** GS 160D-108, GS 160D-108.1 ✅; UNC SOG *Permit Choice & Vested Rights* guidance. NC also has a **statutory development-agreement act (GS 160D Art. 10)** — 🔍 confirm; if it allows long-term DAs that lock the regulatory regime, NC's durability story is strong.

### 2. Referendum reach over the durability instrument
- **Do initiative/referendum extend to local land-use legislative acts? NO.** Per UNC SOG: *"There is no statute authorizing commissioners to have the voters decide whether to approve or disapprove a property rezoning,"* and in NC *"only those elections specifically authorized by the [state] constitution or by statute … may be held."* NC has **no general initiative/referendum** for counties/municipalities. **The ballot-box veto is structurally absent** — like WY, unlike SD. ✅ (UNC SOG Coates' Canons "Limited Room for Referendums in NC"; IRI — NC has no statewide I&R).
- **DA legislative vs. quasi-judicial:** less critical here because there's no referendum channel to exploit; matters only for the breach test. 🔍
- **Citations:** UNC SOG Coates' Canons ✅; Initiative & Referendum Institute (NC: no I&R) ✅.

### 3. Mini-NEPA
- **Exists but largely defanged for private projects.** NC SEPA (GS 113A Art. 1) was **narrowed by SL 2015-90**: review now triggers only when an action involves **> $10,000,000 of State funds** for a project, or use of public land — and is limited to *"direct"* impacts. A privately financed new-city project that takes **no state money and no state land** is largely **outside SEPA**. ✅ (SL 2015-90; NC DEQ SEPA program; UNC SOG 2015 environmental-legislation review). **Strategic rule: keep state funding off the project's critical path** (mirrors the federal-handle logic in [framework/07](../../framework/07-federal-layer.md)). ❓ counsel: confirm no residual SEPA citizen-suit hook and that no anticipated state permit independently triggers review.

### 4. Boundary gatekeeper
- **Boundary commission?** No LAFCO-style commission; but **two NC-specific gatekeepers**:
- **Municipal ETJ — the landmine. GS 160D-202** lets a municipality exercise planning/zoning jurisdiction **up to 1 mile** (and, by population, **2-3 miles**) beyond its corporate limits. **A *hostile* nearby town can impose its zoning on an unincorporated candidate site.** Recent law has trimmed ETJ in places (e.g., eliminated where countywide zoning is in effect; 2025 SL2025-30 froze Wake County ETJ) — so the *current* ETJ status around a candidate site must be checked. ✅ (GS 160D-202; UNC SOG ETJ summaries; SL 2025-30). **This is the single most important NC boundary check at county stage.**
- **Annexation:** NC curtailed *involuntary* annexation (2011 reforms) — annexation is now largely **voluntary/petition-based**. 🔍 confirm current mechanism.
- **Incorporation — legislative act (worst path here).** A NC town "**may be established by an act of the General Assembly**," which enacts its charter; review runs through the **Joint Legislative Commission on Municipal Incorporations** (created 1986). *"No standards restrict the legislature's discretion."* → incorporation is a **lobbying project, not a petition**, and the resulting charter is held at legislative pleasure (**Reedy-Creek state-turnover exposure**, [breach test](../../framework/00-the-question.md#the-breach-test)). ✅ (UNC SOG *Incorporation of a NC Town*; ncleg incorporation-procedure summary). **Implication: prefer county-entitlement + vested plan over incorporation in NC.**

## Water doctrine

- **Doctrine: regulated riparian** (not prior appropriation) — riparian common law overlaid by a state withdrawal-permitting regime. Per [heuristic 1](../../framework/01-heuristics.md), in the Southeast water is *"rarely binding"* as a riparian matter — **but Jones is the exception:**
- **✅ Jones County is inside the Central Coastal Plain Capacity Use Area (CCPCUA)** — one of 15 designated counties. Groundwater withdrawal **>100,000 gal/day requires a capacity-use permit**; the CCPCUA rules' stated objective is *"to reduce ground water withdrawals to a sustainable level and make large users invest in other sustainable sources."* A new-city demand (~6-9 MGD at build-out, [spec](../../framework/06-project-spec.md)) lands in a basin the state is actively **capping** — a serious water-durability constraint, the Southeast analog to WY's compact curtailment. ✅ (NC DEQ CCPCUA; effective 2002-08-01).
- **Authority/database:** NC DEQ Division of Water Resources; CCPCUA permitting + water-use registration ([registry](../../ops/data-sources.md#water-state-specific)). 🔍 surface-water (Trent/Neuse) alternative + interbasin-transfer rules (note SL 2015-90 kept IBT under SEPA).

## First legally operative act / full durable package

- **Cheapest legally operative first step:** county **conditional-district rezoning / PUD + site-specific vesting plan (160D-108.1)** — gives a *statutory* vested right at plan approval, the earliest durable foothold of the three states. Pair with a GS 160D Art. 10 development agreement if it extends the term.
- **Durable package:** 🔍 recorded conditional-zoning/PUD + approved site-specific vesting plan (term extended to horizon) + secured **CCPCUA water permit** + §404 strategy (avoid individual permit) + confirmed **no hostile ETJ** over the site.

## Open questions for counsel (priority order)

1. **§404 / wetlands (decisive, county-stage):** can the candidate footprint proceed on *nationwide* permits and avoid an individual §404 permit + NEPA? (Coastal-plain Jones is the highest-risk water/wetland setting of the three counties — [framework/07](../../framework/07-federal-layer.md).)
2. **CCPCUA water:** is there permittable groundwater headroom for a new-city demand in a capacity-use area the state is capping, or must supply come from surface water / IBT (which re-triggers SEPA)?
3. **ETJ:** is the candidate site within any municipality's 1-3 mi ETJ, and is that municipality friendly or hostile? Current post-reform ETJ status.
4. **Vesting/DA durability:** confirm 160D-108.1 term + extension and whether a GS 160D Art. 10 DA can lock the regime to the project horizon with an enjoinable remedy.
5. **SEPA residual:** confirm no state-funding/state-land hook and no anticipated state permit independently triggers SEPA review.
