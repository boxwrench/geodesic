## Verdict

**Stage 1 (2026-06-13, draft; pre-pilot = all signals run, kills as data):** Jones is **🔴-leaning, and — importantly for the pilot — killed by a completely different layer than the Western counties.** The Southeast kill-first layer is **wetlands/§404**, and Jones is close to the framework's "pervasive wetlands at all candidate sites" kill trigger: the county is dominated by the **Croatan National Forest** (~75% wetlands/pocosin), the **Great Dover Swamp**, **White Oak Pocosin**, and the state-owned **Hofmann Forest**. A new-city footprint here would very likely need an **individual §404 permit + NEPA** on the critical path (the [federal layer](../../framework/07-federal-layer.md) risk, realized). Second hard flag: **scale** — county pop **~9,172** (4th-least-populous in NC), so 50k build-out ≈ 5.5x the county ([heuristic 3](../../framework/01-heuristics.md) kill-review). Plus **CCPCUA water** capping (per [state screen](state-nc.md)) and large federal/state forest blocks complicating assembly. **Offsetting:** NC's *governance* is the most favorable of the three (statutory vesting, no referendum, defanged SEPA — [state screen](state-nc.md)) — but governance strength cannot rescue a site that fails the physical §404 layer ([heuristic 1](../../framework/01-heuristics.md): fall in love at infrastructure, kill at the constraint layer). **Net for the principal:** Jones is the clearest physical-constraint case of the three — strong law, wrong dirt. Recorded as data, not eliminated (pilot rule). **Pilot value:** Jones makes the region-conditional kill-ordering ([heuristic 1](../../framework/01-heuristics.md)) *real* — wetlands/§404 discriminated here exactly as water did in the West.

**Stage 3 (Validate) verdict (🔍 2026-06-13) — Validate *partially rescued* the kill layer, then the constraint migrated.** The 4-agent Validate fan-out (detail in [Stage 3 below](#stage-3--validate-1wk-budget--agent-gathered-2026-06-13-mode-b-draft)) produced the pilot's most consequential single finding: **post-*Sackett v. EPA* (2023) + NC SB 582, isolated pocosin wetlands likely fall OUT of §404** — so the Stage-1 "pervasive wetlands = categorical kill" **downgrades to a siting/delineation CONCERN** (see [L17](../../research/pilot-lessons.md)). But the constraint didn't vanish; it **migrated and multiplied at 50k scale**: (1) **firm water** — the CCPCUA caps groundwater (Jones' own deep wells are stamped CUA25) and the wholesale neighbors are capped too, forcing a ~$50-120M **regional Neuse surface-water project** (NRWASA, ~24 mi) for build-out; (2) a **buildable-area squeeze** — ~75% wet + *realized* record flooding (Florence 2018 put Pollocksville Main St under ~20 ft; surge ran ~50 river-mi up the Trent) leaves doubtful contiguous dry-and-flood-safe upland at ~50k ac; (3) **absorption** — labor import-only. Each is **passable at entry, fails-or-near-fails at build-out** — the cleanest [L16](../../research/pilot-lessons.md) case in the pilot. **Net: Jones is no longer a clean §404 kill, but not a survivor either — its feasibility is now *entirely scale-dependent*** (a ≤~15k-ac build-out could plausibly clear all three; 50k cannot). Governance remains best-in-pilot. Recorded as data; this is the strongest "re-open if the project is smaller" candidate of the two non-survivors.

**Stage 2 (🔍 draft, 2026-06-13):** Topology **Wild West→low-end Gatekeeper** — **no county zoning** (Land Use Plan: *"the County does not have any form of zoning"*) but a working 7-member Planning Board does plats + solar site-plans. Friendliness grade **86/100 — but L15 caveat INVALIDATES it as "friendly board"**: like Fall River's 88.5, it scores high from *absence of a gate*, not a pro-growth machine. ETJ exposure **zero** (towns don't exercise ETJ). The binding constraints remain Stage-1 physical (§404, CCPCUA, scale), not the board. Detail: [Stage 2 below](#stage-2--filter-1d-budget-actual-1d-mode-b-draft-2026-06-13).

## Stage 3 — Validate (1wk budget; 🔍 agent-gathered 2026-06-13, Mode-B DRAFT)

> Screened against the **placeholder spec** ([framework/06](../../framework/06-project-spec.md)); findings flag where the answer flips on the principal's real numbers. 4-agent fan-out. Caches: [water/CCPCUA](../../research/raw/jones/2026-06-13-stage3-water-economics.md) · [transmission](../../research/raw/jones/2026-06-13-stage3-transmission.md) · [land+§404+labor](../../research/raw/jones/2026-06-13-stage3-land-wetlands-labor.md) · [geotech](../../research/raw/jones/2026-06-13-stage3-geotech.md). **Pre-pilot rule: run as data even though Stage 1/2 lean 🔴.** Headline: Validate produced a **material correction to the Stage-1 §404 kill** (see land/§404 below).

- **Water source / pipeline economics:** 🔍 **CONCERN at entry; FAILS on groundwater at build-out — the CCPCUA cap is real and confirmed.** Jones County's own system is **100% groundwater** and its LWSP literally stamps **CUA25 (25% reduction)** on its deep Cretaceous wells vs. CUA0 on shallower Castle Hayne wells — direct confirmation the deep aquifers are throttled. The obvious wholesale fallbacks are *also capped*: New Bern (~24 mi) and ONWASA/Jacksonville (~20 mi) are both 100% groundwater inside/beside the same CCPCUA, with no durable surplus to sell a 6-9 MGD city (New Bern's deep wells run on a temporary, revocable, 50%-reduced permit; its own demand hits ~97% of supply by 2070). **The one credible at-scale source is surface water:** **NRWASA in Kinston (~24 mi)** holds a **30 MGD Neuse River withdrawal permit with ~15 MGD regional headroom** — exactly the surface swap DEQ used to start reversing aquifer decline, so it isn't capped. IBT geography is favorable (Neuse 10-1 and Trent 10-3 are sub-basins of one major basin → a Neuse→Trent delivery likely does *not* trigger an IBT certificate — confirm boundary + return basin). **Flip:** the answer flips on **source *type*** — entry can ride a local Castle Hayne wellfield; build-out is forced entirely onto a **regional Neuse surface-water project** (~$50-120M+ for a ~24-mi main through heavy wetlands, 3-5+ yr permitting, single-supplier concentration risk, coastal SLR/salt-wedge exposure).
- **Transmission tie-in path:** 🔍 **PASS at entry; CONCERN at build-out.** Dual electric service (Jones-Onslow EMC + Duke Energy Progress). Strongest nearby HV is on the Onslow side — DEP's **Jacksonville 230 kV substation + Jacksonville-Grants Creek 230 kV line (~14 mi)** and the Folkstone-Jacksonville 115 kV; a Maysville/SE-county site is plausibly within ~10-20 mi of ≥115 kV, but a **Trenton-interior site likely exceeds the 15-mi spec → new HV tap + substation.** **The local solar (Timber Mill ~112 MW, Trent River) cuts the *wrong* way** — DEP-East is a known **solar-saturated, congestion-prone** region (cf. the Lee-Milburnie relief project), so nearby solar implies a *congested* local network, not import headroom. **Load at scale:** NC's current bright line is **>100 MW = the NCUC large-load integration regime** (Duke 7/2025 filing: transmission study → Letter Agreement w/ refundable advance capital, ~85% min-take, termination fees → Energy Supply Agreement, tranche-queued). **vs. Sweetwater's 200 MW cliff:** better in contractual *form* (negotiated ESA, no statutory PSC-special-contract wall), worse in *physics* (constrained DEP-East pocket vs. a 500 kV export hub) — flip is *earlier* (~100 MW) but *softer*. Highways strong PASS (US-17 four-laning, US-70, future I-42); **freight rail weak** (no in-county line, nearest NS ~30 min); broadband ~PASS; industrial gas unconfirmed.
- **Geological constraints:** 🔍 **CONCERN, with material KILL-by-squeeze risk at the 50k footprint.** No single hazard is a clean kill, but they **overlap on the same ground.** **Soils = two-population problem:** the ~75%-wet fraction is **pocosin peat** (near-zero bearing, large settlement, high water table — AVOID, not treat, at scale); buildable material is the *minority* firm sand uplands (Trenton ~26 ft, Comfort ~52 ft). **Flooding is realized, not modeled:** Hurricane Florence (2018) flooded Trenton's courthouse/schools and **~half of Pollocksville (Main St under ~20 ft)**, 4-5 ft above Floyd. **Storm surge ran ~50 river-miles up the Trent to Pollocksville (~9-10 ft)** — "inland = safe from surge" is FALSE along the river corridors. SLR ~1.1 ft by 2050 / ~3.8 ft by 2100 amplifies it over the city's life. Seismicity routine/low. **A buildable flood-safe upland bench is plausible at ~15,000 ac but doubtful at ~50,000 ac (~1/6 of the county)** given ~75% wet land + Croatan NF + a realized record flood — the decisive site-specific gate is an SSURGO + FEMA NFHL + LiDAR bench analysis at the actual AOI (human gap).
- **Land assembly + §404 at scale + labor (absorption — [L16](../../research/pilot-lessons.md)):** 🔍 **The big Stage-3 correction: §404 downgrades from probable-KILL → CONCERN.** *Sackett v. EPA* (2023) cut federal jurisdiction to wetlands with a **continuous surface connection** — **isolated pocosins likely fall OUT of §404** — and critically, **NC SB 582 (2023 Farm Act) barred the state from backfilling**, so isolated wetlands lose *both* federal and state protection. This materially changes Stage 1: §404 becomes a **siting/delineation problem, not a categorical kill.** *But* wetlands adjacent to the Trent River / Island Creek remain WOTUS; a city-scale fill near them still triggers an **individual permit + likely EIS (~3 yr) + mitigation (~$560k/credit, coastal NC 🔍).** **Land assembly = CONCERN (entry) / FAIL-leaning (build-out):** the two biggest contiguous blocks are off-limits — **Croatan NF** (federal, ~75% wet) and **Hofmann Forest** (~79,000 ac but state/foundation-locked under a $78M/50-yr timber deed + conservation co-ownership, *and* itself White Oak Pocosin wetland; famously listed ~2013, sale collapsed Dec 2014, now effectively unacquirable). A **dry-upland, non-jurisdictional, contiguous ≥5,000-ac private block** is the hard four-way intersection — entry-plausible, 15k-50k ac not demonstrable without GIS; land ~$3-8k/ac. **Absorption = CONCERN (entry) / FAIL-risk (build-out):** adjacent labor force ~140k (Onslow ~72k incl. Camp Lejeune, Craven ~40k, Lenoir ~24k, Jones ~4k) → ~9,000 construction workers at typical share, **nearly all already employed** in a documented eastern-NC shortage; entry absorbable with import, build-out (~20k DU, 5.5× county) is import-only + a self-housing bootstrap.

**Stage 3 net (🔍):** Jones is the most *interesting* Stage-3 result of the three because **Validate partially rescued the Stage-1 kill layer** — Sackett + SB 582 mean §404 is **no longer the categorical wall** it was recorded as, demoting Jones from "strong law, dead dirt" toward "strong law, *hard* dirt." But the constraint didn't vanish, it **migrated and multiplied**: at 50k scale the binding stack is now **(1) firm water — groundwater capped, forcing a ~$50-120M regional Neuse surface project; (2) a buildable-area squeeze — ~75% wet + realized record flooding + surge up the river corridors leaves doubtful contiguous dry-and-flood-safe upland at 50k ac; (3) absorption — labor import-only.** Each is *passable at entry, fails-or-near-fails at build-out* — the cleanest L16 illustration in the pilot. **Read: Jones is no longer a clean §404 kill, but it is not a survivor either** — it's a candidate whose feasibility is now **entirely scale-dependent** and would need an AOI-specific dry-upland + water-project work-up to resolve. Spec-dependence is decisive here: a *smaller* build-out (≤~15k ac, low-thousands pop) could plausibly clear all three; 50k cannot.

## Stage 4 — Commit (1mo budget) — *brief documented rationale; Commit NOT warranted at full scale (see [scorecard](../../results/scorecard.md))*

> Per the pre-pilot rule a 🔴 is recorded, not acted on. Stage 3 *softened* Jones (§404 no longer categorical), so the override threshold is lower than Fall River's — but the candidate still does not warrant a full Commit work-up at 50k scale. This documents the veto stack that *would* apply so an override/scale-down decision is informed.

- **Veto player map (if advanced):** (1) **NC DEQ Division of Water Resources** — the binding gatekeeper, both for any CCPCUA allocation *and* for the NRWASA/Neuse surface-water permit + IBT determination; this is where water lives or dies. (2) **USACE Wilmington District** — the residual §404 authority over *jurisdictional* (Trent/Island Creek-adjacent) wetlands + the EIS; much smaller post-Sackett but not zero. (3) **NC Coastal Land Trust** — latent, $0-detectable conservation veto (Island Creek preserve "buffer from residential development"); no record opposing private Jones development, but a structural actor. (4) **Duke Energy / NCUC** — the >100 MW load-integration process (study + queue), a commercial veto on timeline, not a hard no. **Notably absent:** no county zoning board veto (Wild West), no referendum (NC), no ETJ (towns exercise zero), defanged SEPA — the *governance* is genuinely the best of the three; the vetoes are all **water + environmental-permit + grid**, not political.
- **Best entitlement path + veto stack (if advanced):** **county entitlement + statutory vested plan** (GS 160D-108.1 — NC's best-in-pilot early vesting; *not* incorporation, which is a legislative act in NC). The durable instrument exists and is strong; the veto stack that survives it is **DWR water permit (CCPCUA + Neuse + IBT) → USACE §404/EIS on jurisdictional fringes → grid-integration queue.** Governance is the asset; physical supply + permits are the residual binds.
- **Option/LOI posture:** **none at full scale.** The rational *contingent* move (if the principal's real build-out is small, ≤~15k ac) is a **two-part diligence spend before any option: (a) an AOI-specific dry-upland + post-Sackett jurisdictional-delineation study** to prove a buildable footprint exists, and **(b) an NRWASA/DWR conversation** to confirm uncommitted Neuse surplus + willingness to wholesale out-of-area. Optioning land before (a) and (b) would be premature. **Recommendation: do not advance Jones to Commit at 50k; if the spec shrinks, run the (a)+(b) diligence pair first — Jones is the strongest "re-open if the project is smaller" candidate of the two non-survivors.**
