## Verdict

**South Dakota screens structurally WEAKER than Wyoming on the two variables that matter most** — and the gap is the whole point of running the screen. SD has a **strong, constitutionally and statutorily mandated initiative & referendum tradition that reaches county and municipal land-use legislation** (a "135-year tradition of local control"; SDCL Title 9 ch. 20 mandates I&R for *all* cities and counties). That means a county land-use approval here is plausibly **referendable** — **the California failure mode that WY structurally lacks is present in SD.** SD also **has a little-NEPA (SDEPA, SDCL 34A-9)** that attaches whenever a project needs a state permit/license — a real environmental-review channel, though enforcement appears **agency-driven** (compliance orders/injunctions by DANR) rather than a CEQA-style *private* citizen-suit, which makes it less dangerous than California's. The **decisive conjunction** ([framework/02](../../framework/02-state-screen.md#the-decisive-conjunction)) — private-enforceable mini-NEPA **+** referendum reach — is **partially present**: referendum reach is real; the mini-NEPA's private-suit breadth is the open question that determines how close SD sits to the California trap. Water is **prior appropriation** (same Western kill-first layer as WY). **Provisional posture: PASS to county stage (Fall River) but flagged as a structurally riskier durability environment than WY — the referendum-reach variable is the thing to confirm and, if real, the durability instrument must be engineered to escape the ballot (quasi-judicial entitlement, or incorporation where the project controls the electorate).** This is exactly the kind of state-level differentiation the funnel exists to surface before county spend.

## The four variables

### 1. Vesting timing
- **Rule:** Likely **common-law reliance** (majority rule); **no Texas-style early-vesting statute located** for SD. SD water rights have their own statutory vesting (permit priority by application date — see Water) but that's the *water* right, not the *land-use* entitlement. 🔍 *Identify the controlling SD Supreme Court vested-rights/zoning-estoppel case (candidate: review SD zoning opinions, e.g., the line including* Bechen *and county-zoning cases) and quote its trigger.*
- **Spend stage durability is purchasable:** **late** (reliance rule). Same mitigation question as WY — can a contractual instrument advance it? (→ breach test).

### 2. Referendum reach over the durability instrument
- **Do initiative/referendum extend to local land-use legislative acts? ❗ YES — this is SD's defining risk.** SDCL **Title 9 ch. 20** mandates initiative & referendum for municipal ordinances (referendum petition = **5% of registered voters**), and SD's tradition extends I&R to **county** government; the constitution restricts the legislature from special laws overriding local control. Land-use rezonings/ordinances are within reach. The 2024 **SB 201 / CO2-pipeline** fight (referred to a statewide vote and rejected) is live evidence that SD land-use/siting measures get put to the ballot. 🔍 *Confirm county-level referendum applies specifically to a county zoning/PUD approval and whether such approvals are legislative (referendable) vs. quasi-judicial.*
- **Is a development agreement legislative or contractual?** Decisive here, unlike WY — if a DA/rezoning is a legislative act, **it is referendable.** ❓ for counsel.
- **Citations:** S.D. Const. art. III §1 (I&R) + art. IX (local government) 🔍; SDCL 9-20 ✅ (mandated municipal I&R, 5% threshold — secondary-confirmed, 🔍 official read); Ballotpedia *Laws governing local ballot measures in SD*; SB 201 referendum (2024) ✅.

### 3. Mini-NEPA
- **Exists? YES — SDEPA, SDCL 34A-9** (enacted 1974, amended through 2005). **Scope:** triggers on *state agency action* — a project "directly undertaken" by a public agency, publicly funded, **or requiring the issuance of a lease/permit/license/certificate to a developer.** So a private new-city project that needs *any* state permit can pull SDEPA review. ✅ (scope per SDCL 34A-9 / DANR; 🔍 official section read).
- **Private right of action?** **Appears NOT a CEQA-style private citizen suit** — enforcement reads as **agency-driven** (DANR compliance orders, civil/criminal penalties, injunctions *by the agency*). That makes SDEPA a real review channel but a weaker *opponent* veto than CEQA. ❓ **counsel: confirm whether a third party can sue to compel/enjoin under SDEPA, and the standard of review** — this single answer sets how close SD is to the California trap.
- **Citations:** SDCL **34A-9** ✅ (existence + scope, secondary; 🔍 official); enforcement structure 🔍.

### 4. Boundary gatekeeper
- **Boundary commission?** 🔍 No LAFCO-style commission found; annexation/incorporation run through municipal/county processes + (incorporation) the county commission ordering an election. Confirm.
- **Annexation mechanism:** SDCL Title 9 (municipal annexation) — 🔍 confirm by-resolution vs. petition vs. election and any county role.
- **Incorporation requirements:** **SDCL ch. 9-3** ✅ (structure confirmed via official TOC): **minimum population** (§ 9-3-1), **minimum distance from an existing municipality** (§ 9-3-1.1), survey/boundary map + census, **voters' petition** (§ 9-3-5), **county commissioners' order for election** (§ 9-3-6), **majority vote to incorporate** (§ 9-3-10). Structurally parallel to WY (commission + local vote, not legislature — favorable). 🔍 *read § 9-3-1 for the population number and § 9-3-1.1 for the distance.*
- **ETJ regime:** SD municipalities have platting jurisdiction within a set distance of city limits — 🔍 confirm the extraterritorial-plat distance (analog to WY's one-mile bar).

## Water doctrine

- **Doctrine:** **Prior appropriation** — "first in time, first in right," priority by application date; a permit to appropriate is required for all uses except certain domestic use (SDCL Title 46). Construction within 5 years + 4 to beneficial use. **Western kill-first layer** ([heuristic 1](../../framework/01-heuristics.md)).
- **Basin/authority:** **DANR Water Rights Program** / Chief Engineer / Water Management Board. Fall River County (SW corner, Black Hills flank) implicates the **Cheyenne River basin** and **Madison/Minnelusa aquifers** (Black Hills hydrogeology). 🔍 *At county stage: DANR water-rights search + permit/protest history for the candidate basin/aquifer; confirm whether the relevant source is fully appropriated.*

## Available entitlement paths and veto stacks

| Path | Viable? | Veto stack |
|---|---|---|
| County entitlement | **Yes (default)** but **referendum-exposed** | County commission → **possible county referendum (SD risk)** → SDEPA review if a state permit is needed → DANR water → litigation. *Weaker durability stack than WY because of the ballot channel.* |
| Annexation | **Yes** | Municipal council → possible municipal referendum 🔍 → litigation. |
| Incorporation | **Plausible** — SDCL 9-3 (pop min + distance min + petition + county-ordered election) | Meet thresholds → county commission orders election → majority vote → (distance bar from existing municipality). **Once incorporated, the project controls a local electorate — the cleanest escape from hostile county referenda.** Slow; durability arrives late but maximal. |
| Special district | **Yes** | Formation by petition/county; ongoing district law. |
| Buy already-entitled | **🔍 none logged** | Standing Path-0 track; check stalled vested subdivisions in SW SD. |

## First legally operative act / full durable package

- **Cheapest legally operative first step:** county rezoning/PUD — **but in SD this is the *referendable* step**, so durability is weaker than the WY equivalent until a vested right attaches or the project incorporates. The durable core is again the **vested right** (likely beyond ballot reach) and, for maximal durability, **incorporation** (project controls the electorate).
- **Durable package:** 🔍 recorded plat/PUD past the **referendum window** + vested-rights reliance + secured DANR water permit + (if pursued) incorporation election won.

## Open questions for counsel (priority order)

1. **Referendum reach (decisive, SD's defining risk):** Does the county initiative/referendum power reach a county zoning/PUD/DA approval, and are such approvals legislative (referendable) or quasi-judicial? What is the referendum-petition window and threshold at the county level?
2. **SDEPA private suit:** Can a third-party opponent sue to compel/enjoin under SDCL 34A-9, or is enforcement agency-only? (Sets how close SD sits to the CA decisive conjunction.)
3. **Vesting:** controlling SD vested-rights/zoning-estoppel case + trigger; is a vested right beyond referendum reach (the escape path)?
4. **Breach/remedy:** DA enforceability vs. police-power limit *and* the ballot-revocation path.
5. **Boundary/incorporation specifics:** § 9-3-1 population minimum, § 9-3-1.1 distance-from-existing-municipality, ETJ plat distance.
