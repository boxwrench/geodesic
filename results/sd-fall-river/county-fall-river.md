## Verdict

**Stage 3 (Validate) verdict (🔍 2026-06-13) — the kill is now confirmed and *located*: water.** The 4-agent Validate fan-out (full detail in [Stage 3 below](#stage-3--validate-1wk-budget--agent-gathered-2026-06-13-mode-b-draft)) reorders Stage-1's worries. The two axes that *looked* hard at Stage 1 — assembly and transmission — are the **least** binding (a single-seller ~10,323-ac ranch exists; Hot Springs is itself an in-town 230 kV node). The genuine wall is **water: a hard FAIL at build-out** — the entire county pumps ~0.80 MGD today, there is no firm source for 6-9 MGD, and a *directly analogous* 8,500-gpm withdrawal (Powertech, same aquifer) was **unanimously denied by the SD Water Management Board in 2021** — compounded by **absorption** (labor import-only; ~0% rental vacancy → self-build everything). Karst is a real cost that pushes siting *away* from the water+power corridor. **Unlike Sweetwater, Fall River has no validated path to firm water at 50k scale** — so its 🔴 is a *genuine* kill, not merely an absorption-deferred "enter small." The verdict softens to "concern" only if the principal's real build-out is dramatically smaller (low single-digit thousands). Recorded as data per the pilot rule; [kill-override](../../framework/03-county-screen.md#kill-override--a-kill-is-a-hypothesis-not-a-verdict) path documented in Stage 4.

**Stage 1 (2026-06-13, draft; pre-pilot = all signals run, kills recorded as data):** Fall River is **🟡→🔴-leaning, dominated by a single would-kill: scale.** The county's total population is **~7,453** (Hot Springs ~3,395) — a 50k-person full build-out is **~7x the entire county**, blowing through the [scale-threshold heuristic](../../framework/01-heuristics.md) (flag if entry pop ÷ county pop > 0.5; kill-review if > 1.0). Even the *entry* phase (2-5k) approaches the whole county. This is the headline finding the principal needs. Layered on top: **land assembly is hard** (the **Buffalo Gap National Grassland**, ~117k ac of USFS land in-county, is "intermingled with private/state/tribal lands… not a contiguous block" — the same Western federal-interspersion assembly problem as Sweetwater, different mechanism → confirms [lesson L8](../../research/pilot-lessons.md)); **water durability is a real concern** (Hot Springs depends on the **Madison aquifer**, a confined Black Hills aquifer with *declining levels*, under prior-appropriation); and the **Pine Ridge reservation** abuts the area (proximate tribal/NEPA sensitivity, not an in-county overlay kill). **Net for the principal:** Fall River is the weakest of the three pilot candidates so far — water-state durability risk (SD referendum reach, [state screen](state-sd.md)) *plus* a hard scale-threshold *plus* assembly friction. Recorded, not eliminated (pilot rule); the comparative data is the value. **Best use:** Fall River is the calibration *contrast* — it shows the scale-threshold and assembly signals discriminating hard, which is exactly what Step 2 needs.

## Stage 3 — Validate (1wk budget; 🔍 agent-gathered 2026-06-13, Mode-B DRAFT)

> Screened against the **placeholder spec** ([framework/06](../../framework/06-project-spec.md)); findings flag where the answer flips on the principal's real numbers. 4-agent fan-out. Caches: [water](../../research/raw/fall-river/2026-06-13-stage3-water-economics.md) · [transmission](../../research/raw/fall-river/2026-06-13-stage3-transmission.md) · [land+labor](../../research/raw/fall-river/2026-06-13-stage3-land-labor.md) · [geotech](../../research/raw/fall-river/2026-06-13-stage3-geotech.md). **Pre-pilot rule: this is run as data even though Stage 1/2 already lean 🔴 — Validate confirms the kill and shows *which* layer binds.**

- **Water source / pipeline economics:** 🔍 **FAILS at build-out; CONCERN at entry — the hardest "no" on this site.** Scale shock: the *entire current public water supply for all of Fall River County* is **~0.80 MGD** (Hot Springs ~0.81 MGD); the project's *entry* load (0.5-0.8 MGD) alone equals everything the county pumps today, and build-out (6-9 MGD) is ~10-12× it. **No firm source at scale:** local supply is the confined **Madison** karst aquifer (declining — USGS SIR 2025-5067 finds Black Hills withdrawals already exceed recharge in the most-populated subareas; whether the *southern/Fall River* subarea has headroom is the pivotal unresolved fact, buried in a 27 MB PDF — human gap), plus **Inyan Kara** (gross-alpha/radium treatment needed) and **Minnelusa** (arsenic/iron). **Killer precedent:** Powertech's **8,500 gpm (~12 MGD)** Inyan Kara request — same aquifer, same magnitude as build-out — was paused (2013) and **unanimously blocked by the SD Water Management Board (Oct 2021)** amid mass opposition. Surface alternatives weak: Cheyenne River intermittent/drought-sensitive; **Angostura** is Reclamation *irrigation* water (junior holders shut off in 2008) — ag-to-municipal conversion is legally + federally heavy. The regional fix is a **$2-4B Missouri River pipeline (WDRWS)** that is 20+ yrs out and may not reach Fall River. Durability poor (junior priority, declining aquifer, unresolved 1868-Treaty / Great Sioux Nation reserved-water claims, 40-yr battle-hardened opposition). **Conveyance is NOT the binding constraint** (local wells/Angostura <10 mi). **Flip:** breaks somewhere in the **~1.5-3 MGD band** — well below build-out; *phasing does not rescue it* (the terminal load has no home).
- **Transmission tie-in path:** 🔍 **PASS at entry; CONCERN at build-out — better than Stage-1 feared, not a kill.** Hot Springs is itself a named **230 kV substation node** on Black Hills Energy's backbone (Wyodak-Hot Springs + Teckla-Osage-Rapid City 230 kV corridors); the ≥115 kV node is **in-town (~0 mi)**, far inside the ~10-15 mi screen. The new $350M **Ready Wyoming** line (energized Dec 2025) ties BHE's WY+SD grids, adding import capability. 100-200 MW build-out is physically plausible — BHE is *actively soliciting* large loads (>3 GW pipeline, dedicated data-center unit) — but the constraint is **local 230→69 kV step-down capacity + SPP queue** (not public) and a likely customer-funded substation + **BYO-generation** (paralleling Sweetwater; SD PUC siting permit triggers at 50 MW of *self-built generation*, not at load). **Rail is the soft spot** (Edgemont BNSF yard being closed/bypassed 2025); highways ✅ (US-18/385, no Interstate); broadband ✅; industrial-scale gas unconfirmed.
- **Geological constraints:** 🔍 **CONCERN / COST (not a kill) — but it pushes siting away from water+power.** **Gypsum karst is the defining hazard and it's a formation-belt condition, not point-source:** deep anhydrite/gypsum dissolution (Minnelusa Fm) drives breccia-pipe cover-collapse sinkholes; shallower Spearfish "Red Valley" gypsum rings the whole southern Black Hills flank; **Hot Springs sits on six Spearfish terraces and the Mammoth Site is a collapse sinkhole *inside city limits.*** Real cost signal: Hideaway Hills (Rapid City area) — 85-ft gypsum sinkhole, ~158 homes, ~$45M suit. Soils: Spearfish collapsible/sulfate-corrosive; Cretaceous shales expansive — routine-but-real foundation cost. **Seismicity LOW (SDC A/B).** Radionuclides (Inyan Kara uranium, Radon Zone 2) manageable with code. A buildable bench plausibly exists **but probably not under Hot Springs proper** — you push outboard onto the SE plains / Edgemont side off the evaporite belt, trading karst for expansive clay *and adding distance from the Hot Springs water + 230 kV corridor.*
- **Labor pool within 60 min (absorption — [L16](../../research/pilot-lessons.md)):** 🔍 **CONCERN at entry; FAILS at build-out — import-only, with a housing wall worse than the labor wall.** Fall River's entire labor force is **~3,196** (~125 unemployed). The only real pool is the **Rapid City MSA**, whose *entire* mining/logging/construction sector is **~6-6.35k at ~2% unemployment (fully employed)**, **~57 mi / ~62 min** from Hot Springs (Edgemont ~75-90 min). **Spec's ≥10,000 construction-relevant workers within 60 min is NOT met** — import-only, bidding against Rapid City's own boom + the 2026 national construction shortage. **Housing bootstrap is the hardest wall:** county ~4,101 units, ~2% rental vacancy (Hot Springs ~0%); the hospital spent ~$2.3M for *just 48 units* — there is no boomtown overflow, so the project must self-build a man-camp + essentially all ~20,000 DU. **Land assembly, by contrast, is the *least* binding axis:** a single-seller ~10,323-ac contiguous deeded block ("Open Range Ranch," Edgemont side) exists at ~2× the entry threshold; SW-SD pasture ~$1,080/ac → entry ~$5-7.5M, 50k-ac ~$50-75M (trivial vs. water). But the clean private block is **Edgemont-side (worse for labor/services)** while labor/services are **Hot Springs-side (worse for clean private land + karst)** — land and absorption pull siting in opposite directions.

**Stage 3 net (🔍):** Fall River **confirms its 🔴 as data** — and Validate shows the binding layer is **water (hard FAIL at build-out, no firm source + a directly-on-point blocked precedent) compounded by absorption (labor import-only + a near-zero housing market).** Transmission and land assembly — the two axes that looked hard at Stage 1 — turn out to be the *least* binding (in-town 230 kV; a single-seller 10k-ac ranch). This is the L16 pattern again: entitlement/topology was never the real wall; **physical supply + regional absorption at 50k scale are.** Unlike Sweetwater, Fall River has **no validated path to firm water at build-out** — the kill is genuine, not just absorption-deferred. *Entry phase (~0.5-0.8 MGD, a few hundred units) is conceivable on a small wellfield + man-camp; the 50k city is not.* Spec-dependence: the verdict flips to "concern, not fail" only if the principal's real build-out is **dramatically smaller** (low single-digit thousands) — at anything near 50k, water alone kills it.

## Stage 4 — Commit (1mo budget) — *brief documented rationale; Commit NOT warranted (see [scorecard](../../results/scorecard.md))*

> Per the pre-pilot rule a 🔴 is recorded, not acted on, and the [kill-override](../../framework/03-county-screen.md#kill-override--a-kill-is-a-hypothesis-not-a-verdict) remains open. This section documents the veto stack that *would* apply **if** a human overrode the Stage-3 water kill — so the override decision is informed — rather than a full Commit work-up, which the candidate does not warrant.

- **Veto player map (if overridden):** (1) **SD Water Management Board** — the *de facto* lead veto; it unanimously denied the directly-analogous Powertech withdrawal, and any 6-9 MGD appropriation routes through it. (2) **The tribal/enviro opposition apparatus** — Oglala Sioux Tribe (federal-win record), Black Hills Clean Water Alliance (40-yr lineage), NDN Collective, Great Plains Tribal Water Alliance — mature, networked, litigation-ready, with a proven water/aquifer + treaty/sacred-site playbook (the strongest opposition in the pilot, invisible to the meeting-grade). (3) **SD county initiative & referendum** — Fall River voters *passed* a uranium public-nuisance measure (Nov 2022, 56%); though state-preempted on minerals, the ballot is a live tool against a controversial new city. (4) **USFS** (Buffalo Gap exchange/NEPA) + **Reclamation** (any Angostura conversion). (5) **Bureau of Reclamation / WDRWS** as the only at-scale water gatekeeper.
- **Best entitlement path + veto stack (if overridden):** county-level entitlement is *easy* (Wild West, no zoning) — but that is the trap ([L15](../../research/pilot-lessons.md)): the binding stack is **water-right (SD WMB) → tribal/treaty + NEPA litigation → ballot risk**, none of which the friendly county controls. There is no durable instrument that neutralizes the water veto.
- **Option/LOI posture:** **none recommended.** A land option on the Open Range Ranch is cheap and would secure the *one* easy axis, but optioning land while the *binding* constraint (firm water at scale) has no identified path would be spending on the wrong gate. **Recommendation: do not advance Fall River to Commit; hold as a kill with the override path documented above.**
