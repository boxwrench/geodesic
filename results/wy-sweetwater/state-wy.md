## Verdict

Wyoming screens as a **structurally strong** state on three of the four cheap variables: there is **no county-level initiative or referendum** (voters cannot strike a county land-use approval at the ballot — the California Forever failure mode is absent), **no comprehensive state "little NEPA"** giving private opponents a project-level litigation veto, and **no state boundary commission / LAFCO** — annexation and incorporation run through ordinary municipal and district-court processes. The decisive-conjunction kill (private mini-NEPA + referendum reach) that defines California is **structurally absent here**. The unresolved variable is **vesting timing**: Wyoming appears to follow the **majority common-law reliance rule** (rights vest late, after permit + substantial construction spend) with no Texas-style early-vesting statute found — meaning durability is purchasable only at a *late* spend stage unless a contractual instrument (development agreement / annexation agreement) is engineered to vest earlier. The genuinely open question, and the one that governs the whole verdict, is the **breach test**: whether a Wyoming development agreement or annexation agreement yields *specific performance / injunction* or only *damages* on revocation. That is unresolved in the sources read and is the first question for counsel. **Provisional posture: PASS to county stage (Sweetwater) with vesting + breach-test flagged as the durability risk to close.** Wyoming's dominant structural constraint is water-doctrine (prior appropriation), handled at the county/site layer per [heuristic 1](../../framework/01-heuristics.md).

## The four variables

### 1. Vesting timing
- **Rule (early-statutory / reliance):** Appears to be the **majority common-law reliance rule** (vesting on lawful permit issuance + substantial good-faith reliance/expenditure). **No Texas Ch. 245-style statutory permit-vesting / "freeze at application" statute was located** for Wyoming. 🔍 *Confirm there is no vesting statute; identify the controlling Wyoming Supreme Court vested-rights case and quote its trigger.*
- **Statute / leading case:** County zoning enabling authority is **Wyo. Stat. § 18-5-201 et seq.** (county planning & zoning over unincorporated areas) 🔍; municipal zoning under **Title 15** 🔍. Controlling vested-rights *case* not yet identified — **open item for counsel / case search.**
- **Spend stage at which durability is purchasable:** Under a reliance rule, **late** (post-permit, post-construction — the ~$40M stage in [framework/02](../../framework/02-state-screen.md)). **Mitigation to test:** can a development agreement or annexation agreement contractually advance the vesting point to ~application stage? (→ breach test below.)

### 2. Referendum reach over the durability instrument
- **Do initiative/referendum extend to local land-use legislative acts?** **No — does NOT reach counties.** ✅ **Wyo. Const. art. 3, §52(g)** expressly bars *both* the initiative and the referendum from being used to "enact local or special legislation" (verified against FindLaw mirror of art. 3 §52 + Wyoming SOS materials; 🔍 final read of the official `wyoleg.gov` constitution text still advised). Per Ballotpedia **no Wyoming local government has an initiative/referendum process**; the constitution does not establish county-level I&R. **This is the single strongest durability asset in the screen.**
- **Is a development agreement legislative (referendum-exposed) or contractual/quasi-judicial?** Largely moot for the *ballot* channel if there is no county I&R at all — **this is the key durability asset.** The DA's character still matters for the *breach test* (contract vs. legislative act), addressed below.
- **Citations:** Wyo. Const. art. 3 (I&R); Wyoming SOS I&R Summary; Ballotpedia "Laws governing local ballot measures in Wyoming." 🔍

### 3. Mini-NEPA
- **Exists? Private right of action? Scope:** **No comprehensive Wyoming Environmental Policy Act applying to private projects was found.** Wyoming environmental regulation runs through the **DEQ** (media-specific permits: air, water quality, land quality/mining) and through **federal NEPA** where a federal handle exists — but there is **no state analog to CEQA** giving private opponents a general project-level environmental-review lawsuit. This removes an entire category of veto holder for $0. 🔍 *Confirm no WEPA-type statute; confirm DEQ permit appeals are limited to the Environmental Quality Council / permit-specific standing, not a general citizen project-review suit.*
- **Citations:** Wyo. Stat. Title 35, ch. 11 (Environmental Quality Act — note: this is *pollution-permitting*, not a NEPA-style review act) 🔍; absence of a "little NEPA" cross-checked against academic little-NEPA surveys. **Federal NEPA exposure is handled separately in [framework/07](../../framework/07-federal-layer.md).**

### 4. Boundary gatekeeper
- **Boundary commission?** ✅ **No LAFCO-equivalent state boundary commission** — annexation runs through the municipal governing body and incorporation through the county commissioners + a local election (below); neither routes through a separate state agency veto. (Read across Title 15 ch. 1 art. 2 & 4 — [cached extract](../../research/raw/2026-06-13-wy-title15-cities-towns-official-extract.md).)
- **Annexation mechanism:** ✅ **Wyo. Stat. § 15-1-401 et seq.** (official text read). Before annexation the governing body holds a § 15-1-405 hearing and must find, per **§ 15-1-402(a)**, that it is *"for the protection of the health, safety and welfare,"* that the area *"would constitute a natural, geographical, economical and social part of the annexing city or town,"* and that it is a *"logical and feasible addition."* No boundary commission in the chain. 🔍 *one sub-question left: can the annexing-side electorate force a referendum on the annexation ordinance? — for counsel.*
- **Incorporation requirements:** ✅ **Wyo. Stat. § 15-1-201 et seq.** (official text read): not already incorporated, **pop ≥ 200**, **density ≥ 70/sq mi** (§ 15-1-201); surveyor map + census filed with the **county clerk**, 20-day public examination (§ 15-1-202/-203). **Correction from earlier draft:** the petition goes to the **board of county commissioners**, *not* the district court — the board hears interested parties and, if satisfied, appoints inspectors who **call an election of resident electors** ("for incorporation"/"against incorporation") (§ 15-1-204/-205). So incorporation = commissioners + local vote, **no legislative-approval requirement** (favorable vs. NC).
- **ETJ regime / one-mile rule:** ✅ **§ 15-1-411** — *"All territory within one (1) mile of an incorporated city or town... is potentially an urban area. No territory within a potential urban area may be incorporated... unless the governing body of the city or town"* consents. A candidate **incorporation** site within one mile of Green River or Rock Springs is blockable by that town; map candidate parcels against the one-mile rings at county stage. 🔍 *municipal subdivision-platting ETJ reach (§ 15-1-501 et seq.) still to read.*

## Breach test, per instrument

> **This table is the heart of the durability question.** Cells now carry a **drafted position** (preloaded for counsel to confirm/correct) plus the open edge. Drafts are tagged ❓ — they are not yet ✅ findings, but counsel reviews a starting hypothesis, not a blank. The remedy question is: on revocation, *injunction/specific performance* (durable) or *damages only* (removable-but-compensated)?

**Controlling legal frame (preloaded):** Wyoming has **no located dedicated development-agreement enabling statute** (unlike states with a DA act). So a county "development agreement" lives under the general common-law **contract-zoning vs. conditional-zoning** doctrine, governed by the rule that a government **cannot contract away / bargain away its police power** — a legislative body cannot bind successors not to exercise the zoning power. Wyoming courts already police the *outer bounds* of county zoning authority (a county land-use regulation was struck for regulating *ownership* rather than *use* as exceeding the § 18-5-201 grant — 🔍 confirm the Teton County citation). The durable instrument is therefore more likely the **vested right / recorded plat** (a property interest, enjoinable) than the **executory promise** in a DA (which a successor board may argue it cannot be bound by). This is the hypothesis for counsel.

## Water doctrine

- **Doctrine:** **Prior appropriation** ("first in time, first in right"); beneficial use is the basis, measure, and limit of the right. This is the **kill-first layer for the West** ([heuristic 1](../../framework/01-heuristics.md)) and the single most likely structural kill at the Sweetwater county/site stage.
- **Basin/permit authority and database:** **Wyoming State Engineer's Office (SEO)** is chief water administrator; the **Board of Control** (State Engineer + 4 division superintendents) adjudicates rights. Sweetwater County sits largely in the **Green River Basin** (Water Division IV) plus Great Divide/closed-basin and Platte-headwaters fringes. Use the **SEO e-Permit / water-rights search** ([registry](../../ops/data-sources.md#water-state-specific)). 🔍 *At county stage: pull the basin appropriation status and the State Engineer protest log for municipal applications in the candidate basin — protest frequency is the [stealth-diligence](../../framework/03-county-screen.md#stealth-diligence-asymmetric-information) signal.*

## First legally operative act / full durable package

- **Cheapest legally operative first step in this state:** Likely a **county PUD/zoning approval** on unincorporated land — *but the DA layered on top is the weak durability link* per the breach-test draft. The durable core is more likely the **vested right** (recorded plat + permit + substantial reliance) than the DA's executory promise, because the police-power doctrine lets a successor board re-regulate. **If maximal durability is required, the path is incorporation** (commissioners + a vote you control once residents exist) **or an annexation agreement** that embeds entitlements as contract — both confirmed procedurally, both with the same future-legislation caveat for counsel.
- **The "recorded and challenge-windows-expired" durable package looks like:** 🔍 to be defined once the breach test is answered — provisionally: recorded PUD/plat + executed development or annexation agreement with vested-rights language + secured water permit/appropriation, past all administrative-appeal windows.
