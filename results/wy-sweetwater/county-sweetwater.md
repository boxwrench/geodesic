## Verdict

**Stage 1 — water (2026-06-13, draft):** Sweetwater is **🟡 site-dependent on water — the kill is sub-basin-specific, not county-wide.** Per [heuristic 1](../../framework/01-heuristics.md) water is the West's kill-first layer, and Sweetwater straddles two very different regimes: (a) the **Great Divide Basin** — a closed/endorheic basin (<10" precip, no outlet) covering much of central county, where a candidate site is a **Stage 1 water KILL** per the "closed basins are absolute kills" rule; and (b) the **Green River Basin** (SW county, Rock Springs/Green River) — water physically present but it is **junior Colorado River Compact water under active curtailment pressure** (priority calls were used in the basin in 2020-2022; 2026 state legislation aimed at protecting junior municipal/industrial users), a **durability risk on the water right itself**, not just supply. **Not killed county-wide** because (i) groundwater is unscreened and could rescue either area, and (ii) the project water demand is still placeholder ([framework/06](../../framework/06-project-spec.md)) — at ~7-10k ac-ft/yr full build-out the compact-curtailment exposure is material and must be sized. **Next:** SEO e-Permit basin pull + protest log per candidate AOI; confirm which sub-basin the actual candidate parcels fall in (that single fact flips kill vs. concern). Topology/best-path: deferred to Stage 2. Source detail cached: [research/raw/2026-06-13-sweetwater-water-stage1.md](../../research/raw/2026-06-13-sweetwater-water-stage1.md).

**Stage 1 — full picture (all signals run; pre-pilot = record, don't eliminate):** The two real would-kill threads are **water (sub-basin)** and **land assembly**, both **override-eligible**. Attractors: **transmission strong** (Jim Bridger / Aeolus-Anticline / Gateway 500kV corridor; headroom unverified), no tribal/military kill, and — newly clear — **local politics are pro-growth, not the constraint**.

**The reframe from the agent fan-out (the important update):** Sweetwater's binding constraint is **federal land management, not local opposition.** Three threads converge: (1) the **BLM Rock Springs RMP** put ~1.8M ac (half the planning area) under ACEC designation — *the county itself contemplated suing BLM to stop it*; (2) **sage-grouse Core Areas** blanket much of the basin (5% disturbance cap, lek buffers — policy, not ESA); (3) the **checkerboard** means the only large private block runs through **Orion/Sweetwater Royalties** (ex-Occidental, ~690k ac), acquirable by *negotiated purchase*, not assembly. So the entitlement story is unusually inverted: the *county* wants the project (single-industry, salvation reflex; pro-growth board, ~96% effective P&Z approval) — **the gatekeepers are the BLM, the sage-grouse EO, and a single private landholder.**

**Net for the principal:** Sweetwater remains **🟡 plausible-but-constrained**, but the constraint set is now precise and *transactional/federal* rather than political. Source detail: [overlays/ownership](../../research/raw/2026-06-13-sweetwater-stage1-overlays-ownership.md) · [water](../../research/raw/sweetwater/2026-06-13-water-seo-pull.md) · [transmission/ownership](../../research/raw/sweetwater/2026-06-13-transmission-ownership-pull.md) · [stealth](../../research/raw/sweetwater/2026-06-13-stealth-diligence-pull.md) · [federal](../../research/raw/sweetwater/2026-06-13-federal-layer-pull.md).

**Stage 3 — Validate (🔍 2026-06-13) reorders the constraints.** Two findings change the picture: **water is validated *obtainable at scale*** (a 14,500 ac-ft/yr Green River permit — larger than full build-out — was just granted in-county; the residual issue is compact-curtailment *durability*, addressable via Fontenelle M&I storage), and **geotech is a cost, not a kill** (subsidence avoidable by siting east of Green River; low seismicity). But two *new* binding constraints surface that Stages 1-2 didn't: **(A) labor/housing — the spec FAILS locally** (county labor force ~19,700, <1,000 available, region already short >6,000 construction workers for TerraPower/trona/CO2; import-only in a housing-constrained window), and **(B) a 200 MW transmission cliff** (≥200 MW load → WY PSC Special Contract + likely bring-your-own-generation, queuing behind multi-GW data-center demand on an export corridor). Plus the **Orion land** is mid-merger (Uranium Royalty Corp acquiring ~92% of Sweetwater Royalties, closing ~Q3 2026) — a one-off negotiated surface sale, awkward to transact right now, and the BLM-exchange alternative is 3-7 yrs and entangled in the held Rock Springs RMP. **Revised feasibility read:** the binding constraints are now **labor/housing absorption and power/water *at full build-out scale*, not entitlement** — which sharpens the [scale-threshold](../../framework/01-heuristics.md) point: enter *small* (entry-phase load/labor/water all pass cleanly) and the project is viable; attempt 50k-scale fast and it collides with a tight regional labor/power market. **This is exactly the question only the principal's real spec numbers ([framework/06](../../framework/06-project-spec.md)) can resolve.** Stage 3 detail in the section below.

## Stage 3 — Validate (1wk budget; 🔍 agent-gathered 2026-06-13, Mode-B DRAFT)

> Screened against the **placeholder spec** ([framework/06](../../framework/06-project-spec.md)) — findings flag where the answer flips on the principal's real numbers. Caches: [water](../../research/raw/sweetwater/2026-06-13-stage3-water-economics.md) · [transmission](../../research/raw/sweetwater/2026-06-13-stage3-transmission.md) · [land+labor](../../research/raw/sweetwater/2026-06-13-stage3-land-labor.md) · [geotech](../../research/raw/sweetwater/2026-06-13-stage3-geotech.md).

- **Water source / pipeline economics:** 🔍 **VALIDATED obtainable at scale — durability, not availability, is the residual risk.** Decisive datapoint: a brand-new **~14,500 ac-ft/yr Green River industrial water right** was permitted for Pacific Soda/Dry Creek (BLM ROD May 2025) — *larger than the project's full build-out (~7-10k ac-ft/yr), in this county.* So a new appropriation at scale is demonstrably gettable. Catch: it's **junior / Colorado River Compact curtailment-exposed** (the durability problem, again). **Firm-supply path = contract a block of Fontenelle M&I storage** (WY holds ~120k+ ac-ft) or acquire+convert a senior ag right. Groundwater is weak standalone (deep, low-yield, saline → RO desal ~$350-800/ac-ft + uncosted endorheic brine disposal). **Conveyance passes** for a Green River/Rock Springs/I-80 corridor site (≤~15 mi, inside the 20-30 mi threshold); a Great Divide interior site fails. **Flip:** entry (0.5-0.8 MGD) rides spare regional (JPWB) capacity; build-out (6-9 MGD) requires *owning drought-firm supply*.
- **Transmission tie-in path:** 🔍 **proximity real, headroom study-gated, and a hard regulatory cliff at 200 MW.** The Jim Bridger/Anticline 230-500kV lines are an **export corridor** (built to push ~1,500 MW of wind out), not idle import capacity, and a ~100-200 MW load queues behind multi-GW WY data-center demand (Crusoe/Tallgrass, Prometheus). Coal transition is **neutral-to-negative** for *load* headroom (frees injection, not load; CCS adds parasitic load). **Flip (RMP's own thresholds):** entry ~10 MW = ordinary connection (trivial); 25-199 MW = Large Service Request + $150k study + ~6mo+; **≥200 MW = Wyoming PSC Special Contract + full project-cost security + likely must bring/contract its own generation.** Only a paid PacifiCorp System Impact Study resolves true headroom.
- **Geological constraints:** 🔍 **COST, not a kill** (trending non-issue on the corridor). Trona room-and-pillar **subsidence is concentrated WEST of Green River** (1995 Solvay M5.1 collapse) — a *don't-build-over-the-panels* siting problem; ISR uranium has no voids. **Seismicity LOW** (routine IBC design). Soils need engineered foundations/corrosion protection (expansive Wasatch clay, saline/sodic — worst in the GDB interior), and centralized sewer. Buildable benches plentiful on the I-80 corridor; dunes/badlands localized + avoidable. (SSURGO needs site coordinates — human gap.)
- **Labor pool within 60 min:** 🔍 **FAILS the spec — import-only, and the region is already labor/housing-constrained.** Sweetwater civilian labor force **~19,700** (Apr 2026); ~4.3% unemployment → **<1,000 actively seeking** countywide; construction *lost* 300+ jobs in 2024. The region already needs **>6,000 temp construction workers over 5 yrs** for TerraPower Natrium ($4B), trona expansions, and 2 CO2 projects — developers say outright "we don't have the population." A new city competes for the same national traveling crews **and** scarce housing in the same window. **vs spec ≥10,000 construction-relevant within 60 min → not met locally; must be imported with housing built for them.**

## Stage 4 — Commit (1wk→1mo budget; 🔍 synthesis 2026-06-13, Mode-B DRAFT)

> Sweetwater is the **only pilot county that reaches Stage 4 on its merits** — entitlement-feasible, absorption-gated at full scale (→ "enter small"). This Commit pass maps the veto players, names the best entitlement path + the veto stack that survives it, and sets an option/LOI posture. **Best entitlement path + veto stack:** **County development agreement + a vested development plan**, entitled through the (friendly) County Commission — **not incorporation** (WY incorporation runs through the county commissioners + a *resident election* per Title 15 § 15-1-204/-205, and the § 15-1-411 one-mile bar near Rock Springs/Green River complicates a townsite; ✅ per the [state screen](state-wy.md)). The **durable instrument** is the vested right (drafted-strong for counsel); the residual durability question is the **breach-test remedy on the DA's future-legislation promise** (weak under the police-power "can't bargain away" doctrine — a preloaded counsel question, [state screen](state-wy.md)). **The veto stack that survives a friendly county yes is therefore entirely transactional/federal:** `secure surface from URC → keep BLM off-path (buy, don't exchange) → SEO firm-water (Fontenelle) + §7 depletion → PSC/PacifiCorp at scale.` Local political risk is the *smallest* item in the stack — the L13 inversion, fully realized.

**Option / LOI posture (the one county where positioning is warranted):**

- **Water — act first, it's the long pole on durability.** Open a **Reclamation UC Region + WWDC** conversation on uncommitted **Fontenelle M&I storage** volume + price; this is the firm-supply linchpin for build-out and is independent of the land timing. *(No option spend; a diligence conversation.)*
- **Land — sequence behind the URC close.** **Do not** attempt a binding surface option before the URC merger closes (~Q3 2026) — the counterparty's decision rights are unsettled. **Now:** engage **Welborn** (knows the asset cold) to test surface-sale appetite and price a defined ~5,000-10,000-ac entry block on the I-80 / Green River corridor (the basin that passes water + conveyance + transmission). **At close:** convert to an LOI / purchase option on the entry block, structured to expand to build-out scale.
- **Grid — buy the study, not the load.** Commission a paid **PacifiCorp System Impact Study** for the entry interconnection with an option to study build-out; this is the only thing that resolves the 200 MW cliff from hypothesis to number.
- **Entitlement — DA term sheet in parallel.** Open the County Commission DA + vested-plan conversation early (cheap, friendly board) but **time the binding vote to after** the land + water + grid diligence so the entitlement attaches to a real site.
- **Net posture:** **"enter small, optioned to scale."** Entry-phase load/labor/water all pass cleanly today; the build-out constraints (firm water, 200 MW, labor/housing absorption) are real but *sequenceable* if the project phases. **This is the only pilot county where an option/LOI spend is justified — and even here it is gated on the principal's real build-out scale ([framework/06](../../framework/06-project-spec.md)): if full build-out is CF-scale (~400k) rather than ~50k, the absorption + power + water binds tighten faster than phasing can absorb, and even Sweetwater's "enter small" runway shortens.**
