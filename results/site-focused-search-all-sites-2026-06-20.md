# Site-Focused Search Addendum Across Pilot Sites

Date: 2026-06-20  
Status: draft; focused-search addendum after remote sync  
Rule for this pass: a kill finding is noted, but the search continues. The purpose is to learn what would have to be true for each site to work, what the new project-specific assumptions change, and which search improvements generalize.

## Search Frame

The new focus changes the questions:

- **Self-built power:** weak utility service is not by itself a blocker, but interconnection, generation siting, fuel, private-wire legality, transmission service, and state siting still matter.
- **Water acquisition:** distinguish no new rights from no acquirable/transferable rights. Buying rights, buying land with rights, storage contracts, wholesale water, and surface-water projects are separate paths.
- **County posture depth:** do not rely on friendliness grade alone. Read planning/zoning bodies, recent approvals/rejections, staff recommendations, ballot history, and repeat opposition.
- **Land assembly:** do not imply no large tracts unless the search method is stated. Public listings are incomplete; parcel/assessor and off-market single-owner blocks matter.
- **Flood/wetlands:** screen early, and separately from legal jurisdiction. A wetland can stop a site even when federal jurisdiction is narrowed.

## Sweetwater WY

### Continue-Past-Kill Finding

No current kill. The earlier site-level concerns are now a parcel-package specification:

- stay on the Green River side rather than the Great Divide Basin interior;
- buy from Sweetwater Royalties / New URC if possible, not through a BLM exchange;
- avoid the worst ACEC / sage-grouse core overlays;
- keep entry load below the Rocky Mountain Power large-load cliff if possible;
- firm water through storage or senior-right acquisition, not only a new junior right.

### Land

The search should be seller-first, not listing-first. The URC/Sweetwater transaction release confirms the land base is a strategic royalty/mineral platform, with major fee-surface acreage and long-term optionality. That makes a surface sale possible but not naturally marketed.

Action: define a 5,000-10,000 acre corridor block and test seller appetite after the URC transaction closes or through deal counsel now.

### Water

This is the cleanest case where the principal's water feedback matters. New Green River water appears obtainable: BLM approved the Dry Creek Trona Mine south of Green River in May 2025, including water pipelines and other major facilities. But new rights are junior; build-out needs drought-firm supply.

Action: pursue Fontenelle/WWDC/Reclamation storage availability and search for senior acquirable rights in parallel.

### Power

Self-built power helps, but the regulatory cliff remains. Rocky Mountain Power's Wyoming large-service process distinguishes 25-199 MW from greater than 200 MW, and greater-than-200 MW requests require a special contract and commission approval.

Action: keep entry under 25 MW if feasible; otherwise run the paid PacifiCorp study before land terms imply build-out service.

### County Posture

County posture is favorable but secondary. The real veto stack is landholder, BLM/RMP/sage-grouse, water firmness, and power.

### Next Search

The next deliverable should be a parcel package map: Sweetwater Royalties fee surface x BLM surface x RMP/ACEC x sage-grouse x Green River conveyance x transmission.

## Fall River SD

### Kill Finding, Not Stopping Point

The build-out water kill remains: at placeholder 50k scale, Fall River has no demonstrated firm water path. But continuing past the kill changes what we learn:

- land assembly is not the limiting issue;
- transmission proximity is better than the first pass implied;
- county-level entitlement is thin/easy because there is no zoning;
- the real vetoes are water, state water approval, tribal/enviro opposition, and absorption.

### Land

The repo's Fall River file identifies a single-seller 10,323-acre ranch as evidence that large private tract assembly is possible. A broader public-market search also found a South Dakota example well above 10,000 acres: Dakota Partnership Ranch, reported as roughly 27,000 acres south of Rapid City near the Black Hills. That does not prove a Fall River site, but it does prove the old "no large sites" implication was too strong.

Action: redo the land search by bounded method: Fall River assessor parcels, ranch brokers, public listing aggregators, and off-market ranch blocks near Edgemont/Hot Springs. Record acres, attached water, mineral severance, federal adjacency, and distance to power/water.

### Water

The principal's water-rights point is crucial here. The question should not be "can Fall River get new water?" alone. It should split:

- new Madison/Inyan Kara groundwater permit;
- acquisition of existing ranch/ag rights;
- land purchase with attached rights;
- transfer/change-of-use to municipal/industrial use;
- storage or Angostura/Reclamation path;
- realistic lower-scale water budget.

At 50k build-out, the answer still appears no because the volume is too large and analogous high-volume withdrawals have drawn direct opposition and denial risk. At a low-thousands project, an acquired-rights path may deserve a real search.

Action: if Fall River remains in play for a smaller spec, search DANR water-right records by ranch/AOI and commission a water-rights attorney screen before any land option.

### Power

Self-built power improves the Fall River read. If the project brings generation, sparse utility headroom matters less. But generation siting, SD PUC jurisdiction, fuel, water for thermal generation, and interconnection still matter.

Action: for any smaller Fall River concept, screen self-built generation sites against water use, visual impact, tribal/cultural sensitivity, and PUC siting requirements.

### County Posture

Fall River's high friendliness score is an artifact of no zoning. The county is not a durable permission machine; it is low-capacity and politically reactive. The 2022 uranium public-nuisance initiative, state-preemption fight, and mature Black Hills water/treaty opposition network matter more than routine commission approvals.

Action: treat Fall River as "easy county, hard external vetoes." Do not assign extra value to no zoning without a state-level exemption or water approval strategy.

### Flood/Wetlands/Geotech

Flood/wetlands are not the headline here, but geotech/karst is an actual siting shaper. Hot Springs-side access to water/power trades against gypsum karst and sinkhole risk; Edgemont-side ranch land improves assembly but worsens water/services/labor.

### Next Search

Fall River should not receive full-scale option spend. It can receive a bounded "small project rescue" search:

1. identify actual 5k-15k acre ranch blocks;
2. pull attached/acquirable water rights;
3. test SD Water Management Board path at low-thousands scale;
4. test self-built power siting;
5. stop only after that narrower rescue case is evaluated.

## Jones NC

### Kill Finding, Not Stopping Point

Jones is no longer cleanly killed by the old wetlands framing. Post-Sackett and NC's refusal to backfill isolated-wetland jurisdiction, some isolated pocosin acreage may be less federally constrained than the first screen implied. But the site still fails at 50k scale unless it can prove:

- dry and flood-safe upland;
- non-jurisdictional or avoidable wetlands;
- firm water not dependent on capped CCPCUA groundwater;
- labor/absorption plan.

### Land

Large land exists, but the useful intersection is narrow: private, contiguous, dry, flood-safe, not conservation-locked, and water-serviceable. Croatan and Hofmann prove acreage exists but not acquirable buildable acreage.

Action: do not debate Jones at county scale. Run parcel + NWI + FEMA + LiDAR to find any private dry-upland block of at least 5,000 acres, then scale upward only if the first block exists.

### Water

This is where "buying rights" translates differently than in the West. NC is not a prior-appropriation market where a senior ag right simply solves the problem. Jones is in the Central Coastal Plain Capacity Use Area; NC DEQ says groundwater users in Jones over 100,000 gallons/day need permits, and registration/reporting starts over 10,000 gallons/day.

At entry scale, local wells or wholesale water may be possible. At build-out, the path likely becomes surface water, wholesale/regional supply, or a new conveyance project.

Action: run a water-service search, not a water-right purchase search: Jones County system, nearby wholesale suppliers, NRWASA/Neuse path, CCPCUA permit history, and willingness to serve a private new-city load.

### Power

Self-built power reduces dependence on Duke/Jones-Onslow headroom, but it does not remove siting constraints. In Jones, generation siting intersects wetlands, floodplain, storm resilience, fuel access, and possible NCUC/utility interconnection.

Action: if Jones is retained, test a flood-safe generation/service yard within the same dry-upland search area.

### County Posture

Jones has the best governance law and a weak local veto stack: no county zoning, no referendum, no ETJ currently exercised by the small towns, and statutory vesting is strong. That posture is valuable if the physical site can be made to work.

Action: county posture should be preserved as a positive, but not allowed to wash out the flood/water/buildable-area gate.

### Flood/Wetlands

This is the dominant early screen. Even if isolated wetlands are less regulated post-Sackett, the project still has to avoid real flood and hydrology risk. Hurricane Florence-era flooding and Trent/Pollocksville flood behavior make "inland" an unsafe shorthand.

Action: first paid technical spend, if any, should be AOI-specific NWI/FEMA/LiDAR plus preliminary jurisdictional screen.

### Next Search

Jones gets a scale-down reopen path:

1. identify private dry-upland blocks;
2. overlay NWI/FEMA/LiDAR;
3. test CCPCUA/wholesale/surface-water service;
4. evaluate self-built power only inside the surviving dry-upland area;
5. then decide whether the project can fit at <=15k acres or low-thousands population.

## General Lessons From Continuing Past Kills

1. **Kills are useful labels, not stopping rules.** Fall River's water kill taught that land/power were not the real blockers. Jones' wetlands kill softened after current-law review but migrated to flood/water/buildable-area. Sweetwater's old water concern became a water-firmness strategy.
2. **The project spec is still the master variable.** Fall River and Jones can reopen only at smaller scale. Sweetwater's runway also shortens if build-out is much larger or faster.
3. **Self-built power helps most where utility headroom was the blocker, least where water/flood/land is the blocker.** It helps Sweetwater and Fall River tactically; it does not rescue Jones from hydrology.
4. **Water acquisition is jurisdiction-specific.** Western sites need new-vs-senior-vs-storage rights analysis. Jones needs permit/wholesale/surface-water service analysis.
5. **The next artifacts should be maps, not prose.** Across all sites, the highest-value missing object is an AOI map with ownership, water, flood/wetland, power, and veto overlays.
