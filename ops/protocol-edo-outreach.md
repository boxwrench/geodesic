# Protocol: Economic Development Office Outreach

The second proven instrument: ask the EDO directly. This protocol standardizes the ask so responses are comparable across counties, and classifies what comes back. **Mode D** (human), with templates so anyone can run it identically.

**Honesty posture:** never misrepresent who you are or the project's nature. "Researcher evaluating rural counties for a potential large master-planned development" is true and sufficient. Disclosure strategy beyond that is governed by [framework/01, heuristic 6](../framework/01-heuristics.md).

## Step 1 — Find the right contact (Mode C, 10 min)

County EDO director → if none, regional/multi-county EDC → if none, county administrator or commission chair. *Which of these exists is itself the capacity signal* — record it before any contact.

## Step 2 — Email template (send verbatim, adjust bracketed text)

> **Subject:** Inquiry — siting a large mixed-use development
>
> Hello [name],
>
> I'm part of a team evaluating several rural counties as candidate locations for a large, master-planned mixed-use development — residential and light-industrial, phased over many years. [County] is on our shortlist.
>
> Could we set up 20 minutes by phone? I'd like to understand:
>
> 1. How the county would view a project of this kind — welcome, concerns, or both
> 2. What the county's experience is with large development applications
> 3. Whether any incentives, infrastructure programs, or development-ready sites exist
> 4. Who else we should be talking to
>
> Happy to work around your schedule. Thank you!
>
> [name, contact]

## Step 3 — Call script

Open with the same framing, then ask in order (the ordering matters — facts before vibes):

1. "What's the largest development the county has landed or seriously pursued in the last ten years?" *(precedent + scale ceiling)*
2. "If a project like ours filed tomorrow, walk me through what happens — who reviews it, what boards approve it, how long does that take?" *(capacity + process reality; cross-check against planner-call answers)*
3. "What does the county need most right now — jobs, housing, tax base?" *(hunger diagnostics)*
4. "What incentives or infrastructure support could exist for a project this size?" *(institutional tooling)*
5. "Where would a project like this run into trouble here?" *(the gold question — a candid answer is worth the whole call)*
6. "Who else should we talk to — and who will be skeptical?" *(veto-player mapping)*

Log immediately after: date, person, role, answers per question, plus your read — **facts and read recorded separately**.

## Step 4 — Classification

| Class | Markers | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Survival-hungry** | Leads with population loss, school enrollment, tax base; offers to convene meetings | High-value target; verify capacity separately — hunger ≠ ability |
| **Managed-growth** | Talks process, comp plan alignment, "right kind of development" | Workable; expect conditions; gauge whether process is real or rhetorical |
| **Preservationist** | Leads with character, heritage, "not like [nearby boomtown]" | High friction regardless of need; weight heavily |
| **No-capacity** | Slow/no response, role is part-time/regional, can't answer process questions | County can't *process* you even if willing; you bring everything |

Classification + supporting quotes go in the county file (Stage 2, EDO row) and feed the scorecard's opposition/capacity columns.

## Step 5 — Response-latency log

Record elapsed time from email → reply → call held. Across counties this is a free, surprisingly honest capacity metric. No reply after one follow-up (wait 5 business days) = log as no-capacity signal, then try the phone once.

## Automation note

This protocol stays human. The only scriptable parts: contact discovery (directory scrape), send/follow-up scheduling, and latency logging — worth doing only at Step 4 scale-out, per [step-3 build triggers](step-3-automation.md).
